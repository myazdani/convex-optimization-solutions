# Exercise 2.35: Copositive and completely positive cones

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.35, p. 64](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=78).

Original solution.

Use the trace inner product on symmetric matrices. The copositive cone is
```math
\mathcal C=\bigcap_{z\ge0}\{X:\mathrm{tr}(Xzz^T)\ge0\}.
```
Thus it is a closed convex cone. If $`X,-X\in\mathcal C`$, all nonnegative quadratic forms vanish. Testing $`z=e_i`$ gives $`X_{ii}=0`$, and testing $`z=e_i+e_j`$ then gives $`2X_{ij}=0`$. Hence $`X=0`$, proving pointedness. It is solid: if $`\|E\|_2<1`$, then $`I+E\succ0`$, so a neighborhood of $`I`$ consists of copositive matrices.

Its dual is the completely positive cone
```math
\mathcal C^*
=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^r z_jz_j^T:r\text{ finite},\ z_j\ge0\right\}.
```
Call the cone on the right $`Q`$. Directly from the definitions, $`Q^*=\mathcal C`$. To use the bipolar identity without silently adding a closure, we show $`Q`$ is closed. Let
```math
B=\mathrm{conv}\{zz^T:z\ge0,\ \|z\|_2=1\}.
```
This is compact, being the convex hull of a compact set in finite dimensions, and every element has trace one. Every nonzero $`Y\in Q`$ is $`\tau Z`$ for $`\tau=\mathrm{tr}\,Y>0`$ and $`Z\in B`$. If $`Y_j\in Q`$ converges, then the scalars $`\tau_j=\mathrm{tr}\,Y_j`$ are bounded. Compactness of $`B`$ produces a convergent subsequence of normalized matrices whenever needed; its limit yields $`Y=\tau Z\in Q`$. If $`\tau=0`$, boundedness of $`B`$ gives $`Y=0\in Q`$. Thus $`Q`$ is closed, and
$`\mathcal C^*=Q^{**}=Q`$. Coefficients of a conic combination can be absorbed into each $`z_j`$ by a square root.
