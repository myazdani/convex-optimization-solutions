# Exercise 8.30: Convex trade-off between arc length and tangent jumps

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.30, printed page 453. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=467).

For $`n`$ distinct consecutive points there are $`n-1`$ arcs and $`n-2`$ interior joints. We use those consistent indices; the printed final sums have an off-by-one mismatch. Equivalently one may use the book's $`n`$ arcs after supplying $`n+1`$ points. Put $`d_i=\|a_{i+1}-a_i\|_2>0`$ for $`i=1,\ldots,n-1`$.

An arc with tangent/chord angle $`\theta_i\in(-\pi,\pi)`$ has central angle magnitude $`2|\theta_i|`$ and radius $`d_i/(2|\sin\theta_i|)`$. Its length is therefore
```math
L_i=d_i\,\frac{\theta_i}{\sin\theta_i},\qquad L_i(0)=d_i.
```
This formula includes minor and major arcs and the limiting straight segment.

The even function $`g(\theta)=\theta/\sin\theta`$ is strictly convex on $`(-\pi,\pi)`$. For $`0<\theta<\pi`$,
```math
g''(\theta)=\frac{\theta(1+\cos^2\theta)-2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\sin^3\theta}.
```
The numerator vanishes at zero, and its derivative is $`\sin\theta(3\sin\theta-2\theta\cos\theta)>0`$. On $`(0,\pi/2)`$ this follows from $`\tan\theta>\theta`$; on $`[\pi/2,\pi)`$ the cosine is nonpositive. Thus $`g''>0`$, and the limit at zero is $`1/3`$.

With the prescribed signed joint offsets $`\psi_i`$, the bi-criterion convex problem is
```math
\text{minimize with respect to }\mathbb R_+^2\quad
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}d_i g(\theta_i),
\ \sum_{i=2}^{n-1}|\theta_{i-1}+\theta_i+\psi_i|\right),
\qquad-\pi<\theta_i<\pi.
```
Both components are convex. Absolute values can be replaced by epigraph variables $`s_i\ge\pm(\theta_{i-1}+\theta_i+\psi_i)`$. Weighted minimization $`L+\lambda D`$, $`\lambda\ge0`$, or minimizing $`L`$ subject to $`D\le d`$, traces the supported Pareto trade-off; every weakly efficient objective point is supported by nonnegative weights because the upper image is convex.

The minimum-length endpoint is obtained at $`\theta_i=0`$ for every arc, giving $`L_{\min}=\sum_i d_i`$ and $`D=\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}|\psi_i|`$. To find the opposite endpoint, first minimize $`D`$ by an LP with $`-\pi\le\theta_i\le\pi`$ to obtain its infimum. If that optimal face has a point with every angle strictly inside the bounds, minimize $`L`$ on that face to obtain the efficient minimum-discontinuity endpoint. If it has no such point, the minimum $`D`$ is not attained in the allowed open angle domain; it is approached as some arc lengths diverge at $`|\theta_i|\to\pi`$. In that case the trade-off has a limiting asymptote rather than a finite extreme point. Coincident consecutive data points must first be removed or given a separate convention, since their chord direction is undefined.
