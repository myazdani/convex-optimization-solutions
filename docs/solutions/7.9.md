# Exercise 7.9: Unknown monotone sensor calibration

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.9, printed page 395. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=409).

Because $`0<l\le f'\le u`$, the inverse calibration $`g=f^{-1}`$ exists on the real line and satisfies $`a\le g'\le b`$, where $`a=1/u`$ and $`b=1/l`$. Denote the distinct measured values in increasing order by $`\eta_1<\cdots<\eta_K`$, write $`j(i)`$ for the index with $`y_i=\eta_{j(i)}`$, and introduce
```math
z_j=g(\eta_j),\qquad d_j=g'(\eta_j),\qquad a\le d_j\le b.
```
The correct continuous-data log likelihood includes the change-of-variables factor:
```math
\ell(x,z,d)=\sum_{i=1}^m
\left[\log p(z_{j(i)}-a_i^Tx-b_i)+\log d_{j(i)}\right].\tag{1}
```
It is jointly concave in these finite-dimensional variables. Repeated observations share both their inverse value and derivative.

Here are exact convex feasibility conditions for a continuously differentiable inverse (which also suffice for the derivative-bounded class in the question). For each consecutive pair put $`\Delta_j=\eta_{j+1}-\eta_j>0`$ and require
```math
a\Delta_j\le z_{j+1}-z_j\le b\Delta_j.\tag{2}
```
If the lower equality holds, require $`d_j=d_{j+1}=a`$; if the upper equality holds, require $`d_j=d_{j+1}=b`$. Otherwise the two endpoint derivatives may be arbitrary in $`[a,b]`$. These endpoint requirements are necessary: the nonnegative continuous function $`g'-a`$ with zero integral is identically zero, and similarly for $`b-g'`$. Even with a merely everywhere differentiable $`g`$, a saturated secant forces $`g`$ to be affine on that interval by the slope bounds and hence forces the same endpoint derivatives.

They are sufficient as well. On an interval with an interior secant slope $`s`$, choose a small width $`\delta<\Delta_j/2`$, interpolate the derivative linearly from $`d_j`$ to a constant $`c`$ on the first width, keep it constant in the middle, and interpolate to $`d_{j+1}`$ on the last width. Its integral is
```math
c(\Delta_j-\delta)+\frac\delta2(d_j+d_{j+1}).
```
Choosing $`c=[s\Delta_j-\delta(d_j+d_{j+1})/2]/(\Delta_j-\delta)`$ gives the prescribed increment; for sufficiently small $`\delta`$ this lies in $`[a,b]`$. On a saturated interval use the forced constant derivative. Extend with constant endpoint derivatives outside the sample range. Integration produces a global $`C^1`$ inverse with all the desired values and derivatives; its strictly positive derivative gives the required calibration $`f`$.

The feasible set just described is convex, although not closed: an average can lie on a boundary face of (2) only if both averaged points lie on that face, where the same linear derivative conditions hold. Maximizing (1) over it is therefore an exact finite-dimensional convex formulation of the ML problem; recover $`f`$ using the interpolation construction. An optimum need not exist in this nonparametric class.

For a standard closed relaxation, omit the boundary derivative conditions and set every $`d_j=b`$. This maximizes the Jacobian term, leaving the ordinary convex program maximizing $`\sum_i\log p(z_{j(i)}-a_i^Tx-b_i)`$ under (2), plus the constant $`m\log b`$. When its optimizing secants are all strictly between the bounds, the interpolation construction realizes an actual ML estimate. Boundary cases require the exact formulation above; blindly omitting the Jacobian or declaring an arbitrary piecewise-linear interpolation a differentiable ML estimate would not be justified. With continuous positive noise density, interior secant perturbations show the closed relaxation gives the likelihood supremum even when that supremum is unattained.
