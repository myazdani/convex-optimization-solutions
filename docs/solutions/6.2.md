# Exercise 6.2: Best constant under three norms

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.2, printed page 344. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=358).

Assume there is at least one observation, and write its ordered values as $`b_{(1)}\le\cdots\le b_{(m)}`$.

For the Euclidean norm, minimizing the norm is equivalent to minimizing its square. Completing the square gives
```math
\sum_i(x-b_i)^2=m(x-\bar b)^2+\sum_i(b_i-\bar b)^2,
\qquad \bar b=\frac1m\sum_i b_i.
```
Thus the unique minimizer is the mean.

For the one-norm, the subdifferential of the objective at $`x`$ is
```math
\#\{i:b_i\lt x\}-\#\{i:b_i>x\}
+[-\#\{i:b_i=x\},\#\{i:b_i=x\}].
```
It contains zero exactly when at most half the observations lie strictly on either side. The full solution set is the median interval $`[b_{(\lceil m/2\rceil)},b_{(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1)}]`$.

For the infinity norm, only the extreme observations matter:
```math
\max_i|x-b_i|=\max\{x-b_{(1)},b_{(m)}-x\}
=\frac{b_{(m)}-b_{(1)}}2+
\left|x-\frac{b_{(1)}+b_{(m)}}2\right|.
```
Its unique minimizer is the midrange, $`(b_{(1)}+b_{(m)})/2`$. These formulas also cover $`m=1`$ and repeated observations. For an empty observation vector every scalar is optimal.
