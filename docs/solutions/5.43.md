# Exercise 5.43: Two derivations of the SOCP dual

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.43](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=301). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

## (a) Scalar inequalities and explicit auxiliary equalities

Introduce $`y_i=A_ix+b_i`$ and $`t_i=c_i^Tx+d_i`$, with $`\|y_i\|_2-t_i\le0`$. The dimensions are $`y_i,u_i\in\mathbf R^{n_i}`$, $`t_i,v_i,\lambda_i\in\mathbf R`$, and $`c_i\in\mathbf R^n`$ as required by $`c_i^Tx`$.

Use $`\lambda_i\ge0`$ for the norm inequality, $`u_i`$ for $`A_ix+b_i-y_i=0`$, and $`v_i`$ for $`t_i-c_i^Tx-d_i=0`$. Then

```math
\begin{aligned}
L={}&\left[f+\sum_i(A_i^Tu_i-c_iv_i)\right]^Tx
+\sum_i(b_i^Tu_i-d_iv_i)\\
&+\sum_i\left[\lambda_i\|y_i\|_2-u_i^Ty_i+(v_i-\lambda_i)t_i\right].
\end{aligned}
```

The free $`t_i`$ infimum is finite precisely when $`v_i=\lambda_i`$. Under that equality, Cauchy–Schwarz shows

```math
\inf_{y_i}\{\lambda_i\|y_i\|_2-u_i^Ty_i\}
=\begin{cases}0,&\|u_i\|_2\le\lambda_i,\\-\infty,&\|u_i\|_2>\lambda_i.\end{cases}
```

For the second case take $`y_i`$ in the positive $`u_i`$ direction and let its magnitude tend to infinity. Finally, the free $`x`$ infimum requires $`f+\sum_i(A_i^Tu_i-c_iv_i)=0`$. Eliminating $`\lambda_i=v_i`$ yields

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad &\sum_{i=1}^m(b_i^Tu_i-d_iv_i)\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad &f+\sum_{i=1}^m(A_i^Tu_i-c_iv_i)=0,\\
&\|u_i\|_2\le v_i\quad (i=1,\ldots,m).
\end{aligned}
```

The cone inequalities already imply $`v_i\ge0`$; no additional sign constraint on $`u_i`$ is appropriate.

## (b) Self-dual cone formulation

Let $`\mathcal Q_{n_i+1}=\{(t,y):\|y\|_2\le t\}`$. The primal constraints are

```math
h_i(x)=(c_i^Tx+d_i,A_ix+b_i)\in\mathcal Q_{n_i+1}.
```

Since these cones are self-dual, use $`w_i=(v_i,-u_i)\in\mathcal Q_{n_i+1}`$. A lower-bounding Lagrangian subtracts the nonnegative pairing:

```math
\begin{aligned}
L(x,w)&=f^Tx-\sum_i w_i^Th_i(x)\\
&=\left[f+\sum_i(A_i^Tu_i-c_iv_i)\right]^Tx
+\sum_i(b_i^Tu_i-d_iv_i).
\end{aligned}
```

The same free-$`x`$ stationarity equation follows. Cone membership is exactly $`\|u_i\|_2\le v_i`$, giving the identical dual.

For any feasible primal and dual pair, the gap is

```math
f^Tx-\sum_i(b_i^Tu_i-d_iv_i)
=\sum_i\left[v_i(c_i^Tx+d_i)-u_i^T(A_ix+b_i)\right]\ge0,
```

by the two cone constraints and Cauchy–Schwarz. If all the primal norm inequalities can be made strict and the optimal value is finite, Slater gives strong duality and dual attainment. The derivation itself needs no strict feasibility assumption.

## Validation

Both routes use the same signs, expose every implicit equality, and give an explicit nonnegative primal–dual gap. The source's scalar dimension for $`c_i`$ is interpreted as the vector dimension required by its displayed formula.
