# Exercise 4.43: Semidefinite eigenvalue optimization

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.43](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=217). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

## (a) Largest eigenvalue

Minimize $`t`$ subject to $`A(x)\preceq tI`$. Spectral decomposition makes this inequality equivalent to $`\lambda_1(x)\le t`$, so the epigraph SDP is exact.

## (b) Eigenvalue spread

Minimize $`t-s`$ subject to $`sI\preceq A(x)\preceq tI`$. For fixed $`x`$, the smallest possible difference is attained at $`t=\lambda_1(x)`$, $`s=\lambda_m(x)`$. This proves equivalence of values and recovery.

## (c) Positive-definite condition number

Begin with $`\gamma I\preceq A(x)\preceq\lambda I`$, $`\gamma>0`$, and minimize $`\lambda/\gamma`$. Introduce $`s=1/\gamma>0`$, $`y=sx`$, $`t=s\lambda`$. An exact lifted convex formulation is

```math
\mathrm{minimize}\quad t\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad
I\preceq sA_0+\sum_jy_jA_j\preceq tI,\qquad s>0.
```

To express the strict scalar domain with ordinary non-strict LMIs, add an unrestricted scalar $`z`$ and replace $`s>0`$ by

```math
\begin{bmatrix}s&1\\1&z\end{bmatrix}\succeq0.
```

This block forces $`s>0`$ and is feasible for every positive $`s`$ by choosing $`z=1/s`$. All constraints are therefore affine LMIs.

From a positive-definite original matrix, choose $`\gamma=\lambda_m(A(x))`$, $`\lambda=\lambda_1(A(x))`$ for an equal-value lift. From a lifted point recover $`x=y/s`$; its matrix has eigenvalues in $`[1/s,t/s]`$ and therefore condition number at most $`t`$. These two directions prove exact infima and optimizer recovery. Allowing $`s=0`$ without further analysis could introduce nonrecoverable boundary optima; the additional block avoids this issue.

## (d) Sum of absolute eigenvalues

Use symmetric variables $`U,V\succeq0`$ and the SDP

```math
\mathrm{minimize}\quad\mathrm{tr}(U)+\mathrm{tr}(V)\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad A(x)=U-V.
```

In an orthonormal eigenbasis $`q_i`$ of $`A(x)`$, positivity gives

```math
\sum_i|\lambda_i(x)|
=\sum_i|q_i^T(U-V)q_i|
\le\sum_iq_i^T(U+V)q_i=\mathrm{tr}(U)+\mathrm{tr}(V).
```

Equality is attained by the positive and negative spectral parts of $`A(x)`$. Thus every original point has an equal-value lift, every feasible lift upper-bounds its spectral absolute sum, and the optimizer maps are exact.

## Validation

All four programs have linear objectives and affine LMIs. The strict domain in (c) is retained, and (d) proves both directions rather than assuming a decomposition is spectrally optimal.
