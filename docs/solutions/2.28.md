# Exercise 2.28: Positive semidefiniteness in dimensions one to three

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.28, p. 63](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=77).

Original solution.

For $`n=1`$ the condition is simply $`x_1\ge0`$.

For $`n=2`$, using the book's coefficient arrangement, the exact conditions are
```math
x_1\ge0,\qquad x_3\ge0,\qquad x_1x_3-x_2^2\ge0.
```
For $`n=3`$, they are the seven nonnegative principal minors:
```math
\begin{gathered}
x_1\ge0,\quad x_4\ge0,\quad x_6\ge0,\\
x_1x_4-x_2^2\ge0,\quad x_1x_6-x_3^2\ge0,\quad x_4x_6-x_5^2\ge0,\\
x_1x_4x_6+2x_2x_3x_5-x_1x_5^2-x_4x_3^2-x_6x_2^2\ge0.
\end{gathered}
```
Necessity follows because every principal submatrix of a positive semidefinite matrix is positive semidefinite, so its determinant is a product of nonnegative eigenvalues.

For sufficiency in dimension three, the coefficients of $`\det(tI+X)`$ are one, the trace, the sum of the three order-two principal minors, and the determinant. Under the displayed inequalities they are nonnegative, and the leading term makes the polynomial strictly positive for every $`t>0`$. A negative eigenvalue of the real symmetric matrix would make this determinant vanish at its positive negation, a contradiction. Thus every eigenvalue is nonnegative. The same argument with the trace and determinant proves sufficiency in dimension two. No division by diagonal entries is used, so zero and singular cases are covered.
