# Exercise 6.12: Monotone and convex-monotone interpolation

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.12, printed page 347. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=361).

## (a) Monotone interpolation
Necessity is exactly the defining order property evaluated at the data points. For sufficiency suppose the pairwise order tests hold. Let $`y_-=\min_i y_i`$ and define, on all of $`\mathbb R^k`$,
```math
f(u)=\max\bigl(\{y_-\}\cup\{y_i:u_i\le u\}\bigr).
```
This is a maximum of a finite nonempty set. If $`u\le v`$, the index set for $`u`$ is contained in that for $`v`$, proving monotonicity. At $`u_j`$ the index $`j`$ is included, while every included $`i`$ satisfies $`y_i\le y_j`$ by hypothesis. Hence $`f(u_j)=y_j`$. Coincident points automatically have equal prescribed values. With no data, any constant works.

## (b) Convex and monotone interpolation
A finite convex function on all of $`\mathbb R^k`$ has a subgradient at every point: a supporting hyperplane to its epigraph has a nonzero vertical coefficient because the domain is all of $`\mathbb R^k`$, and normalizing gives the subgradient inequality. Choose $`g_i\in\partial f(u_i)`$. The inequality at every $`u_j`$ is precisely the one in the question. Moreover, for each coordinate and $`t>0`$,
```math
f(u_i)-tg_{i\ell}\le f(u_i-te_\ell)\le f(u_i).
```
It follows that $`g_{i\ell}\ge0`$.

Conversely, given the proposed vectors, define
```math
f(u)=\max_i\{y_i+g_i^T(u-u_i)\}.
```
This finite maximum of affine functions is finite and convex everywhere, and is coordinatewise nondecreasing because all slopes are nonnegative. At $`u_j`$, every affine term is at most $`y_j`$ by the assumed inequalities, while term $`j`$ equals $`y_j`$. Thus it interpolates exactly and has the required full domain.
