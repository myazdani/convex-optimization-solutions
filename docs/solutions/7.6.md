# Exercise 7.6: Location-scale likelihood and the Laplace example

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.6, printed page 394. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=408).

Since $`x=ay-b`$ and $`a>0`$, the transformed density is $`a\,p(ay-b)`$. For $`n\ge1`$ observations the log likelihood is
```math
\ell(a,b)=n\log a+\sum_i\log p(ay_i-b),\qquad a>0.
```
A log-concave $`p`$, with $`\log p=-\infty`$ outside its support, makes every summand concave in $`a,b`$. Maximizing $`\ell`$ is therefore a convex optimization problem.

For the density actually printed in the question, $`p(x)=e^{-2|x|}`$, write $`m=b/a`$. For fixed $`a`$, any sample median minimizes $`\sum_i|y_i-m|`$. Let $`S=\min_m\sum_i|y_i-m|`$. The profiled log likelihood is
```math
n\log a-2aS.
```
If $`S>0`$, its unique maximizing scale is $`\hat a=n/(2S)`$, and the full set of maximizing shifts is $`\hat b=\hat a m`$ for all sample medians $`m`$. This follows either by differentiation or strict concavity in $`a`$. If $`S=0`$ all observations coincide; the likelihood grows without bound as $`a\to\infty`$ and $`b=ay_1`$, so no finite MLE exists.

There is a normalization inconsistency in the exercise: $`e^{-2|x|}`$ integrates to one and has mean zero, but its variance is $`1/2`$, not one. Thus for this printed density the fitted mean is $`m`$ and the fitted variance is $`1/(2\hat a^2)`$. If unit variance is intended, the normalized Laplace density is $`p(x)=2^{-1/2}e^{-\sqrt2|x|}`$; the same derivation instead gives $`\hat a=n/(\sqrt2 S)`$, $`\hat b=\hat a m`$, and variance $`1/\hat a^2`$. Both parametrizations give the same fitted distribution for $`y`$.
