# Exercise 3.55: A direct proof for cumulative log-concave densities

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.55, p. 124](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=138).

Original solution.

Let $`g=e^{-h}`$ with differentiable convex $`h`$, and let $`F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^xg(t)\,dt`$.

## (a) Derivatives and the nonnegative-slope case

The fundamental theorem of calculus and the chain rule give
```math
F'(x)=e^{-h(x)},\qquad F''(x)=-h'(x)e^{-h(x)}.
```
If $`h'(x)\ge0`$, the second derivative is nonpositive, so $`F''(x)F(x)\le0\le(F'(x))^2`$.

## (b) Negative slope

Suppose $`h'(x)<0`$. Convexity gives the tangent lower bound $`h(t)\ge h(x)+h'(x)(t-x)`$. Negating, exponentiating, and integrating yields
```math
\begin{aligned}
F(x)&\le e^{-h(x)}\int_{-\infty}^x e^{-h'(x)(t-x)}\,dt\\
&=\frac{e^{-h(x)}}{-h'(x)}.
\end{aligned}
```
The integral converges because $`-h'(x)>0`$. Multiplication by $`-h'(x)e^{-h(x)}>0`$ gives
```math
F''(x)F(x)\le e^{-2h(x)}=(F'(x))^2.
```
Together the two cases prove log-concavity of $`F`$ wherever it is positive. If a density is extended by zero outside a convex support interval, the same proof applies on its positive interior, and the CDF's zero and constant-one portions preserve the usual extended log-concavity conclusion.
