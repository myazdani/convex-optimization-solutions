# Exercise 4.24: Real conic formulations for complex norm fitting

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.24](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=211). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write $`A=A_R+iA_I`$, $`b=b_R+ib_I`$, and $`x=u+iv`$, with all new quantities real. Define

```math
z=\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\begin{bmatrix}r_R\\r_I\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}A_R&-A_I\\A_I&A_R\end{bmatrix}z
-\begin{bmatrix}b_R\\b_I\end{bmatrix}.
```

This is a bijection between complex decision vectors and real vectors, and each complex residual magnitude is $`\sqrt{(r_R)_i^2+(r_I)_i^2}`$.

## One norm

Use the SOCP

```math
\mathrm{minimize}\quad\sum_i t_i\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad\left\|\begin{bmatrix}(r_R)_i\\(r_I)_i\end{bmatrix}\right\|_2\le t_i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,m.
```

For fixed $`z`$ the optimal $`t_i`$ is the complex magnitude, giving exactly the complex one norm.

## Two norm

The square of the complex two norm is $`\|r_R\|_2^2+\|r_I\|_2^2`$. Minimize this convex quadratic in $`z`$, an unconstrained QP with the same minimizers as the norm. Its optimal value is the square of the original one. An objective-preserving SOCP instead minimizes $`t`$ subject to $`\|(r_R,r_I)\|_2\le t`$.

## Infinity norm

Use the SOCP

```math
\mathrm{minimize}\quad t\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad\left\|\begin{bmatrix}(r_R)_i\\(r_I)_i\end{bmatrix}\right\|_2\le t,
\quad i=1,\ldots,m.
```

For fixed $`z`$ the smallest feasible $`t`$ is exactly the largest complex residual magnitude.

In each case recover $`x=u+iv`$ from the real optimizer. Every original point has the displayed exact lift; every lifted feasible objective bounds the original objective in the correct direction. This proves equivalence of values (with the noted squaring for the QP) and of optimizers. All three attain their minima by closedness of the affine residual range and compactness of norm sublevels there.

## Validation

Real and imaginary parts are coupled in a two-dimensional Euclidean norm for each complex absolute value. Treating them as independent one-norm coordinates would produce a different problem.
