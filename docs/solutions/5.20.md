# Exercise 5.20: A simplified dual for channel capacity

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.20](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=293). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Use the continuous convention $`y\log y=0`$ at zero. Keep $`x\succeq0,y\succeq0`$ in the domain and assign $`\nu`$ to $`Px-y=0`$, $`\lambda`$ to $`\mathbf1^Tx-1=0`$. The Lagrangian is

```math
\sum_i(y_i\log y_i-\nu_i y_i)
+x^T(P^T\nu+\lambda\mathbf1-c)-\lambda.
```

The scalar entropy conjugate gives $`\inf_{y\ge0}(y\log y-\nu y)=-e^{\nu-1}`$, attained at $`y=e^{\nu-1}>0`$. The infimum over nonnegative $`x`$ is finite exactly when $`P^T\nu+\lambda\mathbf1\succeq c`$. Thus a dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad-\lambda-\sum_i e^{\nu_i-1}\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad P^T\nu+\lambda\mathbf1\succeq c.
```

## Eliminating the common shift

Because $`P^T\mathbf1=\mathbf1`$, write $`\nu=z+\alpha\mathbf1`$ and $`\lambda=t-\alpha`$. The constraints become $`P^Tz+t\mathbf1\succeq c`$, independent of $`\alpha`$. Its objective is $`\alpha-t-e^{\alpha-1}\sum_i e^{z_i}`$. Maximizing in $`\alpha`$ gives $`\alpha=1-\log\sum_i e^{z_i}`$ and value $`-t-\log\sum_i e^{z_i}`$. Therefore the simplified dual is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad&-t-\log\sum_i e^{z_i}\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad&P^Tz+t\mathbf1\succeq c.
\end{aligned}
```

Eliminating $`t`$ yields the unconstrained concave maximization

```math
\mathrm{maximize}_{z\in\mathbb R^m}\quad
\min_j\{(P^Tz)_j-c_j\}-\log\sum_i e^{z_i}.
```

The minimum of affine functions is concave, as is negative log-sum-exp. Every such point maps back to the original dual by the displayed optimizing shift and $`t=\max_j(c_j-(P^Tz)_j)`$.

Equivalently set $`q_i=e^{z_i}/\sum_k e^{z_k}`$, a positive probability vector. The objective becomes $`\min_j\{\sum_i p_{ij}\log q_i-c_j\}`$. For the channel choice $`c_j=\sum_i p_{ij}\log p_{ij}`$, it is $`-\max_j D(p_{:j}\Vert q)`$, with natural-log relative entropy. This identifies the negative-capacity value, in nats.

The primal simplex is compact and its entropy objective continuous, so it attains a finite optimum. Delete identically zero rows of $`P`$ if necessary; the associated primal $`y_i`$ is always zero and contributes nothing. On the remaining rows, a strictly positive input distribution gives strictly positive $`y`$, so relative-interior regularity proves strong duality and dual attainment. If zero rows are retained, the displayed finite-$`z`$ dual still has the same supremum, but it may only approach it by sending those coordinates to $`-\infty`$; one should not assert attainment in that redundant representation.

The unit column sums are used exactly for shift elimination, and boundary output probabilities are handled without imposing false strict positivity on the primal.
