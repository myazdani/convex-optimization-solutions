# Exercise 4.56: Convex hulls of unions through perspectives

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.56](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=221). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

For a finite convex function $`f`$, define its perspective for $`s>0`$ by $`g(z,s)=s f(z/s)`$, and give it the source's values $`g(0,0)=0`$ and $`g(z,0)=+\infty`$ for $`z\ne0`$. This extension is convex, although not necessarily closed. To verify convexity for two points with positive scales, apply convexity of $`f`$ with normalized weights $`\theta s_1/(\theta s_1+(1-\theta)s_2)`$ and its complement, then multiply by the combined scale. Combining a positive-scale point with the origin follows from positive homogeneity of the perspective, with equality; combining two origins is trivial. Infinite-valued endpoint cases impose no further condition.

Therefore every proposed inequality $`s_i f_{ij}(z_i/s_i)\le0`$ is convex in its pair of variables. The simplex conditions and $`x=\sum_i z_i`$ are affine, and the objective is convex, so the lifted problem is convex.

For a lifted feasible point, each positive $`s_i`$ gives $`x_i=z_i/s_i\in C_i`$. Every zero-scale block is $`z_i=0`$ under the stated convention. Thus

```math
x=\sum_{i:s_i>0}s_i x_i,
```

a convex combination of points from the union, with at least one positive weight. Conversely every point in the convex hull has a finite convex-combination representation. Group the points according to their sets, add the corresponding weights to obtain $`s_i`$, and replace each nonempty group by its weighted average $`x_i\in C_i`$, using convexity of $`C_i`$. Set $`z_i=s_ix_i`$ for positive weights and $`z_i=0`$ for zero weights. This is a feasible lift with exactly the original $`x`$ and objective. Empty $`C_i`$ simply receive zero weight and need no representative.

These maps prove exact feasible projection, equal infima, and optimizer recovery. One should not replace the specified zero-scale convention by the closed perspective without further analysis: that could introduce recession directions and the closure of a hull rather than the stated hull. If a set has no defining inequalities, it is all of space and the original problem is already unconstrained; it can be handled directly or given the harmless redundant inequality $`0\le0`$ to enforce the zero-scale convention explicitly.

## Validation

The proof handles zero weights, grouping multiple points from the same convex set, and possible nonclosedness, instead of assuming a bilinear convex-combination equality is affine.
