# Exercise 8.12: Uniqueness of extremal ellipsoids

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.12, printed page 449. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=463).

The standard assertion concerns a convex body: a compact convex set with nonempty interior. These assumptions ensure that the positive-volume optima exist. For outer ellipsoids, convexity can equivalently be imposed by taking the convex hull of the set being covered.

## Maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid
Represent it as $`c+B B_2`$ with $`B\succ0`$ symmetric. Feasible pairs $`(B,c)`$ form a convex set: for each $`u\in B_2`$, the corresponding convex combination of $`B_1u+c_1`$ and $`B_2u+c_2`$ remains in the convex body. Maximizing volume is maximizing $`\log\det B`$. Its strict concavity, whose second variation is $`-\mathrm{tr}(B^{-1}HB^{-1}H)<0`$ for nonzero symmetric $`H`$, forces any two optima to have the same $`B`$.

If their centers differed, apply an affine transformation making $`B=I`$ and the centers $`\pm v`$, $`v\ne0`$. Convexity forces the body to contain the capsule $`B_2+[-v,v]`$. This capsule contains the ellipsoid $`(I+vv^T)^{1/2}B_2`$: its support is $`\sqrt{\|a\|^2+(a^Tv)^2}`$, no larger than the capsule's support $`\|a\|+|a^Tv|`$. Support dominance implies inclusion by separation. The ellipsoid has volume larger by $`\sqrt{1+\|v\|^2}>1`$, a contradiction. Therefore the center is unique as well.

## Minimum-volume covering ellipsoid
Represent it as $`\{x:\|Ax+b\|_2\le1\}`$, with $`A\succ0`$ symmetric. The constraints for every point of the body are convex in $`A,b`$, and minimizing volume means maximizing $`\log\det A`$. Strict concavity again forces any two optima to have the same $`A`$.

If their offsets $`b_1,b_2`$ differ, put $`\bar b=(b_1+b_2)/2`$ and $`\rho^2=1-\|b_1-b_2\|^2/4`$. For every covered point,
```math
\|Ax+\bar b\|^2
=\tfrac12\|Ax+b_1\|^2+\tfrac12\|Ax+b_2\|^2
-\tfrac14\|b_1-b_2\|^2\le\rho^2\lt 1.
```
Full dimensionality implies $`\rho>0`$. Dividing $`A,\bar b`$ by $`\rho`$ produces a feasible ellipsoid of volume $`\rho^n`$ times as large, a contradiction. Hence its offset and center are unique.

Without the contextual hypotheses, uniqueness/existence cannot be asserted as printed: a union of two disjoint equal balls has two maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoids; an unbounded set has no finite covering ellipsoid; and a lower-dimensional set can have covering volume infimum zero with no positive-definite optimizer. The proof above states exactly where each hypothesis is used.
