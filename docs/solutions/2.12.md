# Exercise 2.12: Convexity of geometric set constructions

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.12, p. 61](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=75).

Original solution.

## (a) Slab

Convex: it is the intersection of the halfspaces $`a^Tx\le\beta`$ and $`-a^Tx\le-\alpha`$. This includes empty and degenerate slabs.

## (b) Rectangle

Convex: intersect the $`2n`$ coordinate halfspaces $`x_i\le\beta_i`$ and $`-x_i\le-\alpha_i`$.

## (c) Wedge

Convex, as an intersection of two halfspaces.

## (d) Closer to one fixed point than every point of a set

Convex, even if $`S`$ is not convex. For each fixed $`y\in S`$, squaring the comparison gives the halfspace
```math
2(y-x_0)^Tx\le\|y\|_2^2-\|x_0\|_2^2.
```
Their arbitrary intersection is convex. A comparison with $`y=x_0`$ is vacuous; an empty intersection is all of space.

## (e) Closer to one set than another

Not necessarily convex. In one dimension take $`S=\{-1,1\}`$ and $`T=\{0\}`$. Both $`-1`$ and $`1`$ satisfy the distance inequality, but their midpoint $`0`$ does not. In fact, the set is $`(-\infty,-1/2]\cup[1/2,\infty)`$.

## (f) Translates lying in a convex set

Convex, because
```math
\{x:x+S_2\subseteq S_1\}
=\bigcap_{s\in S_2}(S_1-s).
```
Every translate of $`S_1`$ is convex. If $`S_2`$ is empty the condition is vacuous and the result is all of space.

## (g) Fractional distance comparison

For $`\theta=1`$, Exercise 2.7 gives a halfspace. For $`0\le\theta<1`$, expanding and completing the square gives the closed ball
```math
\left\|x-\frac{a-\theta^2b}{1-\theta^2}\right\|_2
\le\frac{\theta\|a-b\|_2}{1-\theta^2}.
```
Thus the set is convex in every allowed case; $`\theta=0`$ gives the singleton $`\{a\}`$.
