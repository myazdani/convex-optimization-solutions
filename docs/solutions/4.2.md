# Exercise 4.2: Log-barrier recession and attainment

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.2](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=203). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Put $`s(x)=b-Ax`$ and choose $`x^0`$ with $`s(x^0)\succ0`$.

## (a) Unbounded domain

If $`v\ne0`$ and $`Av\preceq0`$, the ray $`x^0+tv`$, $`t\ge0`$, stays in the domain and is unbounded. Conversely, let $`x^k`$ be domain points with $`\|x^k-x^0\|_2\to\infty`$. A subsequence of the normalized differences converges to a unit vector $`v`$. Dividing $`A(x^k-x^0)\prec s(x^0)`$ by those norms and passing to the limit yields $`Av\preceq0`$.

## (b) Unbounded objective

If $`Av\preceq0`$ is nonzero, every slack on $`x^0+tv`$ is nondecreasing, and at least one grows to infinity. Hence the sum of negative logarithms tends to $`-\infty`$.

For the converse use the stated theorem of alternatives: absence of such a direction gives $`z\succ0`$ with $`A^Tz=0`$. Then every feasible slack satisfies

```math
z^Ts=z^Tb=z^Ts(x^0)=C>0,\qquad 0<s_i\le C/z_i.
```

Thus $`f_0(x)\ge-\sum_i\log(C/z_i)>-\infty`$. This proves the contrapositive and the equivalence. If there are no rows, the empty sum is identically zero and parts (b)–(d) hold directly; below assume at least one row.

## (c) Attainment and stationarity

Suppose the objective is bounded below, and take the positive vector $`z`$ above. Restrict attention to the nonempty level set $`f_0(x)\le f_0(x^0)=\alpha`$. Its slacks satisfy $`\prod_i s_i\ge e^{-\alpha}`$ as well as the upper bounds $`U_i=C/z_i`$. Consequently

```math
s_i\ge e^{-\alpha}\Big/\prod_{j\ne i}U_j>0.
```

The slack vectors therefore lie in a compact box bounded away from zero. They also lie in the closed affine subspace $`b-\mathrm{range}(A)`$. Minimizing the continuous function $`-\sum_i\log s_i`$ on the intersection of this box, affine subspace, and the closed level inequality attains its minimum. Every such slack has a preimage under $`s=b-Ax`$. This proves attainment even if $`\ker A\ne\{0\}`$, when the level set in the original variable is unbounded. At any minimizer the open-domain first-order condition is

```math
A^T\begin{bmatrix}1/s_1&\cdots&1/s_m\end{bmatrix}^{T}=0.
```

This is also sufficient, by convexity.

## (d) Complete optimal set

The function $`-\sum_i\log s_i`$ is strictly convex on the positive orthant. Two minimizing points therefore must have identical slacks: otherwise their midpoint has strictly smaller objective. Thus $`A(x-x^\star)=0`$. Conversely every $`x^\star+v`$ with $`Av=0`$ has the same positive slacks and objective. Hence

```math
X_{\mathrm{opt}}=x^\star+\ker A.
```

## Validation

The argument treats null directions separately from improving recession directions and proves compactness in slack space rather than incorrectly assuming compactness of the original level set.
