# Exercise 2.10: Convex quadratic sublevel sets and slices

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.10, p. 61](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=75).

Original solution.

Let $`q(x)=x^TAx+b^Tx+c`$ with $`A=A^T`$.

## (a) Positive semidefinite quadratic part

For $`z=tx+(1-t)y`$, direct expansion gives
```math
q(z)=tq(x)+(1-t)q(y)-t(1-t)(x-y)^TA(x-y).
```
If $`A\succeq0`$, $`x,y\in C`$, and $`t\in[0,1]`$, the right side is nonpositive, establishing convexity.

## (b) Restriction to the hyperplane

For $`x,y`$ on $`g^Tx+h=0`$, $`g^T(x-y)=0`$. Consequently
```math
(x-y)^TA(x-y)=(x-y)^T(A+\lambda gg^T)(x-y)\ge0
```
under the stated matrix condition. The same expansion proves convexity of the slice, since the affine equality is preserved along segments.

## Converses

Neither converse is true. For (a), $`q(x)=-\|x\|_2^2-1`$ has convex sublevel set $`\mathbb R^n`$ although $`A=-I_n`$ is negative definite. For (b), in $`\mathbb R^2`$ take $`q(x)=-x_1^2-1`$ and the hyperplane $`x_2=0`$. The slice is the entire first coordinate axis, hence convex. But $`A+\lambda e_2e_2^T=\mathrm{diag}(-1,\lambda)`$ is never positive semidefinite. These examples are nonempty, so failure is not merely an empty-set convention.
