# Exercise 2.2: Testing convexity and affinity on lines

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.2, p. 60](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=74).

Original solution.

Intersections of convex sets are convex, and a line is convex; hence convexity of $`C`$ implies convexity of every line intersection. Conversely, given distinct $`x,y\in C`$, take the line $`L=\{x+t(y-x):t\in\mathbb R\}`$. Convexity of $`C\cap L`$ puts $`(1-t)x+ty`$ in $`C`$ for every $`t\in[0,1]`$. Equal endpoints require no argument.

The affine assertion is identical with $`t\in\mathbb R`$: intersections of affine sets are affine, and if every $`C\cap L`$ is affine then it contains the entire line through any two distinct points of $`C`$. This is the defining two-point condition for affinity. Empty sets and singletons satisfy these conditions vacuously (using the customary convention that the empty set is affine).
