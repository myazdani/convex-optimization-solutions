# Exercise 4.31: Exact recursive beam-design geometric programming

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.31](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=213). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Use the original terminal values $`v_{N+1}=y_{N+1}=0`$, which are constants, not positive GP variables. For positive widths and heights define

```math
d_i=\frac{6F}{Ew_ih_i^3}>0.
```

The original physical recursion becomes

```math
v_i=(2i-1)d_i+v_{i+1},\qquad
y_i=(i-1/3)d_i+v_{i+1}+y_{i+1},\quad i=N,\ldots,1.
```

Dividing these expressions by the positive variables on the left and relaxing equality to an upper bound gives exactly the two posynomial inequalities in the proposed GP. The equation defining $`d_i`$ is the monomial equality $`Ew_ih_i^3d_i/(6F)=1`$. All width, height, aspect-ratio, stress, and endpoint-deflection constraints are precisely the original ones in normalized GP form. Terms involving the zero terminal constants are simply omitted from the last-segment posynomials.

To prove exactness, first take an original feasible design $`(w,h)`$. Compute $`d`$ by its defining monomial and $`\widehat v,\widehat y`$ by the physical recursion. These are positive for indices $`1,\ldots,N`$ and satisfy the lifted inequalities with equality, including $`\widehat y_1\le y_{\max}`$. The objective remains the same volume.

Conversely take any lifted feasible point. Its $`d`$ equals the physical value. Compute the exact physical recursion for its widths and heights. Backward induction proves

```math
\widehat v_i\le v_i,\qquad\widehat y_i\le y_i.
```

At $`N+1`$ this is equality. If it holds at $`i+1`$, the nonnegative coefficients and lifted recurrence inequalities give

```math
\widehat v_i=(2i-1)d_i+\widehat v_{i+1}\le v_i,
\qquad
\widehat y_i=(i-1/3)d_i+\widehat v_{i+1}+\widehat y_{i+1}\le y_i.
```

Hence the true endpoint deflection satisfies $`\widehat y_1\le y_1\le y_{\max}`$. The recovered design is original-feasible, and its volume is unchanged. Thus the feasible designs project exactly, the optimal values agree, and optimizers transfer in both directions. Tightness of every lifted recurrence at every optimizer is unnecessary; replacing the auxiliaries with their exact recursive values always gives an equally good optimal lift.
