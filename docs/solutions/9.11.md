# Exercise 9.11: Gradient and Newton methods after an increasing composition

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.11, printed page 515. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=529).

Put $`a=\phi'(f(x))`$, $`b=\phi''(f(x))\ge0`$, $`q=\nabla f(x)`$, and $`H=\nabla^2f(x)`$. The chain rule gives
```math
\nabla g=aq,\qquad\nabla^2g=aH+bqq^T.
```
Thus the negative gradient for $`g`$ is $`a`$ times that for $`f`$. If $`a>0`$, they define the same ray; their numerical line-search parameters are rescaled by $`1/a`$.

For the Newton comparison assume $`H\succ0`$ and $`a>0`$. Let $`\lambda_f^2=q^TH^{-1}q`$. Applying the rank-one inverse formula, or multiplying the claimed direction by the Hessian, yields
```math
\Delta x_{g,\rm nt}
=\frac{a}{a+b\lambda_f^2}\,\Delta x_{f,\rm nt},
\qquad\Delta x_{f,\rm nt}=-H^{-1}q.
```
The multiplier is positive and at most one. Thus Newton directions also lie on the same ray, though pure unit-step iterations generally differ.

With an exact line search over the full nonnegative ray, a strictly increasing $`\phi`$ preserves the minimizing points on that ray. The gradient methods therefore have identical iterates when started together; so do the Newton methods under the stated nonsingularity conditions. Backtracking with a fixed Armijo test need not preserve the iterates because the objective changes are transformed nonlinearly.

If “increasing” means only nondecreasing, the initial claim about equivalent minimizers needs qualification: a flat portion can enlarge the minimizing set. At a point with $`a=0`$, the composed gradient vanishes and its Hessian may be singular; the positive-ray/Newton comparison then need not apply. These are genuine qualifications, not uses of the inverse formula with a zero denominator.
