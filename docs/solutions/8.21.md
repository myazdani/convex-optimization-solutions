# Exercise 8.21: Analytic center as a maximum-likelihood estimate

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.21, printed page 450. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=464).

With residuals $`r_i=y_i-a_i^Tx`$, the log likelihood on its positive domain is
```math
m\log\alpha_r+r\sum_i\log(1-r_i^2)
=m\log\alpha_r+r\sum_i[\log(1-r_i)+\log(1+r_i)].
```
The two positive factors are exactly the slacks of the inequalities $`y_i-1<a_i^Tx<y_i+1`$. Since $`r>0`$, maximizing the likelihood is equivalent to minimizing the sum of negative logarithms of these $`2m`$ slacks, the analytic-center objective for the stated inequality description.

Thus every analytic center is an ML estimate, and the optimizer sets coincide. This presumes a strictly consistent parameter exists. Otherwise all model likelihoods are zero and the logarithmic analytic center is undefined. If $`A`$ has full column rank, the strictly feasible measurement polyhedron is bounded and the barrier strictly convex, giving a unique center. With a nontrivial nullspace the same residual-space optimum exists and the parameter estimates differ by nullspace vectors. The normalizing constant $`\alpha_r`$ is independent of $`x`$ and does not affect the estimate.
