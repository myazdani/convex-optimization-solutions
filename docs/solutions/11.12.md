# Exercise 11.12: Barrier approximation of a convex-concave game

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.12, printed page 627. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=641).

## (a) Convex-concavity
For fixed $`z`$, $`tf_0(w,z)`$ is convex in $`w`$ and each $`-\log(-f_i(w))`$ is convex by the increasing convex composition rule. Their sum is convex in $`w`$. For fixed $`w`$, $`tf_0(w,z)`$ is concave and $`\log(-\tilde f_i(z))`$ is concave, since its negative is the convex log barrier. Thus the proposed function is convex-concave on its product of strict feasible domains. Under the stated unique-saddle assumption its first-order saddle equations form the residual system for the game Newton method.

## (b) Saddle-point error bounds
At a barrier saddle point write $`\bar w=w_*(t),\bar z=z_*(t)`$. Fixing $`\bar z`$ gives a usual scalar-inequality centering problem in $`w`$. The multipliers $`\lambda_i=1/[t(-f_i(\bar w))]`$ are nonnegative, and saddle stationarity makes $`\bar w`$ minimize the convex Lagrangian for $`f_0(w,\bar z)`$. Its gap is $`m/t`$. Weak duality therefore gives
```math
f_0(\bar w,\bar z)\le\inf_{w\in W}f_0(w,\bar z)+m/t.
```
Similarly, fix $`\bar w`$ and regard the maximization in $`z`$ as minimizing the convex function $`-f_0(\bar w,z)`$. Its barrier multipliers are $`\tilde\lambda_i=1/[t(-\tilde f_i(\bar z))]`$, giving
```math
-f_0(\bar w,\bar z)\le\inf_{z\in Z}[-f_0(\bar w,z)]+\tilde m/t.
```
Negating this is the second requested bound. Adding them yields
```math
\sup_{z\in Z}f_0(\bar w,z)-\inf_{w\in W}f_0(w,\bar z)
\le(m+\tilde m)/t.
```
The argument permits unattained infima or suprema because weak duality bounds the full feasible sets. Increasing $`t`$ thus yields an explicit approximate-saddle certificate.
