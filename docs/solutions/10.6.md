# Exercise 10.6: The constrained Newton decrement is model improvement

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.6, printed page 558. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=572).

At a feasible point let $`g=\nabla f(x)`$, $`H=\nabla^2f(x)`$, and let $`v_*`$ solve the equality-constrained quadratic model. Its KKT equations are
```math
Hv_*+A^Tw=-g,\qquad Av_*=0.
```
Multiplication by $`v_*^T`$ gives $`g^Tv_*=-v_*^THv_*`$. Therefore
```math
\widehat f(x+v_*)=f(x)+g^Tv_*+\tfrac12v_*^THv_*
=f(x)-\tfrac12v_*^THv_*.
```
The convex quadratic's KKT conditions are sufficient, and nonsingularity of the KKT matrix makes its minimizer unique. By definition the constrained Newton decrement satisfies $`\lambda(x)^2=v_*^THv_*`$. Rearranging proves $`f(x)-\inf_{A(x+v)=b}\widehat f(x+v)=\lambda(x)^2/2`$. This formula allows a full-space singular Hessian, provided it is positive definite on feasible directions.
