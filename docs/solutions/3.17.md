# Exercise 3.17: Concavity of power means below order one

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.17, p. 116](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=130).

Original solution.

Let $`S=\sum_i x_i^p>0`$, $`v_i=x_i^{p-1}`$, and $`D=\mathrm{diag}(x_i^{p-2})`$, where $`x_i>0`$, $`p<1`$, and $`p\ne0`$. Differentiating $`f=S^{1/p}`$ gives
```math
\nabla f=S^{1/p-1}v,\qquad
\nabla^2f=(1-p)S^{1/p-2}(vv^T-SD).
```
For any direction $`z`$, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yields
```math
\left(\sum_i z_ix_i^{p-1}\right)^2
=\left(\sum_i (z_ix_i^{(p-2)/2})x_i^{p/2}\right)^2
\le\left(\sum_i z_i^2x_i^{p-2}\right)\left(\sum_i x_i^p\right).
```
Thus $`z^T(vv^T-SD)z\le0`$. The scalar factor $`(1-p)S^{1/p-2}`$ is positive for all the allowed positive and negative exponents. Hence the Hessian is negative semidefinite throughout the convex positive orthant and $`f`$ is concave. This directly includes $`p=1/2`$ and $`p=-1`$, the two requested special cases, as well as $`n=1`$, where the function is linear.
