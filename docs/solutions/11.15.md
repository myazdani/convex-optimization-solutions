# Exercise 11.15: A generalized logarithm is strictly cone-increasing

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.15, printed page 628. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=642).

The definition gives a concave function on $`\mathrm{int}K`$, a negative-definite Hessian, and log homogeneity $`\psi(sw)=\psi(w)+\theta\log s`$ with $`\theta>0`$.

## (a) Gradient in the dual cone
For $`y,w\in\mathrm{int}K`$, the supporting inequality for concavity gives
```math
\psi(w)+\theta\log s=\psi(sw)
\le\psi(y)+\nabla\psi(y)^T(sw-y),\qquad s>0.
```
If $`w^T\nabla\psi(y)\le0`$, the right side is bounded above as $`s\to\infty`$, while the left side diverges. Thus the pairing is positive for every interior $`w`$. Every point of the proper cone is a limit of interior points, so continuity yields nonnegative pairing with every $`w\in K`$. This is exactly $`\nabla\psi(y)\in K^*`$.

## (b) Strict dual interior
Suppose this dual vector were on the boundary. Compactness of the unit directions in the closed cone and the dual-interior characterization imply a nonzero $`w\in K`$ with $`w^T\nabla\psi(y)=0`$. For sufficiently small $`\epsilon>0`$, $`y+\epsilon w`$ is still in the interior, but
```math
w^T\nabla\psi(y+\epsilon w)
=\epsilon\,w^T\nabla^2\psi(y)w+o(\epsilon)<0,
```
contradicting part (a). Therefore the gradient is in $`\mathrm{int}K^*`$. In particular along a nonzero cone displacement between interior points, integration of its positive gradient pairing makes the function strictly increase, giving the stated cone-monotonicity interpretation.
