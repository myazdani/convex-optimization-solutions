# Exercise 5.36: Linear equality alternatives from inequality alternatives

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.36](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=299). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write $`Ax=b`$ as $`Ax\preceq b`$, $`-Ax\preceq-b`$. The linear theorem of alternatives says that exactly one of this system and

```math
\lambda_+,\lambda_-\succeq0,\qquad
A^T(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)=0,\qquad
b^T(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)\lt 0
```

is feasible. Setting $`y=\lambda_+-\lambda_-`$ gives the alternative $`A^Ty=0,b^Ty<0`$. Conversely any such $`y`$ decomposes into its componentwise positive and negative parts, producing the two nonnegative multipliers.

Existence of a null vector with $`b^Ty\ne0`$ is equivalent to existence of one with negative pairing, because its sign can be reversed without changing $`A^Ty=0`$. Thus

```math
Ax=b\text{ is solvable}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{there is no }y\text{ with }A^Ty=0,\ b^Ty\ne0.
```

Mutual exclusivity follows directly too: if $`Ax=b`$ and $`A^Ty=0`$, then $`b^Ty=x^TA^Ty=0`$. This recovers the condition that $`b`$ be orthogonal to the nullspace of $`A^T`$.

## Validation

The strict alternative certificate is used; a nonzero null vector with zero pairing is not an infeasibility witness.
