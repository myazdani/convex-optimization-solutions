# Exercise 5.39: Two equivalent semidefinite partitioning bounds

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.39](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=299). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

## (a) Rank-one matrix formulation

For $`x_i\in\{-1,1\}`$, the matrix $`X=xx^T`$ is PSD, rank one, and unit diagonal. Conversely a rank-one PSD matrix has the form $`xx^T`$ by spectral decomposition, and its unit diagonal forces $`x_i^2=1`$. The identity $`\mathrm{tr}(Wxx^T)=x^TWx`$ proves exact correspondence of values and feasible solutions, up to the harmless sign ambiguity $`x\leftrightarrow-x`$.

## (b) Dropping rank

Removing the rank-one condition enlarges the feasible set without changing the objective, so the SDP optimum is a lower bound on the partitioning optimum. A rank-one SDP optimizer factors into a sign vector attaining that lower bound, certifying a globally optimal partition. A higher-rank solution alone gives no such certificate.

## (c) Conic duality between the two bounds

Assign free multipliers $`\nu_i`$ to $`X_{ii}-1=0`$ and retain $`X\succeq0`$ in the domain. The Lagrangian is

```math
\mathrm{tr}((W+\mathrm{diag}\nu)X)-\mathbf1^T\nu.
```

Its infimum is $`-\mathbf1^T\nu`$ if $`W+\mathrm{diag}\nu\succeq0`$, and $`-\infty`$ otherwise, using a PSD rank-one ray in a negative-eigenvalue direction. The SDP relaxation's dual is therefore exactly the stated Lagrangian dual of the nonconvex partitioning problem.

The relaxation is strictly feasible at $`X=I`$ and has a compact feasible set, since PSD unit-diagonal matrices have $`|X_{ij}|\le1`$. It has a finite attained optimum. Slater gives an attained dual optimizer with the same value, so the two SDP bounds are identical. This equality does not assert equality with the original nonconvex partitioning optimum.
