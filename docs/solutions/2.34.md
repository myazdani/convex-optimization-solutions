# Exercise 2.34: The lexicographic cone

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.34, p. 64](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=78).

Original solution.

## (a) Cone properties and the dimension qualification

Nonnegative scaling preserves the first nonzero coordinate's positivity, with scaling by zero producing zero. The cone is also convex: for a sum of two nonzero lexicographically positive vectors with positive coefficients, the earliest nonzero coordinate among the summands is positive; if the indices coincide, the two positive entries add. Zero or zero coefficients do not change this conclusion.

For $`n\ge2`$, it is not closed: $`(1/j,-1,0,\ldots,0)`$ belongs but converges to $`(0,-1,0,\ldots,0)`$, which does not. Thus it is not proper. It is nevertheless pointed and solid, since it contains $`\{x:x_1>0\}`$. The book's nonproperness assertion needs $`n\ge2`$; for $`n=1`$ this cone is exactly $`\mathbb R_+`$ and is proper.

## (b) Total order

For distinct $`x,y`$, the first nonzero coordinate of $`y-x`$ is either positive or negative. In the first case $`x\le_{\mathrm{lex}}y`$; in the second $`y\le_{\mathrm{lex}}x`$. Equality satisfies both. Reflexivity follows from zero membership; transitivity follows from closure under addition; antisymmetry follows since a nonzero vector and its negative cannot both have a positive first nonzero coordinate. Hence this is a linear (total) ordering.

## (c) Dual

The answer, including $`n=1`$, is the ray
```math
K_{\mathrm{lex}}^*=\{\lambda e_1:\lambda\ge0\}.
```
Every such vector pairs nonnegatively with the cone. Conversely, testing the vectors $`(1,u_2,\ldots,u_n)`$, with the last coordinates arbitrary, forces $`y_2=\cdots=y_n=0`$. Testing $`e_1`$ gives $`y_1\ge0`$. Equivalently the closure of the lexicographic cone is $`\{x:x_1\ge0\}`$, whose dual is this ray.
