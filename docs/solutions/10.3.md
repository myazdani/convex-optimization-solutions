# Exercise 10.3: Newton steps for the equality-constrained dual

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.3, printed page 557. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=571).

Let $`x=x(\nu)`$ be the unique minimizer of the Lagrangian and $`H=\nabla^2f(x)\succ0`$. It satisfies $`\nabla f(x)+A^T\nu=0`$.

## (a) Dual derivatives and step
The implicit-function theorem gives $`Dx(\nu)=-H^{-1}A^T`$. Differentiating $`g(\nu)=f(x)+\nu^T(Ax-b)`$ cancels the terms multiplying $`Dx`$ and gives
```math
\nabla g(\nu)=Ax-b,\qquad
\nabla^2g(\nu)=-AH^{-1}A^T.
```
This proves twice differentiability. With full-row-rank $`A`$ as in the chapter, the Hessian is negative definite. The Newton step for maximizing the dual is
```math
\Delta\nu=(AH^{-1}A^T)^{-1}(Ax-b).
```
It is the same root-finding Newton step as for $`-g`$ minimization; the positive sign on the displayed residual is intentional.

## (b) Strong concavity from the KKT inverse bound
Put $`S=AH^{-1}A^T\succ0`$. Block elimination shows the lower-right block of $`\begin{bmatrix}H&A^T\\A&0\end{bmatrix}^{-1}`$ is $`-S^{-1}`$. A principal block has operator norm no larger than the entire matrix, so $`\|S^{-1}\|_2\le K`$. Its eigenvalues are positive, hence $`S\succeq K^{-1}I`$. The derivative formula then gives $`\nabla^2g(\nu)\preceq-K^{-1}I`$, exactly the claimed strong concavity.
