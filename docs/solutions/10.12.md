# Exercise 10.12: Structured Newton steps for trace-inverse optimization

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.12, printed page 559. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=573).

Use the trace inner product on symmetric matrices and remove redundant equality constraints so their functionals are independent. At $`X\succ0`$ the gradient and Hessian operator are
```math
\nabla f(X)=-X^{-2},\qquad
\mathcal H_X[V]=X^{-1}VX^{-2}+X^{-2}VX^{-1}.
```
Diagonalize $`X=U\mathrm{diag}(\lambda)U^T`$. In that basis the inverse Hessian acts entrywise:
```math
\bigl(U^T\mathcal H_X^{-1}[W]U\bigr)_{ij}
=\frac{\lambda_i^2\lambda_j^2}{\lambda_i+\lambda_j}(U^TWU)_{ij}.\tag{1}
```
All weights are positive, so the operator is positive definite.

## Primal Newton step
At a feasible point, set $`W_j=\mathcal H_X^{-1}[A_j]`$ and $`S_{ij}=\mathrm{tr}(A_iW_j)`$. The KKT equations are
```math
\mathcal H_X[\Delta X]+\sum_j\nu_jA_j=X^{-2},\qquad
\mathrm{tr}(A_i\Delta X)=0.
```
Because $`\mathcal H_X^{-1}[X^{-2}]=X/2`$, eliminate the matrix step to obtain
```math
S\nu=b/2,\qquad\Delta X=X/2-\sum_j\nu_jW_j.
```
The Gram matrix $`S`$ is positive definite for independent constraints. Compute all matrices in the eigenbasis, form the weighted trace products there, and transform the final step back only once.

The eigendecomposition costs $`O(n^3)`$, rotating $`p`$ dense constraint matrices costs $`O(pn^3)`$, forming $`S`$ costs $`O(p^2n^2)`$, and its factorization costs $`O(p^3)`$. When $`p`$ and $`n`$ have the same order, the total is $`O(n^4)`$, versus $`O(n^6)`$ for a dense unstructured Newton system in $`n(n+1)/2`$ matrix coordinates.

## Dual and its Newton step
Let $`C(\nu)=\sum_i\nu_iA_i`$. The Lagrangian infimum is $`-\infty`$ if $`C`$ has a negative direction. For $`C\succeq0`$,
```math
\mathrm{tr}(X^{-1})+\mathrm{tr}(CX)\ge2\,\mathrm{tr}(C^{1/2}),
```
because their difference is $`\|X^{-1/2}-X^{1/2}C^{1/2}\|_F^2`$. For $`C\succ0`$, equality holds at $`X=C^{-1/2}`$; for singular $`C\succeq0`$, let $`X`$ tend to infinity on its nullspace to attain the infimum as a limit. Thus the dual is
```math
\text{maximize }2\,\mathrm{tr}(C(\nu)^{1/2})-b^T\nu
\quad\text{subject to }C(\nu)\succeq0.
```
On its smooth interior $`C\succ0`$, minimize $`F=b^T\nu-2\mathrm{tr}(C^{1/2})`$. With $`X=C^{-1/2}`$,
```math
(\nabla F)_i=b_i-\mathrm{tr}(A_iX),\qquad
(\nabla^2F)_{ij}=\mathrm{tr}(A_i\mathcal H_X^{-1}[A_j]).
```
To derive the Hessian, differentiate $`C=X^{-2}`$, giving $`dX=-\mathcal H_X^{-1}[dC]`$. In an eigenbasis of $`C`$ with eigenvalues $`\mu_i`$, the entrywise inverse weights are $`[\sqrt{\mu_i\mu_j}(\sqrt{\mu_i}+\sqrt{\mu_j})]^{-1}`$. Build this same Gram matrix and solve its $`p\times p`$ Newton system. The cost is again $`O(pn^3+p^2n^2+p^3)=O(n^4)`$ when $`p\asymp n`$. Line search must preserve $`C\succ0`$; if no positive-definite dual combination exists, this smooth-interior Newton implementation has no initialization and a boundary-capable conic method is needed.
