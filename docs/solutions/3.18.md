# Exercise 3.18: Trace inverse and determinant-root curvature

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.18, p. 116](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=130).

Original solution.

Fix $`X\succ0`$ and a symmetric line direction $`V`$. Put $`B=X^{-1/2}VX^{-1/2}=U\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)U^T`$. The line remains positive definite exactly on the interval where every $`1+t\lambda_i>0`$. Convexity or concavity on every such line proves the matrix-function claim.

## (a) Trace inverse

Using the factorization $`X+tV=X^{1/2}(I+tB)X^{1/2}`$ and cyclicity of trace,
```math
\mathrm{tr}((X+tV)^{-1})=\sum_i\frac{c_i}{1+t\lambda_i},
\qquad c_i=(U^TX^{-1}U)_{ii}>0.
```
Its second derivative is $`\sum_i2c_i\lambda_i^2/(1+t\lambda_i)^3\ge0`$, so the trace inverse is convex. The weights must be retained: congruence does not preserve the ordinary eigenvalues of $`X+tV`$.

## (b) Determinant root

Let $`g(t)=\det(X+tV)^{1/n}>0`$. The determinant factorization gives
```math
\log g(t)=\frac1n\log\det X+\frac1n\sum_i\log(1+t\lambda_i).
```
Writing $`a_i=\lambda_i/(1+t\lambda_i)`$,
```math
\frac{g''(t)}{g(t)}
=\frac1{n^2}\left(\sum_i a_i\right)^2
-\frac1n\sum_i a_i^2\le0
```
by Cauchy–Schwarz. Therefore the determinant root is concave on the positive definite cone. For $`n=1`$ equality holds and the function is linear.
