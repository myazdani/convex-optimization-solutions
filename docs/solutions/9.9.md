# Exercise 9.9: Variational characterization of the Newton decrement

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.9, printed page 515. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=529).

Assume $`H=\nabla^2f(x)\succ0`$, and write $`g=\nabla f(x)`$. With $`w=H^{1/2}v`$, the constraint $`v^THv=1`$ becomes $`\|w\|=1`$ and
```math
-v^Tg=-w^TH^{-1/2}g\le\|H^{-1/2}g\|=\sqrt{g^TH^{-1}g}=\lambda(x).
```
For $`g\ne0`$, equality holds at $`w=-H^{-1/2}g/\lambda`$, or $`v=-H^{-1}g/\lambda`$. If $`g=0`$, every unit-Hessian-norm vector gives zero. This proves the first supremum formula.

The ratio $`-v^Tg/\sqrt{v^THv}`$ is unchanged by positive scaling of nonzero $`v`$. Normalizing it to Hessian norm one therefore gives exactly the same supremum, proving the second formula. Positive definiteness is essential for the ordinary inverse and denominator; a semidefinite Hessian requires a range condition and a pseudoinverse version instead.
