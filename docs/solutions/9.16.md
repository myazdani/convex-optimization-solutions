# Exercise 9.16: Curvature of a one-dimensional self-concordant function

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.16, printed page 516. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=530).

The domain is an interval, and $`f''\ge0`$ by convexity.

## (a) Reciprocal square-root curvature
Where $`f''>0`$,
```math
\left|\frac d{dx}(f'')^{-1/2}\right|
=\frac{|f'''|}{2(f'')^{3/2}}\le1,
```
which is exactly equivalent to the standard self-concordance inequality. Equality with derivative $`+1`$ gives $`(f'')^{-1/2}=x+c>0`$, and two integrations give
```math
f(x)=-\log(x+c)+a x+b.
```
Equality with derivative $`-1`$ instead gives $`(f'')^{-1/2}=c-x>0`$, hence $`f(x)=-\log(c-x)+a x+b`$. The constants are arbitrary subject to the indicated positive affine factors on the interval. The affine addition does not alter the second or third derivative.

## (b) Zero curvature cannot occur at an isolated region boundary
Suppose $`f''(x_0)>0`$. On its positive connected component, part (a) implies
```math
\frac1{\sqrt{f''(x)}}\le\frac1{\sqrt{f''(x_0)}}+|x-x_0|.
```
If that component had a finite endpoint inside the domain where the curvature became zero, continuity of $`f''`$ would make the left side tend to infinity, while the right side remained bounded. This is impossible. Thus a positive component extends throughout the connected domain. Either such a component exists and $`f''>0`$ everywhere, or $`f''=0`$ everywhere and $`f`$ is affine. No closedness of the domain is needed for this alternative.
