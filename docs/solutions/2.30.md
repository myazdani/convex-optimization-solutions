# Exercise 2.30: Properties of generalized inequalities

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.30, p. 64](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=78).

Original solution.

Let $`K`$ be a proper cone in $`\mathbb R^n`$, $`n\ge1`$. Recall $`x\preceq_K y`$ means $`y-x\in K`$, while $`x\prec_K y`$ means $`y-x\in\mathrm{int}\,K`$.

## Nonstrict properties

- Addition: if $`y-x,v-u\in K`$, then $`(y+v)-(x+u)\in K`$, because a convex cone is closed under sums.
- Transitivity: $`z-x=(z-y)+(y-x)\in K`$ when both differences are in $`K`$.
- Nonnegative scaling: $`\gamma(y-x)\in K`$ for $`\gamma\ge0`$, by the cone property.
- Reflexivity: $`x-x=0\in K`$.
- Antisymmetry: if both $`y-x`$ and its negative lie in $`K`$, pointedness forces $`y-x=0`$.
- Limits: if $`y_i-x_i\in K`$ and the differences tend to $`y-x`$, closedness gives $`y-x\in K`$.

## Strict properties

Containment $`\mathrm{int}\,K\subseteq K`$ gives strict-implies-nonstrict.

For mixed addition, if $`d\in\mathrm{int}\,K`$ choose $`\epsilon>0`$ with $`B(d,\epsilon)\subset K`$. For $`e\in K`$,
```math
B(d+e,\epsilon)=B(d,\epsilon)+e\subseteq K+K\subseteq K.
```
Thus $`\mathrm{int}\,K+K\subseteq\mathrm{int}\,K`$, proving the listed strict-plus-nonstrict addition property (and strict transitivity as well).

For $`\gamma>0`$, multiplication by $`\gamma`$ is a homeomorphism preserving $`K`$, so it preserves its interior; this proves strict scaling.

Zero is not interior to a proper cone: a ball around zero, followed by nonnegative scaling, would fill all of $`\mathbb R^n`$, contradicting pointedness. Hence $`x\not\prec_K x`$.

Finally, if $`d=y-x\in\mathrm{int}\,K`$, choose a ball as above. Whenever $`\|u\|+\|v\|<\epsilon`$, the perturbed difference $`(y+v)-(x+u)=d+v-u`$ remains in that ball. Thus $`x+u\prec_K y+v`$ for sufficiently small perturbations, as required.
