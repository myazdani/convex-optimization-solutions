# Exercise 7.8: Log-concave noise and sign measurements

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.8, printed page 395. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=409).

Let $`F(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t p(v)dv`$ and $`\bar F(t)=\int_t^\infty p(v)dv`$ for the log-concave noise density, and let $`s_i=a_i^Tx+b_i`$. The observation likelihood factors are
```math
\Pr(y_i=-1\mid x)=F(-s_i),\qquad
\Pr(y_i=1\mid x)=\bar F(-s_i).
```
Both distribution tails are log-concave. To check the hypothesis needed for the marginalization rule, the function $`p(v)\mathbf1_{\{v\le t\}}`$ is jointly log-concave in $`(v,t)`$: its support halfspace is convex and its logarithm on that support is the concave function $`\log p(v)`$. Integrating over $`v`$ therefore makes $`F(t)`$ log-concave. Applying the same argument to the halfspace $`v\ge t`$ proves it for $`\bar F(t)`$.

Consequently the ML problem is the convex extended-value minimization
```math
-\sum_{i:y_i=-1}\log F(-a_i^Tx-b_i)
-\sum_{i:y_i=1}\log\bar F(-a_i^Tx-b_i).
```
Zero-probability observations contribute $`+\infty`$. No symmetry or zero-mean assumption on the noise is needed. Convexity does not guarantee a finite maximizer: completely separated observations can approach perfect likelihood only at infinity.
