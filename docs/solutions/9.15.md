# Exercise 9.15: Three self-concordant conic barriers

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.15, printed page 516. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=530).

It suffices to check each restriction to an affine line. If the line's $`y`$ coordinate varies, use $`s=y>0`$ as its affine parameter and write its other coordinate(s) as $`as+b`$. Self-concordance is preserved by invertible affine reparametrization. We apply the logarithmic-composition rule proved in Exercise 9.14.

## (a) Second-order-cone barrier
Set
```math
g(s)=\frac{\|as+b\|^2}{s}-s
=(\|a\|^2-1)s+2a^Tb+\frac{\|b\|^2}{s}.
```
It is convex, with $`g''=2\|b\|^2/s^3`$ and $`|g'''|=3g''/s`$. Moreover $`-\log(-g(s))-\log s=-\log(s^2-\|as+b\|^2)`$. The rule proves the line restriction is self-concordant. If $`y`$ is constant along the line, the log argument is a positive concave quadratic in the line parameter; it factors into positive affine factors on its positivity interval, and its negative logarithm is a sum of one or two affine logarithmic barriers. This covers those lines as well.

## (b) Power-cone barrier
Let $`r=1/p\in(0,1]`$. Factor the log argument and write
```math
f(x,y)=[-\log(y^r-x)-\log y]
+[-\log(y^r+x)-\log y].
```
On a line $`x=as+b,y=s`$, each bracket has the rule's form with $`g_\pm(s)=\pm(as+b)-s^r`$. For $`r<1`$,
```math
g_\pm''=r(1-r)s^{r-2}\ge0,\qquad
|g_\pm'''|=(2-r)g_\pm''/s\le3g_\pm''/s.
```
For $`r=1`$ both higher derivatives vanish. Each bracket is self-concordant and so is their sum. At fixed $`y`$, the two log arguments are positive affine functions of the line parameter and give the result directly.

## (c) Exponential epigraph barrier
On a line with $`x=as+b,y=s`$, take $`g(s)=as+b-\log s`$. It is convex with $`g''=s^{-2}`$ and $`|g'''|=2s^{-3}\le3g''/s`$. Its composition is exactly $`-\log s-\log(\log s-as-b)`$. On a line of constant $`y`$, the only nonconstant term is a negative logarithm of a positive affine function, which is self-concordant by direct differentiation.

The three domains are convex: respectively a second-order cone interior, the epigraph of the convex function $`|x|^p`$, and the epigraph of the exponential. Thus the linewise checks establish the full multivariable claims.
