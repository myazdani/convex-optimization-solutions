# Exercise 8.22: Center of gravity minimizes integrated squared distance

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.22, printed page 450. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=464).

For the expression to be a finite-valued minimization, assume $`0<V:=\int_C1\,du<\infty`$ and $`\int_C\|u\|^2du<\infty`$, as holds for a bounded set of positive volume. Set $`c=V^{-1}\int_Cu\,du`$. Expansion and the equality $`\int_C(u-c)du=0`$ give
```math
\int_C\|u-x\|^2du
=\int_C\|u-c\|^2du+V\|x-c\|^2.
```
The first term is constant and the second is nonnegative with equality only at $`x=c`$. Therefore the center of gravity is the unique minimizer, and the objective is a strictly convex quadratic with Hessian $`2VI`$. Convexity of $`C`$ is not needed for this calculation; the integrability conditions are. Nonempty interior alone, as literally stated in the question, does not make the center of gravity or objective finite for an unbounded set such as $`\mathbb R^n`$.
