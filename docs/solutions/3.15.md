# Exercise 3.15: Power and logarithmic utility functions

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.15, p. 115](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=129).

Original solution.

## (a) Logarithmic limit

For fixed $`x>0`$, write $`x^\alpha=e^{\alpha\log x}`$. Differentiability of the exponential at zero gives
```math
\lim_{\alpha\to0}\frac{x^\alpha-1}{\alpha}
=\lim_{\alpha\to0}\frac{e^{\alpha\log x}-1}{\alpha}
=\log x.
```

## (b) Concavity, monotonicity, normalization

For $`0<\alpha\le1`$ and $`x>0`$,
```math
u_\alpha'(x)=x^{\alpha-1}>0,\qquad
u_\alpha''(x)=(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-2}\le0.
```
Thus the functions are increasing and concave on the positive halfline. Their continuous extension to zero has value $`-1/\alpha`$; passing to endpoint limits in the concavity inequality proves concavity on $`\mathbb R_+`$ as required. For $`\alpha=0`$, $`(\log x)'=1/x>0`$ and $`(\log x)''=-1/x^2<0`$ on its positive domain. Every function satisfies $`u_\alpha(1)=0`$. For $`\alpha=1`$ the function is affine; for $`0\le\alpha<1`$ it is strictly concave on the positive domain.
