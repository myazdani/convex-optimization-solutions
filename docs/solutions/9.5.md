# Exercise 9.5: An explicit backtracking bound

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.5, printed page 514. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=528).

Let $`g=\nabla f(x)`$, $`v=\Delta x`$, and $`g^Tv<0`$. On every candidate segment contained in the domain, the Hessian upper bound gives
```math
f(x+tv)\le f(x)+t g^Tv+\tfrac M2t^2\|v\|^2.
```
Thus the Armijo test with $`0<\alpha\le1/2`$ holds whenever
```math
t\le\frac{-2(1-\alpha)g^Tv}{M\|v\|^2}.
```
In particular it holds for $`0<t\le T=-g^Tv/(M\|v\|^2)`$, the bound requested. Starting at one and multiplying by $`\beta\in(0,1)`$, at most
```math
J=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{\log T}{\log\beta}\right\rceil\right\}
```
reductions are needed (and at most $`J+1`$ function tests). The sharper Armijo threshold can replace $`T`$. The lower strong-convexity constant is not used in this proof.

The assertion presumes these candidate points remain in the domain, as in a globally smooth objective on $`\mathbb R^n`$. For a restricted open domain, add its maximum feasible step to the threshold; a Hessian bound by itself cannot guarantee feasibility. Exercise 9.3 is a concrete reason this qualification matters.
