# Exercise 4.32: A first-order matching monomial

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.32](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=213). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write the desired positive monomial as $`\widehat f(x)=c\prod_i x_i^{a_i}`$. Its derivative is $`\partial_i\widehat f(x)=a_i\widehat f(x)/x_i`$. Matching both value and gradient at $`x^0\succ0`$ therefore uniquely determines

```math
a_i=\frac{x_i^0\partial_i f(x^0)}{f(x^0)},\qquad
c=f(x^0)\prod_i(x_i^0)^{-a_i}>0.
```

Equivalently,

```math
\widehat f(x)=f(x^0)\prod_i\left(\frac{x_i}{x_i^0}\right)^{x_i^0\partial_i f(x^0)/f(x^0)}.
```

Differentiability of both functions gives $`f(x)-\widehat f(x)=o(\|x-x^0\|)`$. This is the precise first-order meaning of being very close nearby; the supplied differentiability hypothesis does not justify a second-order error bound. Continuity and $`f(x^0)>0`$ ensure $`f`$ remains positive in a sufficiently small neighborhood.

Another interpretation is to linearize $`\log f(e^y)`$ at $`y^0=\log x^0`$ and exponentiate. Its gradient components are exactly the exponents above. The exponents may be negative, zero, or fractional, as allowed for geometric-programming monomials.

## Validation

The approximation matches every first partial derivative as well as the value, and uses no unwarranted convexity or smoothness assumption on $`f`$.
