# Exercise 4.4: Averaging under finite symmetries

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.4](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=203). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write $`R=(1/k)\sum_{Q\in G}Q`$; no orthogonality assumption is necessary.

## (a) Fixed points

For any $`P\in G`$, left multiplication $`Q\mapsto PQ`$ permutes the elements of the finite group. Thus $`PR=R`$, so $`Rx`$ belongs to the fixed subspace. Conversely, $`Rx=x`$ for every fixed point, so $`R`$ is a projection onto that subspace (not necessarily an orthogonal projection).

## (b) Objective improvement

Jensen's inequality and invariance give

```math
f(Rx)\le\frac1k\sum_{Q\in G}f(Qx)=f(x).
```

## (c) Optimization

Let $`x^\star`$ be any optimizer. Every orbit point $`Qx^\star`$ is feasible by feasible-set invariance. Their average is feasible because that set is convex. Part (b) shows its objective is no larger than the optimum, hence it is also optimal, and part (a) places it in the fixed subspace. Adding the linear equations $`(Q-I)x=0`$ therefore preserves the optimum and leaves at least one optimizer. Individual constraint functions need not be invariant. In fact the same averaging argument preserves the infimum even when it is unattained, by applying it to every feasible point.

## (d) Permutations

A vector fixed by every permutation has equal coordinates, because it is fixed by each transposition. Averaging all permutations of a vector gives $`(\mathbf1^Tx/n)\mathbf1`$. Applying (c) to the unconstrained minimization proves that a minimizer of the form $`\alpha\mathbf1`$ exists whenever any minimizer exists. The one-dimensional problem $`\min_t f(t\mathbf1)`$ has the same infimum.

## Validation

Only the finite-group permutation property, convexity, and feasible-set invariance were used. The proof does not assume individual constraints share the symmetry.
