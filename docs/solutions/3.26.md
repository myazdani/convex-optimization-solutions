# Exercise 3.26: Further convexity properties of eigenvalue functions

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.26, p. 118](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=132).

Original solution.

Use the variational identities supplied in the exercise, with $`1\le k\le n`$.

## (a) Sum of largest eigenvalues

For each fixed $`V`$ with $`V^TV=I`$, $`\mathrm{tr}(V^TXV)=\mathrm{tr}(VV^TX)`$ is linear in $`X`$. The given representation of the largest-$`k`$ eigenvalue sum as the supremum of these functions therefore proves convexity on $`\mathbb S^n`$.

## (b) Geometric mean of smallest eigenvalues

The stated representation is $`k^{-1}`$ times the infimum of the same linear expressions over the fixed index set $`\{V:\det(V^TV)=1\}`$. An infimum of affine functions is concave: evaluating at a mixture and bounding each summand by its own infimum gives the concavity inequality. Hence the smallest-$`k`$ geometric mean is concave on $`\mathbb S_{++}^n`$.

## (c) Logarithm of the smallest-eigenvalue product

For $`V^TV=I`$, each diagonal entry $`(V^TXV)_{ii}=v_i^TXv_i`$ is positive and affine in $`X\succ0`$. Thus
```math
h_V(X)=\sum_{i=1}^k\log(v_i^TXv_i)
```
is concave. The product identity and monotonicity and continuity of logarithm give
```math
\sum_{i=n-k+1}^n\log\lambda_i(X)
=\inf_{V^TV=I}h_V(X).
```
For fixed positive definite $`X`$, the infimum product is strictly positive, so no zero-log ambiguity occurs. A pointwise infimum of concave functions is concave, proving the claim. These arguments include $`k=1`$ and $`k=n`$ and require no differentiability of eigenvalues at multiplicities.
