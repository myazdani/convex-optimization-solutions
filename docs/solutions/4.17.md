# Exercise 4.17: Activity planning with quantity discounts

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.17](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=209). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

For nonnegative activity $`x_j`$, the concave revenue is

```math
r_j(x_j)=\min\{p_jx_j,\ p_j^{\mathrm{disc}}x_j+(p_j-p_j^{\mathrm{disc}})q_j\}.
```

The difference between the two affine expressions is $`(p_j-p_j^{\mathrm{disc}})(x_j-q_j)`$, proving the stated branch choice on either side of the discount threshold.

Introduce revenue variables $`y_j`$ and solve

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad &\sum_j y_j\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad &Ax\preceq c^{\max},\qquad x\succeq0,\\
&y_j\le p_jx_j,\\
&y_j\le p_j^{\mathrm{disc}}x_j+(p_j-p_j^{\mathrm{disc}})q_j,
\quad j=1,\ldots,n.
\end{aligned}
```

This is an LP (or negate its objective for minimization form). Any resource-feasible activity vector lifts with $`y_j=r_j(x_j)`$. Every feasible lift has total $`y`$ no greater than the true revenue, and at an optimizer each revenue inequality pair must attain its minimum, since raising any slack $`y_j`$ improves the objective without affecting other constraints. Hence the LP and the activity problem have identical supremum and corresponding optimizers.

Negative entries of $`A`$ are permitted and correctly represent generated resources. They can make the problem unbounded despite finite resource limits; the formulation does not assume boundedness. If the LP is feasible with a finite optimal value, linear-program attainment gives an optimal activity vector.

## Validation

The hypograph uses the minimum of the two price lines, and the signs of resource generation and maximization are preserved.
