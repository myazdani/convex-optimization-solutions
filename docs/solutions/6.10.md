# Exercise 6.10: Shape-constrained quadratic regression

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.10, printed page 347. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=361).

Concavity is exactly $`P\preceq0`$. Because the box has nonempty interior, coordinatewise nondecrease on it is equivalent to
```math
(Pz+q)_j\ge0\quad\text{for every }z\in[l,u],\quad j=1,\ldots,n.
```
Necessity follows from one-sided coordinate differences in the interior, followed by continuity to the boundary. Sufficiency follows by integrating the nonnegative gradient along the segment between any coordinatewise ordered pair.

Let $`c=(l+u)/2`$ and $`h=(u-l)/2\succ0`$. Minimizing an affine row of the gradient over the box gives the exact finite conditions
```math
(Pc+q)_j\ge\sum_k h_k|P_{jk}|,\qquad j=1,\ldots,n.
```
Once these monotonicity conditions hold, the minimum of $`f`$ over the box is $`f(l)`$. Thus nonnegativity requires only $`\tfrac12l^TPl+q^Tl+r\ge0`$; no enumeration of all box vertices is needed.

The complete convex formulation is
```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^N
(\tfrac12 x_i^TPx_i+q^Tx_i+r-y_i)^2\\
\text{subject to}&P=P^T,\quad P\preceq0,\\
&(Pc+q)_j\ge\sum_kh_kT_{jk},\quad T_{jk}\ge P_{jk},\ T_{jk}\ge-P_{jk},\\
&\tfrac12l^TPl+q^Tl+r\ge0.
\end{array}
```
Each fitted value is affine in the decision variables, so the objective is convex quadratic. The displayed program is a semidefinite-constrained convex QP; an ordinary SDP results by replacing the quadratic objective by $`s`$ and imposing $`\begin{bmatrix}s&e^T\\e&I_N\end{bmatrix}\succeq0`$, where $`e`$ is the vector of fitted errors. The auxiliary $`T`$ inequalities are exact since nonnegative $`h`$ makes their smallest feasible values the absolute values of $`P`$.
