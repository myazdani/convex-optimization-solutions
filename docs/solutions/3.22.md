# Exercise 3.22: Convexity by composition rules

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.22, p. 117](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=131).

Original solution.

## (a) A nested logarithm

Put $`L(x)=\log\sum_i e^{a_i^Tx+b_i}`$, a convex function. The domain is $`L(x)<0`$, a convex strict sublevel set. On $`s<0`$, $`h(s)=-\log(-s)`$ satisfies $`h'(s)=-1/s>0`$ and $`h''(s)=1/s^2>0`$. Thus $`h\circ L`$ is convex by the increasing convex composition rule.

## (b) Negative square root of a hyperbolic expression

For $`u>0`$, define $`a(x,u,v)=v-\|x\|_2^2/u`$. It is concave, and the domain is precisely $`u>0,a>0`$, a convex set. The function $`H(s,t)=-\sqrt{st}`$ is convex and nonincreasing in both positive arguments. Therefore
```math
-\sqrt{uv-\|x\|_2^2}=H(u,a(x,u,v))
```
is convex by the nonincreasing-convex/concave composition rule. The condition $`a>0,u>0`$ already implies $`v>0`$.

## (c) Negative logarithm of the same expression

On the same domain,
```math
-\log(uv-\|x\|_2^2)=-\log u-\log a(x,u,v).
```
The first term is convex; the second is convex because $`-\log`$ is convex and decreasing and $`a`$ is concave and positive. Their sum is convex.

## (d) Negative generalized cone radius

For $`t>0`$, let $`a(x,t)=t-\|x\|_p^p/t^{p-1}`$, which is concave by the given perspective fact. On $`a\ge0,t>0`$,
```math
-(t^p-\|x\|_p^p)^{1/p}=-a(x,t)^{1/p}t^{1-1/p}.
```
The negative weighted geometric mean is convex and nonincreasing in both nonnegative arguments. Composing with the concave $`a`$ and affine $`t`$ proves convexity where $`t>0`$. The domain condition is $`t\ge\|x\|_p`$, a convex norm cone. Its only point with $`t=0`$ is $`(0,0)`$, where the stated function is zero. Since its absolute value is at most $`t`$, the extension is continuous there. Taking limits from $`t>0`$ in the convexity inequality proves convexity on the entire stated closed cone, including its boundary.

## (e) Negative logarithm of the generalized radius power

The domain $`t>\|x\|_p`$ is convex and has $`t>0,a(x,t)>0`$. Factorization gives
```math
-\log(t^p-\|x\|_p^p)=-(p-1)\log t-\log a(x,t).
```
Both terms are convex by the same decreasing-convex composition rule, with $`p-1>0`$. This proves the final claim without asserting that the unfactored expression inside the logarithm is itself concave.
