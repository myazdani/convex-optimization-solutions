# Exercise 7.11: Decomposing randomized detectors

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.11, printed page 395. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=409).

For every function $`h:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}`$, let $`T_h`$ put a one in row $`h(k)`$ of column $`k`$ and zeros elsewhere. Define $`\theta_h=\prod_{k=1}^nT_{h(k),k}`$. Then $`\theta_h\ge0`$ and
```math
\sum_h\theta_h=\prod_k\sum_iT_{ik}=1.
```
The entry $`(i,k)`$ in $`\sum_h\theta_hT_h`$ is $`T_{ik}\prod_{j\ne k}\sum_rT_{rj}=T_{ik}`$. This constructs a decomposition using at most $`m^n`$ deterministic detectors.

The sharp worst-case bound is much smaller:
```math
N\le n(m-1)+1.
```
The affine space of matrices with unit column sums has dimension $`d=n(m-1)`$. If a decomposition has more than $`d+1`$ positive weights, its vertices are affinely dependent: there are numbers $`c_h`$, not all zero, with $`\sum_hc_hT_h=0`$ and $`\sum_hc_h=0`$. They have both signs. Replacing weights by $`\theta_h-tc_h`$, with $`t=\min_{c_h>0}\theta_h/c_h`$, preserves the matrix and total weight, keeps weights nonnegative, and removes at least one vertex. Repetition establishes the bound.

It is sharp in the worst case: the detector polytope has a nonempty relative interior of dimension $`d`$. There are finitely many sets of at most $`d`$ deterministic vertices, and each of their convex hulls lies in a proper affine subspace of dimension at most $`d-1`$. A finite union of such sets cannot cover the relative interior. Thus some randomized detectors require $`d+1`$ vertices. For $`m=1`$, the bound reduces correctly to one.
