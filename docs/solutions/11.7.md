# Exercise 11.7: The central-path tangent and objective monotonicity

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.7, printed page 624. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=638).

## (a) Tangent formula
Centering stationarity is $`t\nabla f_0(x(t))+\nabla\phi(x(t))=0`$. Where
```math
H_t=t\nabla^2 f_0(x(t))+\nabla^2\phi(x(t))\succ0,
```
the implicit-function theorem gives a differentiable path. Differentiating the stationarity equation yields
```math
H_t x'(t)+\nabla f_0(x(t))=0,
\qquad x'(t)=-H_t^{-1}\nabla f_0(x(t)).
```
A unique minimizer alone need not imply nonsingular Hessian; the formula presumes the usual nonsingular centering system. With equality constraints the same differentiation gives the corresponding KKT solve with $`Ax'(t)=0`$.

## (b) Objective decreases
Where the tangent formula applies,
```math
\frac d{dt}f_0(x(t))
=-\nabla f_0(x(t))^TH_t^{-1}\nabla f_0(x(t))\le0.
```
Equality holds only when the gradient vanishes in this unconstrained nonsingular setting. The monotonicity conclusion does not need differentiability of the path: for centers $`x_1,x_2`$ at $`t_1<t_2`$, their two minimizing inequalities imply, on addition,
```math
(t_2-t_1)(f_0(x_2)-f_0(x_1))\le0.
```
Thus the objective is nonincreasing under just the existence of the centers. “Decreases” need not be strict, for example if all centers coincide with an unconstrained optimum.
