# Exercise 4.33: Convex formulations of posynomial combinations

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.33](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=213). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

All original variables lie in the positive orthant. For any posynomial $`p`$, $`p(e^y)`$ is a positive sum of exponentials of affine functions, hence convex; $`\log p(e^y)`$ is convex by log-sum-exp.

## (a) Maximum of two posynomials

Use $`x=e^y`$ and minimize $`\max\{p(e^y),q(e^y)\}`$, a convex maximum of convex functions. Equivalently introduce an epigraph $`t>0`$, minimize $`t`$, and impose $`p(x)/t\le1`$, $`q(x)/t\le1`$. This is a GP; taking logs of all positive variables gives a convex formulation. The exact epigraph lift is $`t=\max(p(x),q(x))`$.

## (b) Sum of exponentiated posynomials

Under $`x=e^y`$, minimize

```math
\exp(p(e^y))+\exp(q(e^y)).
```

Each term is convex because the outer exponential is convex and increasing and the inner posynomial after substitution is convex. Their sum is convex. This direct convex expression is valid even though the original expression need not itself be a posynomial.

## (c) Posynomial over a positive difference

Introduce $`t>0`$. The epigraph inequality is exactly

```math
\frac{p(x)}{t r(x)}+\frac{q(x)}{r(x)}\le1.
```

The left side is a posynomial in $`x,t`$, since division by a positive monomial preserves that class. Moreover its first term is strictly positive, so it forces $`q(x)<r(x)`$. Conversely, whenever the original denominator is positive, the displayed inequality is equivalent to $`t\ge p(x)/(r(x)-q(x))`$. Thus minimizing $`t`$ is an exact GP. With $`y=\log x`$ and $`s=\log t`$, one may minimize $`s`$ subject to the log of the displayed posynomial being at most zero, a convex log-sum-exp constraint. The original optimum is the exponential of this logarithmic optimum.

## Validation

Each transformation has an inverse $`x=e^y`$; the epigraph cases have exact lifts. Strict positivity of the difference in (c) follows from the constraint rather than being lost in a non-strict relaxation.
