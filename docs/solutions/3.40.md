# Exercise 3.40: Derivatives of the conjugate via an inverse gradient

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.40, p. 121](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=135).

Original solution.

Let $`H=\nabla^2f(\overline x)\succ0`$ and $`\overline y=\nabla f(\overline x)`$. Work on the open domain where the stated twice-continuous differentiability holds. The inverse function theorem applied to $`\nabla f`$ gives neighborhoods of $`\overline x,\overline y`$ and a continuously differentiable local inverse $`x(y)`$ with
```math
\nabla f(x(y))=y,\qquad D x(y)=[\nabla^2f(x(y))]^{-1}.
```
Shrink the neighborhoods so these Hessians stay positive definite. Convexity makes each stationary point $`x(y)`$ a global maximizer of $`y^Tx-f(x)`$. It is unique: another minimizer of $`f(x)-y^Tx`$ would make the whole joining segment minimizing by convexity, contradicting strictly positive curvature in a neighborhood of $`x(y)`$.

Consequently, throughout the dual neighborhood,
```math
f^*(y)=y^Tx(y)-f(x(y)).
```
Differentiating and using $`\nabla f(x(y))=y`$ cancels the terms containing $`Dx(y)`$, leaving
```math
\nabla f^*(y)=x(y),\qquad
\nabla^2f^*(y)=Dx(y)=[\nabla^2f(x(y))]^{-1}.
```
At $`y=\overline y`$ these are exactly $`\nabla f^*(\overline y)=\overline x`$ and $`\nabla^2 f^*(\overline y)=H^{-1}`$. The local inverse also proves that $`\overline y`$ is an interior point of the conjugate domain, so the displayed derivatives are ordinary derivatives rather than merely subgradients.
