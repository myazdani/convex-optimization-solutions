# Exercise 11.5: A scalar-barrier formulation of an SOCP

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.5, printed page 623. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=637).

Write $`u_i=A_ix+b_i`$, $`s_i=c_i^Tx+d_i`$, and $`\delta_i=s_i^2-\|u_i\|^2`$. The strict domain is $`s_i>\|u_i\|`$ for every $`i`$.

## (a) Barrier identity
The two scalar inequalities for cone $`i`$ have positive slacks $`s_i-\|u_i\|^2/s_i=\delta_i/s_i`$ and $`s_i`$. Their barrier sum is
```math
-\log(\delta_i/s_i)-\log s_i=-\log\delta_i.
```
Thus $`\phi(x)=-\sum_i\log(s_i^2-\|u_i\|^2)`$, exactly the usual degree-two logarithmic barrier for each SOC in the generalized-inequality method.

## (b) A certificate valid for the original closed cones
At a minimizing center, define the SOC dual vectors
```math
z_i=\frac1{t\delta_i}(-2u_i,2s_i).
```
They lie in the interior of the self-dual SOC because $`s_i>\|u_i\|`$. If $`F_i(x)=(u_i,s_i)=D_ix+e_i`$, stationarity of the centering objective gives
```math
f=\sum_iD_i^Tz_i.
```
Therefore these vectors are feasible for the original SOCP dual, whose objective is $`-\sum_i e_i^Tz_i`$: the Lagrangian $`f^Tx-\sum_i z_i^TF_i(x)`$ becomes constant in $`x`$. Its gap at the center is
```math
f^Tx+\sum_i e_i^Tz_i
=\sum_i z_i^TF_i(x)=\sum_i\frac{2(s_i^2-\|u_i\|^2)}{t\delta_i}
=\frac{2m}{t}.
```
Weak duality applies to every original cone-feasible point, including cone apices omitted from the scalar functions' domains. Hence the center is $`2m/t`$-suboptimal for the original SOCP, not merely for its open-domain reformulation. Existence of a strict center for each chosen parameter is the same initialization/existence requirement as in the barrier method generally.
