# Exercise 5.35: Relative sensitivity of geometric programs

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.35](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=298). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Use logarithmic variables $`y=\log x`$. The transformed objective and constraints are $`\log f_i(e^y)`$ and $`\log h_j(e^y)`$; the exponential substitution is implicit in the source's shorthand. Let $`P=p^\star(0,0)>0`$ be finite, and define the transformed optimal value

```math
q^\star(u,v)=\log p^\star(u,v).
```

This equality follows from the increasing continuous logarithm on a positive finite infimum. Under the stated ordinary differentiability near zero, the value is locally positive and $`q^\star`$ is differentiable there. The usual convex-value sensitivity theorem (equivalently, continuity at an interior finite-value perturbation and the supporting-dual inequality) gives

```math
\nabla_u q^\star(0,0)=-\lambda^\star,\qquad
\nabla_v q^\star(0,0)=-\nu^\star.
```

To see the supporting relation, optimal dual variables under strong duality satisfy $`q^\star(u,v)\ge q^\star(0,0)-(\lambda^\star)^Tu-(\nu^\star)^Tv`$. Differentiability makes that supporting vector the unique gradient. Local finite-valued differentiability supplies the required continuity regularity; alternatively one may assume Slater for the convex-form GP directly.

The chain rule gives the derivatives in original objective units:

```math
\frac{\partial p^\star}{\partial u_i}(0,0)=-P\lambda_i^\star,
\qquad
\frac{\partial p^\star}{\partial v_j}(0,0)=-P\nu_j^\star.
```

Relaxing inequality $`i`$ by $`\alpha`$ percent multiplies its right-hand side by $`1+\alpha/100`$, so $`u_i=\log(1+\alpha/100)=\alpha/100+o(\alpha)`$. Therefore

```math
\frac{p^\star(u,0)-P}{P}=-\lambda_i^\star\frac{\alpha}{100}+o(\alpha).
```

The objective decreases by approximately $`\alpha\lambda_i^\star`$ percent. Nonnegative inequality multipliers give the expected direction. Equality multipliers can have either sign, so a positive multiplicative equality perturbation may improve or worsen the objective.

If the objective infimum is zero, its logarithm is $`-\infty`$ and relative percentage sensitivity is undefined; the ordinary finite convex-dual sensitivity statement requires the positive finite baseline used above. Positivity of each posynomial value alone does not exclude a zero unattained infimum.

## Validation

The multipliers differentiate log optimal value; multiplying by the baseline objective converts them to derivatives of the actual value. Percent units and exponential perturbation are kept distinct.
