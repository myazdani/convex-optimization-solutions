# Exercise 4.44: Maximizing a polynomial lower bound by SDP

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.44](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=217). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Introduce the desired global lower bound $`\gamma`$ and let $`v(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^k)^T`$. A real univariate polynomial of degree at most $`2k`$ is nonnegative everywhere if and only if it has a positive semidefinite Gram representation $`v(t)^TQv(t)`$. For completeness, necessity follows by factoring a nonnegative polynomial: every real root has even multiplicity and every conjugate pair supplies a positive quadratic. Each factor is a sum of two squares, and the identity $`(a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)=(ac-bd)^2+(ad+bc)^2`$ combines them into a sum of squares of degree at most $`k`$. Their coefficient vectors produce $`Q\succeq0`$. Sufficiency is immediate from the quadratic form. The zero polynomial is included.

Writing $`p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{2k}x_{j+1}t^j`$, the exact SDP is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad&\gamma\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad&Q\succeq0,\\
&\sum_{a+b=j}Q_{ab}=x_{j+1}-\gamma\,\mathbf1_{\{j=0\}},
\quad j=0,\ldots,2k,\\
&l_i\le\sum_{j=0}^{2k}x_{j+1}t_i^j\le u_i,
\quad i=1,\ldots,m.
\end{aligned}
```

Indices of $`Q`$ run from zero through $`k`$. The coefficient equalities say exactly $`p(t)-\gamma=v(t)^TQv(t)\ge0`$ for every real $`t`$.

Every SDP point yields a polynomial obeying the samples and a valid global lower bound. Conversely any sample-feasible polynomial with finite infimum can be lifted at every lower bound, including its infimum, by the univariate representation. Hence the maximization values agree. If no feasible polynomial is bounded below, no finite $`\gamma`$ is possible and the SDP is infeasible; this correctly represents the absence of any finite global lower bound. An optimizer is recovered from the polynomial coefficients whenever one exists; no general attainment assumption is needed.

## Validation

All coefficient matching equations are linear, and the univariate nonnegativity equivalence is exact rather than only an SOS relaxation.
