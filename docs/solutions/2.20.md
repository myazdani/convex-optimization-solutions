# Exercise 2.20: A theorem of alternatives for strictly positive solutions

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.20, p. 63](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=77).

Original solution.

## Linear-algebra lemma

Because $`b\in\mathcal R(A)`$, choose $`x_0`$ with $`Ax_0=b`$. The solution set is $`x_0+\ker A`$. If $`c^Tx=d`$ on this set, then $`c\perp\ker A`$, hence $`c=A^T\lambda`$ for some $`\lambda`$; evaluating at $`x_0`$ gives $`d=b^T\lambda`$. Conversely these two identities immediately give $`c^Tx=\lambda^TAx=\lambda^Tb=d`$ on the solution set.

## The alternatives

If $`x>0`$ and $`Ax=b`$, any $`\lambda`$ with $`A^T\lambda\ge0`$ and $`A^T\lambda\ne0`$ satisfies
```math
b^T\lambda=x^TA^T\lambda>0.
```
Thus the two systems cannot both be feasible.

Conversely suppose $`L=\{x:Ax=b\}`$ does not meet the open positive orthant. The separating-hyperplane theorem gives $`c\ne0`$ and $`\delta`$ such that
```math
c^Tx\le\delta\le c^Tz\qquad(x\in L,\ z>0).
```
Since $`L=x_0+\ker A`$, boundedness above forces $`c\perp\ker A`$, so $`c^Tx=d_0`$ on $`L`$. On the orthant, if some $`c_i<0`$, making $`z_i`$ arbitrarily large violates the lower bound; thus $`c\ge0`$. Taking $`z\to0`$ through positive vectors gives $`\delta\le0`$, and hence $`d_0\le0`$. The lemma provides $`\lambda`$ with
```math
A^T\lambda=c\ge0,\qquad A^T\lambda\ne0,\qquad b^T\lambda=d_0\le0.
```
This is the required certificate. The assumption $`b\in\mathcal R(A)`$ is used to make the affine solution set nonempty and the lemma valid.
