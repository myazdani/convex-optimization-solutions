# Exercise 9.25: Hessian structure for one- and two-dimensional smoothing

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.25, printed page 518. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=532).

Assume the convex penalty is twice differentiable at the residuals, as required to form an ordinary Newton Hessian.

## (a) One-dimensional fit
Let $`D`$ be the first-difference matrix, $`(Dx)_i=x_{i+1}-x_i`$. Then
```math
\nabla f=\psi'(x-y)+2\lambda D^TDx,
\qquad H=\mathrm{diag}(\psi''(x_i-y_i))+2\lambda D^TD.
```
For $`n>1`$, the Hessian is tridiagonal: the smoothing contribution has diagonal $`2\lambda`$ at the endpoints, $`4\lambda`$ in the interior, and adjacent off-diagonal entries $`-2\lambda`$. For $`n=1`$ the smoothing term is absent. A positive-definite tridiagonal system factors and solves in $`O(n)`$ time and storage. Since the path Laplacian's nullspace consists of constants, a nonnegative data diagonal makes $`H`$ positive definite exactly when at least one data second derivative is positive.

## (b) Two-dimensional grid
Vectorize the $`n\times n`$ array, and let $`L_n=D^TD`$ be the one-dimensional path Laplacian. The Hessian is
```math
H=\mathrm{diag}(\psi''(x_{ij}-y_{ij}))
+2\lambda(L_n\otimes I+I\otimes L_n).
```
Each pixel couples only to its at most four horizontal/vertical neighbors. Its diagonal smoothing coefficient is $`2\lambda`$ times its grid degree, and each neighbor coefficient is $`-2\lambda`$. In row ordering this is block tridiagonal with tridiagonal diagonal blocks and diagonal off-diagonal blocks. The matrix has $`O(n^2)`$ nonzeros rather than being a dense $`n^2\times n^2`$ matrix.

Banded Cholesky with bandwidth $`n`$ costs $`O(n^4)`$ time and $`O(n^3)`$ storage; a grid nested-dissection ordering improves factorization to $`O(n^3)`$ time and $`O(n^2\log n)`$ storage. Matrix-vector products cost $`O(n^2)`$, enabling preconditioned iterative solves when appropriate. As in one dimension, positive definiteness holds if at least one penalty second derivative is positive on the connected grid; otherwise the constant mode is unpenalized. A nonsmooth penalty requires a smoothing or nonsmooth method instead of these classical Hessian formulas.
