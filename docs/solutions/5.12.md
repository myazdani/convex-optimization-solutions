# Exercise 5.12: The analytic-center dual

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.12](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=290). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Introduce positive slacks $`y=b-Ax`$ and use multipliers $`\nu`$ for $`y-b+Ax=0`$. The Lagrangian is

```math
L=-\sum_i\log y_i+\nu^T(y-b+Ax),\qquad y\succ0.
```

The infimum over unrestricted $`x`$ is finite only if $`A^T\nu=0`$. For one positive slack,

```math
\inf_{y>0}(-\log y+\nu y)=\begin{cases}
1+\log\nu,&\nu>0,\\
-\infty,&\nu\le0.
\end{cases}
```

For positive $`\nu`$ this follows from stationarity $`y=1/\nu`$ and convexity; for nonpositive $`\nu`$, let $`y\to\infty`$. Therefore the dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad m+\sum_i\log\nu_i-b^T\nu\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad A^T\nu=0,\quad\nu\succ0.
```

The objective is concave on its domain and the constraint is affine. If the primal strict domain is nonempty and its value is finite, the log-barrier attainment result ensures a minimizer exists, and affine-equality regularity yields strong duality. The KKT relation is $`\nu_i^\star=1/(b_i-a_i^Tx^\star)`$; then $`A^T\nu^\star=0`$ is exactly primal stationarity. It also gives $`b^T\nu^\star=m`$, since $`\nu^T(b-Ax)=m`$, verifying that the dual objective equals the primal barrier value.

If no positive null vector $`\nu`$ exists but the primal domain is nonempty, the barrier is unbounded below by the theorem of alternatives established in Exercise 4.2. If the primal domain is empty, the formula remains a valid dual derivation but the preceding attainment/strong-duality assertion is not invoked.

## Validation

Strict positivity of dual slacks is an effective-domain requirement, including at zero multipliers; it is not replaceable by weak nonnegativity.
