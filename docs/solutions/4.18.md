# Exercise 4.18: Separating points by hyperplanes or spheres

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.18](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=209). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Assume both finite point sets are nonempty, as in the separation question.

## (a) A nonzero weak separator

Introduce the nonnegative margins implicitly as $`b-a^Tv^i`$ and $`a^Tw^j-b`$. Use the LP feasibility system

```math
\begin{aligned}
a^Tv^i&\le b,\quad i=1,\ldots,K,\\
a^Tw^j&\ge b,\quad j=1,\ldots,L,\\
\sum_{i=1}^K(b-a^Tv^i)+\sum_{j=1}^L(a^Tw^j-b)&\ge1.
\end{aligned}
```

The final inequality is affine. If $`a=0`$, the first two families force $`b=0`$, contradicting the last inequality. Thus every feasible point yields a genuine separator.

Conversely, let $`a\ne0,b`$ weakly separate the sets. All margins are nonnegative. If their sum were zero, every point would satisfy $`a^Tx=b`$. The augmented-data full-row-rank assumption then forces $`(a,-b)=0`$, a contradiction. The margin sum is therefore positive; scaling $`a,b`$ by its reciprocal makes the last inequality hold with equality. Thus the LP detects every separator under the given rank assumption, including separators touching some points. A strict positive margin at every point would incorrectly exclude valid weak separators.

## (b) Sphere separator

Let the variables be the center $`c\in\mathbb R^n`$ and $`s\in\mathbb R`$, where $`s=R^2-\|c\|_2^2`$. Squaring the nonnegative distances and canceling $`\|c\|_2^2`$ gives the linear system

```math
\|v^i\|_2^2-2(v^i)^Tc\le s,\quad i=1,\ldots,K,
\qquad
\|w^j\|_2^2-2(w^j)^Tc\ge s,\quad j=1,\ldots,L.
```

From a feasible solution recover $`R=\sqrt{s+\|c\|_2^2}`$. This square root is real: any inside point gives $`s+\|c\|_2^2\ge\|v^i-c\|_2^2\ge0`$. The squared inequalities then imply precisely the required distance inequalities. Conversely any separating sphere supplies a feasible $`(c,s)`$ by the defining substitution. Zero radius and boundary points are allowed.

## Validation

The hyperplane normalization uses the stated rank hypothesis rather than a nonconvex norm equality. The sphere formulation includes a proof that its recovered radius is nonnegative without adding a nonconvex constraint.
