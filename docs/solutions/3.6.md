# Exercise 3.6: Halfspace, conic, and polyhedral epigraphs

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.6, p. 114](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=128).

Original solution.

Assume a nonempty function domain and finite function values on that domain; outside it use the value $`+\infty`$.

## Halfspace

The epigraph is a halfspace exactly when the domain is all of $`\mathbb R^n`$ and $`f(x)=a^Tx+b`$ is affine. Indeed, write a halfspace epigraph as $`u^Tx+vt\le w`$. Its closure under raising $`t`$ forces $`v\le0`$. The case $`v=0`$ would include every real $`t`$ over any feasible $`x`$, impossible for a finite-valued function there. Thus $`v<0`$, and solving for $`t`$ gives the affine lower boundary over all $`x`$.

## Convex cone

The epigraph is a convex cone exactly when $`f`$ is convex and positively homogeneous, its domain is a convex cone containing zero, and $`f(0)=0`$. Here positive homogeneity means $`f(\lambda x)=\lambda f(x)`$ for $`\lambda>0`$. Epigraph convexity is equivalent to function convexity. Epigraph scaling in both directions for positive $`\lambda`$ forces equality of its lower boundaries, giving homogeneity. Containment of the origin gives $`f(0)\le0`$; applying homogeneity at zero gives $`f(0)=0`$. Conversely these properties directly make the epigraph convex and closed under nonnegative scaling. Such functions are called sublinear; extended-valued domains allow cone restrictions.

## Polyhedron

The epigraph is a polyhedron exactly when the domain is a polyhedron $`P`$ and on it $`f`$ is the maximum of finitely many affine functions:
```math
f(x)=\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}(a_i^Tx+b_i),\qquad x\in P.
```
This formula yields the polyhedral epigraph through the constraints $`x\in P`$ and $`t\ge a_i^Tx+b_i`$. Conversely, write a polyhedral epigraph using finitely many linear inequalities and equalities in $`(x,t)`$. Upward closure in $`t`$ forces every inequality's $`t`$ coefficient to be nonpositive and every equality's to be zero. Zero coefficients define a polyhedral domain; negative coefficients yield the finitely many affine lower bounds on $`t`$. At least one lower bound is needed to avoid an entire vertical line. Taking their maximum gives the formula. Thus this is precisely a closed convex piecewise-affine function on a polyhedral domain, not an arbitrary piecewise-affine function.
