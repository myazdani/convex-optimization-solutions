# Exercise 6.6: Duals of four separable penalties

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.6, printed page 345. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=359).

Use the Lagrangian $`L=\sum_i\phi(r_i)+\nu^T(Ax-b-r)`$. Infimizing in $`x,r`$ gives the common dual
```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&-b^T\nu-\sum_i\phi^*(\nu_i)\\
\text{subject to}&A^T\nu=0,
\end{array}
\qquad \phi^*(v)=\sup_u(vu-\phi(u)).
```
Here are explicit conjugates and hence complete dual objectives and domains.

## (a) Deadzone-linear penalty
For $`|v|>1`$, sending $`u`$ to infinity with matching sign gives an infinite conjugate. For $`|v|\le1`$, maximizing over $`[-1,1]`$ gives $`|v|`$, and either exterior ray gives no larger value. Hence
```math
\phi^*(v)=|v|\quad(|v|\le1),
```
and the dual maximizes $`-b^T\nu-\|\nu\|_1`$ subject to $`A^T\nu=0,\ \|\nu\|_\infty\le1`$.

## (b) Huber penalty
Its exterior slopes are $`\pm2`$, so the conjugate is finite only for $`|v|\le2`$. In that interval the interior maximizer $`u=v/2`$ yields $`\phi^*(v)=v^2/4`$, including the endpoints. Thus maximize $`-b^T\nu-\|\nu\|_2^2/4`$ subject to $`A^T\nu=0,\ \|\nu\|_\infty\le2`$.

## (c) Logarithmic barrier
The maximizer of $`vu+\log(1-u^2)`$ is the unique point satisfying $`v=2u/(1-u^2)`$. With $`s=\sqrt{1+v^2}`$, write it stably as $`u=v/(1+s)`$; this includes $`v=0`$. Since $`vu=s-1`$ and $`1-u^2=2/(1+s)`$,
```math
\phi^*(v)=\sqrt{1+v^2}-1+\log\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+v^2}},
\qquad v\in\mathbb R.
```
Substitute this expression in the common dual, with no additional bound on $`\nu`$.

## (d) Relative deviation
On $`0<u\le1`$, maximize $`vu-1/u`$; on $`u\ge1`$, maximize $`(v-1)u`$. If $`v>1`$ the second supremum is infinite. If $`-1\le v\le1`$, both branches are maximized at $`u=1`$, giving $`v-1`$. If $`v<-1`$, the first branch has maximizer $`u=1/\sqrt{-v}`$, giving $`-2\sqrt{-v}`$, which dominates $`v-1`$. Therefore
```math
\phi^*(v)=\begin{cases}
-2\sqrt{-v},&v\lt -1,\\
v-1,&-1\le v\le1,\\
+\infty,&v>1.
\end{cases}
```
The dual is the common formula with $`\nu_i\le1`$ and this piecewise objective. In all four cases the formula is valid as a weak dual even when the primal is infeasible. For (a),(b), the penalty is finite everywhere, so convex affine-equality regularity gives equality of finite optimal values. For (c),(d), the same conclusion holds whenever some $`x`$ has residual in the open penalty domain; this is the precise required feasibility condition.
