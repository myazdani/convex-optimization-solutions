# Exercise 6.11: Minimum-norm direction interpolation

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.11, printed page 347. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=361).

For each sample put $`B_j=[F_1(v_j)\ \cdots\ F_n(v_j)]\in\mathbb R^{p\times n}`$, so $`F(v_j)=B_jx`$. Since $`\|q_j\|_2=1`$, a nonzero vector has angle zero with $`q_j`$ exactly when it equals a positive multiple of $`q_j`$. Its length is then that multiple.

Thus the direction and minimum-length requirements together are exactly
```math
B_jx=\alpha_jq_j,\qquad\alpha_j\ge\epsilon,
\qquad j=1,\ldots,m.
```
Minimize $`\tfrac12\|x\|_2^2`$ subject to these affine constraints, a convex QP with the same minimizer as $`\|x\|_2`$. Alternatively eliminate $`\alpha_j`$ and impose
```math
(I-q_jq_j^T)B_jx=0,\qquad q_j^TB_jx\ge\epsilon.
```
The sign inequality matters: a collinear vector in the opposite direction has angle $`\pi`$. If the affine inequalities are feasible, their closedness and the coercive strictly convex objective ensure existence and uniqueness of $`x`$. Infeasibility correctly reports that the specified directions cannot be interpolated by the given linear span.
