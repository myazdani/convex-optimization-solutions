# Exercise 5.18: Strict polyhedral separation through LP multipliers

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.18](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=292). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

For two nonempty polyhedra, use nonnegative vectors $`\lambda,\mu`$, a normal $`a`$, and a level $`\gamma`$. The following is an LP feasibility system:

```math
\begin{aligned}
A^T\lambda&=-a,&C^T\mu&=a,\\
-b^T\lambda&\ge\gamma+1,&d^T\mu&\le\gamma-1,\\
\lambda&\succeq0,&\mu&\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
```

For every $`x\in P_1`$, multiplication by $`\lambda`$ gives $`a^Tx\ge-b^T\lambda\ge\gamma+1`$. For every $`y\in P_2`$, multiplication by $`\mu`$ gives $`a^Ty\le d^T\mu\le\gamma-1`$. Thus every feasible point gives strict separation with a positive margin. Nonemptiness of both sets excludes $`a=0`$ automatically.

Conversely, disjoint nonempty polyhedra have positive attained distance. One way to see this is that their difference $`P_1-P_2`$ is a closed polyhedron (a projection of a polyhedron), does not contain zero, and has an attained nearest point to zero. The corresponding closest pair supplies a strict separating normal by first-order optimality. Its minimum and maximum affine levels on the two polyhedra are finite and attained. Scale the normal and choose a midpoint level so that both gaps are at least one. The two bounded support LPs then have attained dual optima: minimizing $`a^Tx`$ over $`P_1`$ gives multiplier condition $`A^T\lambda=-a`$ and value $`-b^T\lambda`$; maximizing over $`P_2`$ gives $`C^T\mu=a`$ and value $`d^T\mu`$. These multipliers satisfy the displayed system.

Equivalently eliminate $`a,\gamma`$ and solve $`A^T\lambda+C^T\mu=0`$, $`-b^T\lambda-d^T\mu\ge2`$, $`\lambda,\mu\succeq0`$, then recover $`a=C^T\mu`$ and any level in $`[d^T\mu+1,-b^T\lambda-1]`$.

Empty input polyhedra can be detected first by LP feasibility. If both are empty, any nonzero normal and level works vacuously. If only $`P_1`$ is empty and the nonempty $`P_2`$ is a proper subset of space, choose any nonzero defining row $`c_j^Tx\le d_j`$, take $`a=c_j`$ and $`\gamma=d_j+1`$. If only $`P_2`$ is empty, use a nonzero defining row $`a_i^Tx\le b_i`$ of $`P_1`$ and take separator normal $`a=-a_i`$, level $`\gamma=-b_i-1`$. A nonempty proper polyhedron has such a row after discarding constant inequalities. If the nonempty set is all of $`\mathbb R^n`$, no nonzero-normal strict separator exists despite disjointness. Thus the usual genuine-hyperplane assertion needs nonempty sets or these separate cases; the multiplier system alone may return a zero normal for a vacuous separation. No separation claim about arbitrary disjoint closed convex sets is used: polyhedrality supplies closedness of the difference and a positive gap.

## Validation

The support-dual signs, two unit margins, and nonzero-normal guarantee under nonempty sets are all checked.
