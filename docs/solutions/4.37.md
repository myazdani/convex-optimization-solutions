# Exercise 4.37: Lifting generalized posynomials into a GP

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.37](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=214). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

For each occurrence of a generalized posynomial $`h_i(x)=\phi_i(f_{i1}(x),\ldots,f_{ik_i}(x))`$, introduce positive upper bounds $`z_{ij}`$ and impose

```math
f_{ij}(x)/z_{ij}\le1.
```

These are posynomial inequalities because each denominator is a monomial in a new variable. Replace $`h_i`$ by the ordinary posynomial $`\phi_i(z_i)`$. Keep the original monomial equalities. Thus the lifted GP minimizes $`\phi_0(z_0)`$ subject to $`\phi_i(z_i)\le1`$ for all constraint indices, the displayed bounds, and the unchanged monomial equalities.

Every exponent of every argument in $`\phi_i`$ is nonnegative, so $`\phi_i`$ is coordinatewise nondecreasing on the positive orthant. Consequently $`z_{ij}\ge f_{ij}(x)`$ implies

```math
h_i(x)\le\phi_i(z_i).
```

Thus a lifted feasible point projects to an original feasible $`x`$, and its objective upper-bounds the original one. Conversely every original feasible point lifts with $`z_{ij}=f_{ij}(x)>0`$, preserving every generalized value and the objective exactly. These two directions give identical infima and optimizer recovery; if a lifted optimizer has slack in a zero-exponent argument, that causes no difficulty. Repeated inner posynomials can share one upper-bound variable without changing the proof.

For recursively nested generalized posynomials, repeat this construction from the innermost nodes outward. Nonnegative exponents at each outer node ensure the same monotonicity argument. Negative outer exponents would reverse monotonicity and invalidate this upper-bound lift, which explains the source's restriction.

## Validation

All added constraints are ordinary GP constraints, the new variables are positive, and the feasible-set projection and objective inequalities establish full equivalence.
