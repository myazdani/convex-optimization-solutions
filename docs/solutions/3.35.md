# Exercise 3.35: Support-function identities and set inclusion

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.35, p. 120](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=134).

Original solution.

Use nonempty sets unless stated otherwise, so support values lie in $`\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}`$.

## (a) Convex hull

A linear functional at a finite convex combination is at most its supremum over the original set. Hence $`S_{\mathrm{conv}\,B}(y)\le S_B(y)`$. The reverse inequality follows from $`B\subseteq\mathrm{conv}\,B`$. Thus the functions are equal.

## (b) Minkowski sum

For nonempty $`A,B`$,
```math
S_{A+B}(y)=\sup_{a\in A,b\in B}(y^Ta+y^Tb)
=\sup_{a\in A}y^Ta+\sup_{b\in B}y^Tb=S_A(y)+S_B(y).
```
The upper bound is immediate; the lower bound follows by choosing independently arbitrarily near-maximizing points. If a supremum is infinite, keep a fixed point in the other set and take an unbounded maximizing sequence.

## (c) Union

A supremum over a union is the maximum of the two suprema, giving $`S_{A\cup B}(y)=\max\{S_A(y),S_B(y)\}`$.

## (d) Inclusion in a closed convex set

Inclusion $`A\subseteq B`$ immediately implies $`S_A\le S_B`$. Conversely, if some $`a\in A\setminus B`$ exists, strict separation from nonempty closed convex $`B`$ yields a vector $`y`$ with $`y^Ta>S_B(y)`$. Since $`S_A(y)\ge y^Ta`$, the support inequality fails. Thus the support inequalities imply inclusion.

Empty sets can be handled with $`S_\varnothing=-\infty`$: (a), (c), and (d) retain their natural meanings. In (b), an empty summand makes the Minkowski sum empty; when the other support is $`+\infty`$, the written sum $`-\infty+(+\infty)`$ is undefined, so the nonempty-set qualification avoids that extended-arithmetic ambiguity.
