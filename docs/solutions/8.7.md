# Exercise 8.7: Strict separation of two ellipsoids

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.7, printed page 448. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=462).

The support extrema of $`E_i=x_i+P_i^{1/2}B_2`$ in direction $`a`$ are
```math
\max_{x\in E_i}a^Tx=a^Tx_i+\|P_i^{1/2}a\|_2,
\qquad
\min_{x\in E_i}a^Tx=a^Tx_i-\|P_i^{1/2}a\|_2.
```
The inequality in the question is exactly $`\max_{E_2}a^Tx<\min_{E_1}a^Tx`$, and hence implies disjointness.

Conversely, disjoint ellipsoids are disjoint compact convex sets. Choose a closest pair $`p\in E_1,q\in E_2`$; their distance is positive. Put $`a=p-q\ne0`$. First-order optimality along every segment in the two convex sets yields
```math
a^T(x-p)\ge0\ (x\in E_1),\qquad
a^T(y-q)\le0\ (y\in E_2).
```
Their supporting levels have gap $`a^T(p-q)=\|p-q\|^2>0`$. Substituting the support extrema above proves the stated strict inequality. Compactness matters: it turns disjointness into a positive separation gap.
