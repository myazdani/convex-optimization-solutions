# Exercise 10.11: Linear-time Newton steps for resource allocation

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.11, printed page 559. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=573).

The problem is $`\min\sum_i f_i(x_i)`$ subject to $`\sum_i x_i=b`$. Put $`g_i=f_i'(x_i)`$, $`d_i=f_i''(x_i)\ge m>0`$, and count scalar function/derivative evaluations as constant work.

## (a) Reduced formulation
Eliminate $`x_n=b-\sum_{i<n}x_i`$. The reduced gradient has entries $`g_i-g_n`$ and the Hessian is $`D+d_n\mathbf1\mathbf1^T`$, where $`D=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1})`$. Sherman–Morrison gives
```math
(D+d_n\mathbf1\mathbf1^T)^{-1}
=D^{-1}-\frac{D^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^TD^{-1}}
{d_n^{-1}+\mathbf1^TD^{-1}\mathbf1}.
```
Applying this to the negative reduced gradient needs only componentwise operations and scalar sums, hence $`O(n)`$ time and storage rather than dense cubic work.

## (b) Scalar dual
With the sign convention $`\nu(\sum_i x_i-b)`$, the dual is $`g(\nu)=-b\nu-\sum_i f_i^*(-\nu)`$. Its minimizing primal coordinates satisfy $`f_i'(x_i(\nu))=-\nu`$. The assumed readily computable scalar inverses give
```math
g'(\nu)=\sum_i x_i(\nu)-b,\qquad
g''(\nu)=-\sum_i\frac1{f_i''(x_i(\nu))},
\qquad\Delta\nu=\frac{\sum_i x_i(\nu)-b}{\sum_i1/f_i''(x_i(\nu))}.
```
Each evaluation and Newton step costs $`O(n)`$. At a dual optimum, the recovered coordinates meet the budget and stationarity, hence solve the primal.

## (c) Direct KKT formulation
At a feasible point the Newton equations are $`d_i v_i+w=-g_i`$ and $`\sum_i v_i=0`$. Therefore
```math
w=-\frac{\sum_i g_i/d_i}{\sum_i1/d_i},\qquad
v_i=-\frac{g_i+w}{d_i}.
```
Again this is $`O(n)`$ work, with no dense factorization. All three approaches have the same linear per-step arithmetic order when structure is exploited; the dual approach additionally assumes the scalar inverse/conjugate operations stipulated in the question. If $`n=1`$, the budget already fixes the primal variable and no reduced Newton solve remains.
