# Exercise 9.7: Steepest descent and the dual norm

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.7, printed page 515. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=529).

Write $`g=\nabla f(x)`$ and let $`\|g\|_* =\max_{\|u\|\le1}g^Tu`$. Compactness of the unit ball makes the maximum attained.

## (a) Normalized direction
By symmetry of the norm ball, minimizing $`g^Tu`$ on it gives $`-\|g\|_*`$. This proves $`g^T\Delta x_{\rm nsd}=-\|g\|_*`$. If $`g\ne0`$, every minimizing direction has norm one; if $`g=0`$, every ball point is optimal.

## (b) Unnormalized direction
By definition $`\Delta x_{\rm sd}=\|g\|_*\Delta x_{\rm nsd}`$. Multiplying the identity in (a) gives $`g^T\Delta x_{\rm sd}=-\|g\|_*^2`$.

## (c) Quadratically regularized linear model
For any $`v`$, dual-norm Hölder inequality gives
```math
g^Tv+\tfrac12\|v\|^2
\ge-\|g\|_*\|v\|+\tfrac12\|v\|^2
=\tfrac12(\|v\|-\|g\|_*)^2-\tfrac12\|g\|_*^2.
```
The unnormalized direction attains both inequalities, so it minimizes this expression. Conversely every minimizer for $`g\ne0`$ must have norm $`\|g\|_*`$ and attain the negative dual pairing, hence is a scaled normalized steepest direction. Thus the exercise's argmin identity is set-valued if the norm has a nonunique exposed face. For $`g=0`$, the regularized problem has the unique minimizer zero.
