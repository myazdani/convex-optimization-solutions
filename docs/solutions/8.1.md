# Exercise 8.1: Uniqueness of metric projection

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.1, printed page 447. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=461).

Fix $`x_0`$. Choose a point $`c\in C`$ and restrict the distance minimization to $`C\cap\{x:\|x-x_0\|\le\|c-x_0\|\}`$. This set is nonempty, closed and bounded, hence compact in finite dimension. Continuity of the norm gives a nearest point.

If two distinct nearest points $`p,q`$ existed, their common distance $`d`$ would be positive (distance zero permits only $`x_0`$). Strict convexity of the norm's unit ball gives
```math
\left\|\frac{p+q}{2}-x_0\right\|
=d\left\|\frac{(p-x_0)/d+(q-x_0)/d}{2}\right\|<d.
```
Their midpoint is in $`C`$, contradicting minimality. Thus the projection exists and is unique. Here “strictly convex norm” means strict convexity of its unit ball, not strict convexity along a ray, which no norm has.
