# Exercise 3.53: Log-concavity of the density of an independent sum

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.53, p. 123](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=137).

Original solution.

Independence gives the density of $`z=x+y`$ as the convolution
```math
h(z)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(u)g(z-u)\,du.
```
Define $`H(z,u)=f(u)g(z-u)`$. It is jointly log-concave: where positive, its log is $`\log f(u)+\log g(z-u)`$, a sum of concave functions composed with affine maps. Including zeros, the same statement follows directly from the multiplicative log-concavity inequality; the support is convex as an intersection of affine inverse images of the two convex supports.

The marginalization theorem for log-concave functions (the integration-preserves-log-concavity result of §3.5.2) therefore implies that integrating $`H`$ over $`u`$ leaves a log-concave function of $`z`$. Thus $`h`$ is log-concave. Tonelli's theorem gives $`\int h(z)\,dz=(\int f)(\int g)=1`$, verifying that the marginal is indeed a probability density. Independence is used specifically in the product-convolution formula.
