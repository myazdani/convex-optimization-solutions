# Exercise 4.14: Matrix approximation in the induced infinity norm

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.14](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=208). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write $`B(x)=A_0+\sum_{\ell=1}^k x_\ell A_\ell`$. Introduce an $`m\times n`$ matrix $`U`$ of entrywise absolute-value bounds and a scalar $`t`$ for the largest row sum. The LP is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{minimize}\quad&t\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad&-U_{ij}\le B(x)_{ij}\le U_{ij},\quad i=1,\ldots,m,\ j=1,\ldots,n,\\
&\sum_{j=1}^nU_{ij}\le t,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.
\end{aligned}
```

Every constraint is affine because the entries of $`B`$ are affine in $`x`$. The first pair automatically forces $`U_{ij}\ge0`$.

For any $`x`$, take $`U_{ij}=|B(x)_{ij}|`$ and $`t=\max_i\sum_j|B(x)_{ij}|`$. This is an LP-feasible lift with exactly the original objective. Conversely any LP-feasible point satisfies

```math
\|B(x)\|_\infty=\max_i\sum_j|B(x)_{ij}|
\le\max_i\sum_jU_{ij}\le t.
```

These two directions prove equality of optimal values. Retaining $`x`$ from an LP optimizer gives an original optimizer; the exact lift of any original optimizer gives an LP optimizer. Some entries of $`U`$ can remain slack at an optimum if they do not change the largest row bound, so componentwise tightness of $`U`$ is not necessary.

The optimum is attained: the matrices $`B(x)`$ form a closed affine subspace in finite-dimensional matrix space, and the norm has compact bounded sublevel sets there. An optimal matrix has an $`x`$ preimage, even if that preimage is nonunique.

## Validation

The formulation uses the maximum of absolute row sums, not the maximum absolute entry. Auxiliary feasibility and both optimizer maps are proved explicitly.
