# Exercise 5.5: The dual of a general linear program

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.5](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=288). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Assign multipliers $`\lambda\succeq0`$ to $`Gx-h\preceq0`$ and unrestricted $`\nu`$ to $`Ax-b=0`$. The Lagrangian is

```math
L(x,\lambda,\nu)
=(c+G^T\lambda+A^T\nu)^Tx-h^T\lambda-b^T\nu.
```

The infimum of a nonconstant affine function over unrestricted $`x\in\mathbb R^n`$ is $`-\infty`$. If its coefficient is zero, it is constant. Consequently

```math
g(\lambda,\nu)=\begin{cases}
-h^T\lambda-b^T\nu,&G^T\lambda+A^T\nu+c=0,\\
-\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
```

Making the effective-domain equality explicit gives the dual LP

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad&-h^T\lambda-b^T\nu\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad&G^T\lambda+A^T\nu+c=0,\qquad\lambda\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
```

Equality multipliers are free, and no rank assumption is needed. The formula is valid even if either LP is infeasible. If the primal has a finite optimum, the usual finite-dimensional LP strong-duality theorem gives equality of primal and dual values with an attained dual optimum; this is not a consequence of the algebra alone.

## Validation

All constants and signs follow from writing the constraints as left side minus right side, and the implicit stationarity equality is included explicitly.
