# Exercise 3.32: Products and ratios on an interval

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.32, p. 119](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=133).

Original solution.

Let $`z=\theta x+(1-\theta)y`$ with $`0\le\theta\le1`$. The identity used below is
```math
[\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)]
[\theta g(x)+(1-\theta)g(y)]
=\theta f(x)g(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)g(y)
-\theta(1-\theta)[f(x)-f(y)][g(x)-g(y)].
```

## (a) Convex functions with matching monotonicity

Convexity bounds $`f(z)`$ and $`g(z)`$ above by their endpoint averages. All quantities are positive, so multiplying preserves the bounds. Matching monotonicity makes the product of differences in the last term nonnegative. The identity therefore gives
$`f(z)g(z)\le\theta f(x)g(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)g(y)`$, proving convexity. Both the nondecreasing and nonincreasing cases are covered.

## (b) Concave functions with opposite monotonicity

Concavity bounds both values below by their positive endpoint averages. Multiplication now gives a lower bound. Opposite monotonicity makes the product of differences nonpositive, so the displayed identity bounds that lower bound below by the average of the two endpoint products. This proves concavity of $`fg`$.

## (c) A convex-over-concave ratio

Since $`g`$ is positive and concave, $`1/g`$ is convex by composition with the decreasing convex reciprocal. Since $`g`$ is nonincreasing, $`1/g`$ is nondecreasing. Apply part (a) to the positive convex nondecreasing functions $`f`$ and $`1/g`$ to conclude that $`f/g`$ is convex. No differentiability is required in any of the three arguments.
