# Exercise 4.52: Pareto values lie on the achievable boundary

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.52](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=220). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`y`$ be a Pareto optimal value. By definition it is achieved by a feasible decision, so $`y\in O\subseteq\mathrm{cl}\,O`$. Suppose it were an interior point of $`O`$. Choose any nonzero $`k\in K`$; the proper-cone assumption ensures such a vector exists. For sufficiently small $`\epsilon>0`$, the point $`y-\epsilon k`$ lies in an open ball around $`y`$ contained in $`O`$. It is an achievable value satisfying $`y-\epsilon k\preceq_K y`$ and is distinct from $`y`$, contradicting Pareto optimality.

Thus $`y\notin\mathrm{int}\,O`$. Combining this with its membership in the closure gives $`y\in\mathrm{bd}\,O`$, and consequently $`P\subseteq O\cap\mathrm{bd}\,O`$.

## Validation

No convexity or closedness of the achievable set is assumed. Boundary membership alone is necessary, not sufficient, for Pareto optimality.
