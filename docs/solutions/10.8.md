# Exercise 10.8: Propagation of feasibility in infeasible-start Newton iterations

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.8, printed page 558. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=572).

The primal block of the Newton equations is $`A\Delta x=-(Ax-b)`$. For a step size $`t`$, the updated primal residual is therefore exactly
```math
A(x+t\Delta x)-b=(1-t)(Ax-b).
```
## (a) An initially satisfied equality
Any residual coordinate that is zero stays zero after multiplication by $`1-t`$. Induction proves the equality remains satisfied at every subsequent iterate, independent of whether the other constraints are feasible.

## (b) An equality becomes satisfied
If a previously nonzero coordinate becomes zero, the scalar multiplier $`1-t`$ must be zero, so the algorithm took a full step $`t=1`$. The displayed identity then makes every primal residual coordinate zero at once. All remain zero afterward by part (a). These are exact-arithmetic statements; numerical feasibility tolerances or rounding can make an individual coordinate appear zero without an exact full step.
