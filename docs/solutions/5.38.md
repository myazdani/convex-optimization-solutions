# Exercise 5.38: Unique no-arbitrage price in a two-state market

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.38](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=299). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

First suppose $`S\ne0`$ (the usual asset interpretation has $`S>0`$), and put $`H_u=\max(0,Su-K)`$, $`H_d=\max(0,Sd-K)`$. Since $`u>r>1>d`$, bond and stock span the two-dimensional payoff space.

The positive state prices solving their pricing equations are uniquely

```math
\rho_u=\frac{r-d}{r(u-d)},\qquad
\rho_d=\frac{u-r}{r(u-d)}.
```

Indeed $`r(\rho_u+\rho_d)=1`$, $`S(u\rho_u+d\rho_d)=S`$, and both prices are strictly positive. The option price is therefore forced to be

```math
C=\rho_uH_u+\rho_dH_d
=\frac1r\left[\frac{r-d}{u-d}H_u+\frac{u-r}{u-d}H_d\right].
```

For explicit replication, buy $`\Delta=(H_u-H_d)/(S(u-d))`$ shares and $`B=(uH_d-dH_u)/(r(u-d))`$ units of the unit-price bond. Their payoffs satisfy $`rB+Su\Delta=H_u`$, $`rB+Sd\Delta=H_d`$, and their initial cost is the displayed $`C`$. If the option is more expensive, sell it and buy the replicating portfolio; if cheaper, reverse the trade. Future liabilities cancel in both states, leaving a strictly positive initial surplus. Investing the surplus risklessly gives a zero-cost positive-payoff arbitrage.

Conversely, at this price the strictly positive state-price vector prices all three assets. Any portfolio with nonnegative payoffs and a strictly positive payoff in some state has strictly positive state-priced cost, so it cannot produce arbitrage from zero or negative cost. This proves sufficiency as well as uniqueness.

If $`S=0`$, both option payoffs equal $`H=\max(0,-K)`$. The stock has zero cost and zero payoff, while $`H/r`$ units of the bond replicate the option, forcing $`C=H/r`$ by the same arbitrage argument. This is also the displayed formula, since $`\rho_u+\rho_d=1/r`$. Although the stock and bond no longer span all two-state payoffs, the positive weights above still price all three assets and prove absence of arbitrage at that price.

The formula includes all strike regimes, also holds algebraically for $`S<0`$, and requires no subjective up-move probability.

## Validation

Pricing weights are determined by traded payoffs and positive because $`d<r<u`$. Every different price admits the displayed replication arbitrage.
