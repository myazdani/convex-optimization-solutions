# Exercise 3.14: Convex-concave functions and saddle points

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.14, p. 115](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=129).

Original solution.

## (a) Hessian blocks

On the stated convex product domain, twice differentiability gives the necessary and sufficient conditions
```math
\nabla^2_{xx}f(x,z)\succeq0,\qquad
\nabla^2_{zz}f(x,z)\preceq0
```
at every point. These follow by applying the Hessian criterion with the other variable fixed. The mixed blocks need not vanish or have a particular sign. As usual, full Hessian tests refer to open domains; on a relative affine domain use its tangent directions.

## (b) Stationarity implies saddle and strong max-min properties

At $`(\widetilde x,\widetilde z)`$ both partial gradients vanish. Convexity in $`x`$ gives
$`f(x,\widetilde z)\ge f(\widetilde x,\widetilde z)`$, and concavity in $`z`$ gives
$`f(\widetilde x,z)\le f(\widetilde x,\widetilde z)`$. This proves the saddle inequalities on the product domain.

Write $`v=f(\widetilde x,\widetilde z)`$. For every $`z`$, $`\inf_xf(x,z)\le f(\widetilde x,z)\le v`$; for $`z=\widetilde z`$, the infimum equals $`v`$. Therefore $`\sup_z\inf_xf(x,z)=v`$. Similarly, for every $`x`$, $`\sup_zf(x,z)\ge f(x,\widetilde z)\ge v`$, while at $`x=\widetilde x`$ the supremum equals $`v`$. Hence
```math
\sup_z\inf_x f(x,z)=v=\inf_x\sup_z f(x,z).
```
Both outside extrema are attained at the indicated saddle coordinates.

## (c) Saddle implies stationarity

Without needing convexity or concavity, the saddle inequalities say that $`\widetilde x`$ minimizes the differentiable function $`x\mapsto f(x,\widetilde z)`$, and $`\widetilde z`$ maximizes $`z\mapsto f(\widetilde x,z)`$. At an interior point the ordinary first-order necessary conditions give both partial gradients zero, hence $`\nabla f(\widetilde x,\widetilde z)=0`$.

The interior qualification is part of the usual unrestricted differentiability interpretation and is necessary if boundary-constrained domains are instead admitted. For example, $`f(x,z)=x`$ on $`[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R`$ has a saddle at $`(0,0)`$ but its ambient $`x`$ derivative is one; only feasible-direction optimality holds there.
