# Exercise 8.24: A classifier robust to perturbing its weights

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.24, printed page 451. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=465).

For a fixed sample $`x_i`$, minimization over weight errors gives
```math
\min_{\|u\|\le\rho}(a+u)^Tx_i=a^Tx_i-\rho\|x_i\|.
```
For a negative-class sample the analogous maximum is $`a^Ty_j+\rho\|y_j\|`$. Both bounds are attained by errors aligned with the corresponding sample, and the universal requirement permits testing each sample separately. Therefore the exact optimization problem is the SOCP
```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&\rho\\
\text{subject to}&a^Tx_i-b\ge\rho\|x_i\|_2,\quad i=1,\ldots,N,\\
&b-a^Ty_j\ge\rho\|y_j\|_2,\quad j=1,\ldots,M,\\
&\|a\|_2\le1,\quad\rho\ge0.
\end{array}
```
All sample norms are constants, so the margin inequalities are affine. A zero sample yields the correct constraint on $`b`$ alone; one should not divide its gap by a zero norm. For nonzero samples, the margin of a fixed separator is the minimum signed gap divided by that sample's norm. When the optimum is positive, scaling shows that a nonzero normal can be chosen with norm one. The error ball quantifies changes only in $`a`$; allowing errors in $`b`$ would produce a different robustness problem.
