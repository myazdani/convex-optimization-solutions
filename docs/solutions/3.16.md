# Exercise 3.16: Convexity and quasiconvexity classifications

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.16, p. 116](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=130).

Original solution.

All classifications use precisely the domains stated in the exercise.

## (a) Exponential minus one

$`f''(x)=e^x>0`$, so the function is convex and not concave. It is strictly increasing on the line, so both its sublevel and superlevel sets are intervals: it is both quasiconvex and quasiconcave.

## (b) Product

The Hessian $`\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}`$ is indefinite, so $`x_1x_2`$ is neither convex nor concave. It is quasiconcave: a positive superlevel condition is $`x_2\ge\alpha/x_1`$, the epigraph of a convex function, and nonpositive levels give the whole domain. It is not quasiconvex: $`(1,4)`$ and $`(4,1)`$ have value four while their midpoint has value $`25/4>4`$.

## (c) Reciprocal product

The Hessian is
```math
\begin{bmatrix}
2/(x_1^3x_2)&1/(x_1^2x_2^2)\\
1/(x_1^2x_2^2)&2/(x_1x_2^3)
\end{bmatrix}.
```
Its first diagonal entry is positive and determinant is $`3/(x_1^4x_2^4)>0`$, proving strict convexity and hence quasiconvexity. It is not concave. It is not quasiconcave either: the two points in (b) have reciprocal value $`1/4`$, while their midpoint has value $`4/25<1/4`$.

## (d) Ratio

The positive-domain ratio is both quasiconvex and quasiconcave: for every level $`\alpha`$, multiplication by $`x_2>0`$ turns each level inequality into an affine halfspace, intersected with the domain. It is neither convex nor concave; its Hessian has determinant $`-1/x_2^4<0`$.

## (e) Quadratic-over-linear

It is convex as the perspective of the square. Explicitly, for a direction $`(u,v)`$ its Hessian quadratic form is
```math
\frac{2}{x_2}\left(u-\frac{x_1}{x_2}v\right)^2\ge0.
```
It is therefore quasiconvex and is not concave, as seen on $`x_2=1`$. It is not quasiconcave: $`(1,1)`$ and $`(-1,1)`$ have value one, while their midpoint has value zero.

## (f) Weighted geometric mean

For $`0<\alpha<1`$, with $`f=x_1^\alpha x_2^{1-\alpha}`$, the Hessian satisfies
```math
\begin{bmatrix}u&v\end{bmatrix}\nabla^2 f(x)\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\end{bmatrix}
=-\alpha(1-\alpha)f
\left(\frac{u}{x_1}-\frac{v}{x_2}\right)^2\le0.
```
Thus it is concave and quasiconcave. It is not convex, because its restriction to $`x_2=1`$ is strictly concave. It is not quasiconvex: the points $`(2^{1/\alpha},1)`$ and $`(1,2^{1/(1-\alpha)})`$ both have value two, but their midpoint has strictly greater value. Strictness follows from the displayed curvature along their segment: the endpoints are not proportional, so its tangent direction never has equal coordinate-to-position ratios.

For $`\alpha=0`$ or $`1`$, the function is a coordinate projection and is affine; all four properties hold.
