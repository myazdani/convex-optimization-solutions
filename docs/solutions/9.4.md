# Exercise 9.4: Why the perspective formulation is not a routine Newton problem

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.4, printed page 514. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=528).

For a scalar residual $`r`$ and $`y>0`$,
```math
\frac{r^2}{y}+y\ge2|r|,
```
with equality exactly at $`y=|r|`$ when $`r\ne0`$. If $`r=0`$, the infimum zero is approached only as $`y\downarrow0`$. Consequently the reformulation has the same infimum as twice the one-norm fitting objective, but it need not attain that infimum. Its smooth open-domain expression therefore does not guarantee a usable Newton minimization problem.

There is a second, systematic obstruction. In a direction $`(h,k)`$ its Hessian quadratic form is
```math
D^2f(x,y)[(h,k),(h,k)]
=\sum_i\frac2{y_i}\left(a_i^Th-\frac{r_i}{y_i}k_i\right)^2,
\qquad r_i=a_i^Tx-b_i.
```
It is a sum of only $`m`$ rank-one forms on $`n+m`$ variables. For $`n>0`$ the Hessian is always singular, so ordinary Newton steps defined by its inverse are unavailable. As a minimal example, minimizing $`x^2/y+y`$ with $`y>0`$ has infimum zero and no optimizer, while the original $`\min_x|x|`$ has optimizer zero. Its Hessian has determinant zero everywhere.

Thus the infimum identity is correct, but the proposed conclusion about directly applying the chapter's Newton method is not. A solver would need a different formulation or additional regularization/algorithmic analysis; differentiability and convexity alone are insufficient.
