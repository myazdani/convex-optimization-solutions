# Exercise 3.10: Variance alone versus scaled zero-mean noise

Original solution.

## (a) Counterexample to ordering by variance

Take $`x_0=0`$ and the convex function $`f(x)=|x|`$. Let $`v`$ equal $`10`$ and $`-10`$ with probability $`1/100`$ each, and zero otherwise. Let $`w`$ equal $`1`$ and $`-1`$ with probability $`1/2`$ each. Both means vanish, but
```math
\mathrm{var}(v)=2>1=\mathrm{var}(w),
\qquad
\mathbb E f(v)=1/5<1=\mathbb E f(w).
```
Thus variance does not order all convex expected losses.

## (b) Scaling a fixed zero-mean noise

Define $`h(t)=\mathbb E f(x_0+tv)`$ for nonnegative $`t`$ where these expectations are defined and the random points lie in the domain. Expectation preserves the pointwise convexity in $`t`$, so $`h`$ is convex. Jensen's inequality gives $`h(t)\ge f(x_0)=h(0)`$ because $`\mathbb Ev=0`$. If $`0\le s<t`$, convexity therefore yields
```math
h(s)\le(1-s/t)h(0)+(s/t)h(t)\le h(t).
```
Hence the expectation is nondecreasing in the noise scale. The conclusion is non-strict: an affine $`f`$ or identically zero noise can give a constant expectation. The same argument permits $`+\infty`$ values when extended expectations are well defined; finite expectations suffice for the ordinary statement.
