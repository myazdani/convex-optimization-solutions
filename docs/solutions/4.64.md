# Exercise 4.64: A convex formulation for two-stage recourse

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.64](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=225). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Use one shared first-stage variable $`x`$ and one second-stage vector $`z_i`$ for each scenario. The deterministic equivalent is

```math
\mathrm{minimize}_{x,z_1,\ldots,z_S}\quad
\sum_{i=1}^S\pi_i f(x,z_i,i).
```

For any two policies, joint convexity of each scenario cost bounds its value at their componentwise convex combination by the corresponding convex combination of costs. Multiplying by nonnegative probabilities and summing proves convexity of the full objective. Thus this is an ordinary convex optimization problem in $`n+Sq`$ variables.

Any feasible tuple specifies an admissible policy: choose its single $`x`$ before the scenario is known, then choose its $`z_i`$ when scenario $`i`$ occurs. Conversely any policy of the prescribed form supplies exactly such a tuple, and its expected cost is exactly the displayed objective. Hence values and optimizers correspond without relaxation. Separate first-stage variables for each scenario would improperly allow advance knowledge of the scenario and generally yield a lower, invalid cost.

Optionally define $`g_i(x)=\inf_z f(x,z,i)`$. Partial minimization makes each proper $`g_i`$ convex, and the infimum value equals that of $`\inf_x\sum_i\pi_i g_i(x)`$. The finite lifted formulation is preferable for recovering the policy: an inner infimum might be unattained, in which case this reduced expression alone does not produce an actual $`z_i`$. Zero-probability scenarios may be omitted from the objective and assigned arbitrary second-stage values in this unconstrained setting.

## Validation

The common first-stage choice enforces nonanticipation exactly. Joint convexity is needed, and no existence of an optimal policy is asserted without an attainment condition.
