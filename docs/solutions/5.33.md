# Exercise 5.33: l1 sensitivity from an exact certificate

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, printed p. 283
([official PDF, p. 297](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=297)).
The analytic task is solved below using the supplied data; LP computations
are optional independent illustrations. Row numbers in this note are
one-based.

## Optimality and uniqueness at zero

At \(x_0=\mathbf1\), the residual is
\(r_0=(2,0,0,-1,0,1)\). A dual certificate is

\[
s=(1,-1/2,1/2,-1,0,1),\qquad A^Ts=0,\quad\|s\|_\infty\leq1.
\]

For every \(x\), the inequality \(\|Ax+b\|_1\geq s^T(Ax+b)=s^Tb=4\)
holds. The candidate attains 4 because the saturated certificate coordinates
have the signs of its nonzero residuals. Thus it is optimal.

For a uniqueness argument, take \(I=\{2,3,5\}\), the zero-residual rows.
The certificate is strictly inside (-1,1) on those coordinates. Equality in
the dual bound therefore forces \((Ax+b)_I=0\) at every optimum. The
square matrix \(A_I\) has determinant -344, so these three equations have
exactly one solution. The candidate is the unique optimum.

## Continue the zero residuals

Preserving the same three zero residuals gives

\[
x(\epsilon)=-A_I^{-1}(b_I+\epsilon d_I)
 =(1-3\epsilon,\,1+2\epsilon,\,1).
\]

Substitution, without fitting any numerical curve, gives

\[
r(\epsilon)=(2+10\epsilon,\,0,\,0,\,-1+\epsilon,\,0,\,1-2\epsilon).
\]

The same certificate remains valid whenever its saturated coordinates agree
with those residual signs. The three requirements are

\[
\epsilon\geq-1/5,\qquad\epsilon\leq1,\qquad\epsilon\leq1/2.
\]

Their intersection is the closed interval \([-1/5,1/2]\), and throughout it

\[
p^\star(\epsilon)=s^T(b+\epsilon d)=4+7\epsilon.
\]

The solution remains unique, including both endpoints: the strict certificate
coordinates on \(I\) still force the same nonsingular three-equation system.
This is the maximal interval for this affine value expression. Outside it,
the unique point that could attain the fixed dual certificate bound has a
residual with the wrong sign, so no point attains that bound.

## Numerical verification

The primal LP introduces nonnegative epigraph variables \(t\) with
\(-t\leq Ax+b+\epsilon d\leq t\); its three \(x\) coordinates are free.
A second LP independently maximizes \((b+\epsilon d)^Ts\) subject to
\(A^Ts=0\), \(-1\leq s\leq1\). Both are solved with SciPy/HiGHS at zero,
several interior points, and both endpoints. The module reports primal/dual
gaps, feasibility residuals, analytic-versus-LP objective and point errors.

Run `convex-solutions run 5.33 --output artifacts/5.33` to generate the
results and `5_33_sensitivity.png`. Tests verify the exact certificate and
matrix identities as well as solver agreement; they reject use of the local
formula outside its proven interval.
