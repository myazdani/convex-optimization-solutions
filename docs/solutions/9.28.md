# Exercise 9.28: Sparse-plus-low-rank Newton steps for quadratic barriers

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.28, printed page 519. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=533).

At a strictly feasible point compute $`\alpha_i=(-x^TA_ix-b_i^Tx-c_i)^{-1}>0`$, $`q_i=2A_ix+b_i`$, and define
```math
S=2\sum_i\alpha_iA_i\succ0,\qquad
U=[\alpha_1q_1\ \cdots\ \alpha_mq_m],\qquad
g=\sum_i\alpha_iq_i.
```
The Hessian is exactly $`H=S+UU^T`$. It is a sparse matrix (the union pattern of the $`A_i`$) plus rank at most $`m`$, rather than an inherently dense matrix to be formed.

Factor $`S`$ once with sparse Cholesky. Solve $`Sy=g`$ and $`SW=U`$, the latter using $`m`$ right-hand sides. Form and factor $`K=I_m+U^TW\succ0`$. The Newton direction is
```math
\Delta x=-y+W K^{-1}U^Ty.
```
Multiplication by $`S+UU^T`$ verifies that its right-hand side is $`-g`$, or equivalently it is Woodbury applied without explicit inverses.

The cost is sparse matrix-vector products and assembly in $`O(\sum_i\mathrm{nnz}(A_i))`$, one sparse factorization of $`S`$, $`m+1`$ sparse triangular-solve pairs, $`O(nm^2)`$ to form the small Gram matrix, and $`O(m^3)`$ to factor it. Memory includes $`O(nm)`$ for $`W`$. This is advantageous when $`m\ll n`$ and the union sparsity pattern has manageable fill; sparsity of each input alone does not guarantee that their union or its Cholesky factor stays sparse. The method remains algebraically exact in that case, but the claimed saving must be assessed from the actual pattern.
