# Exercise 7.4: Joint Gaussian mean and covariance estimation

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.4, printed page 393. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=407).

## (a) Unconstrained estimates
Expand $`y_k-a=(y_k-\mu)+(\mu-a)`$ and use $`\sum_k(y_k-\mu)=0`$. This gives
```math
\sum_k(y_k-a)(y_k-a)^T=N Y+N(a-\mu)(a-\mu)^T,
```
and, by taking its product trace with $`R^{-1}`$, exactly
```math
l(R,a)=\frac N2\left[-n\log(2\pi)-\log\det R
-\mathrm{tr}(R^{-1}Y)-(a-\mu)^TR^{-1}(a-\mu)\right].
```
For each $`R\succ0`$, the last quadratic is uniquely minimized by $`a=\mu`$. If $`Y\succ0`$, put $`C=Y^{1/2}R^{-1}Y^{1/2}\succ0`$. Apart from constants, maximizing the remaining likelihood amounts to maximizing
```math
\log\det C-\mathrm{tr}C=\sum_i(\log\lambda_i(C)-\lambda_i(C)).
```
Each summand is at most $`-1`$, with equality only at $`\lambda_i=1`$. Hence uniquely $`C=I`$, $`R=Y`$, and $`a=\mu`$.

## (b) Concavity on the specified region
Let $`g(R)=-\log\det R-\mathrm{tr}(R^{-1}Y)`$. Along a symmetric direction $`H`$, set $`B=R^{-1/2}HR^{-1/2}`$ and $`C=R^{-1/2}YR^{-1/2}`$. Differentiating the inverse twice gives
```math
D^2g(R)[H,H]=\mathrm{tr}(B^2)-2\mathrm{tr}(CB^2).
```
On $`0\prec R\preceq2Y`$ we have $`C\succeq I/2`$. Since $`B^2\succeq0`$, this second derivative is nonpositive; therefore $`g`$ is concave on that convex region.

The remaining term $`-(a-\mu)^TR^{-1}(a-\mu)`$ is jointly concave. Indeed its negative is jointly convex, as witnessed by the identity
```math
v^TR^{-1}v=\sup_z\{2z^Tv-z^TRz\},
```
a supremum of affine functions of $`(R,v)`$, with maximizer $`z=R^{-1}v`$. Combining the two results proves joint concavity of $`l`$. If $`Y`$ is singular the region contains no positive definite $`R`$; this does not create an additional nonempty concavity claim.
