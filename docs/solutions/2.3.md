# Exercise 2.3: Closed midpoint-convex sets

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.3, p. 60](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=74).

Original solution.

Fix $`a,b\in C`$. Repeated midpoint operations show by induction that
```math
(1-j/2^m)a+(j/2^m)b\in C,
\qquad m\ge0,\quad j=0,\ldots,2^m.
```
Indeed the even-index points at level $`m+1`$ are the level-$`m`$ points, and every odd-index point is the midpoint of its two neighboring level-$`m`$ points. For an arbitrary $`t\in[0,1]`$, choose dyadic numbers $`t_m\to t`$. The displayed points converge to $`(1-t)a+tb`$, which lies in $`C`$ because $`C`$ is closed. Thus $`C`$ is convex. Closure is substantive: $`\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb R`$ is midpoint convex but not convex.
