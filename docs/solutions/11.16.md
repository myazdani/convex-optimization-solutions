# Exercise 11.16: Differential identities for logarithmic homogeneity

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.16, printed page 628. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=642).

Write $`g=\nabla\psi(y)`$ and $`H=\nabla^2\psi(y)\prec0`$.

## (a) Gradient scaling
Differentiate $`\psi(sy)=\psi(y)+\theta\log s`$ with respect to $`y`$, holding $`s`$ fixed. The chain rule gives $`s\nabla\psi(sy)=\nabla\psi(y)`$, which is the required identity.

## (b) Gradient-Hessian relation
Differentiate the identity in (a) with respect to $`s`$ at $`s=1`$. It gives $`Hy=-g`$, or $`\nabla\psi(y)=-\nabla^2\psi(y)y`$.

## (c) Radial curvature
Differentiating the original log-homogeneity relation with respect to $`s`$ at one gives $`y^Tg=\theta`$. Combining it with (b) yields $`y^THy=-\theta`$. Here the middle symbol is the Hessian $`\nabla^2\psi`$.

## (d) Inverse-Hessian pairing
Negative definiteness makes $`H`$ invertible. Substitute $`g=-Hy`$ to obtain
```math
g^TH^{-1}g=(-Hy)^TH^{-1}(-Hy)=y^THy=-\theta.
```
Every identity holds at all interior points and for every positive scaling, with no normalization of $`y`$ required.
