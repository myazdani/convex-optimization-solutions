# Exercise 5.11: A dual for regularized sum-of-norms fitting

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.11](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=290). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Introduce $`y_i=A_ix+b_i`$ and multipliers $`\nu_i`$ for $`A_ix+b_i-y_i=0`$. Write $`w=\sum_i A_i^T\nu_i`$. The Lagrangian is

```math
\sum_i(\|y_i\|_2-\nu_i^Ty_i)
+\tfrac12\|x-x_0\|_2^2+w^Tx+\sum_i b_i^T\nu_i.
```

The infimum over $`y_i`$ is zero if $`\|\nu_i\|_2\le1`$, by Cauchy–Schwarz, and $`-\infty`$ otherwise, by sending $`y_i`$ to infinity in the direction of $`\nu_i`$. Completing the square in $`x`$ gives minimizer $`x=x_0-w`$ and value $`x_0^Tw-\tfrac12\|w\|_2^2`$. Thus the dual is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad&\sum_i\nu_i^T(A_ix_0+b_i)
-\tfrac12\left\|\sum_iA_i^T\nu_i\right\|_2^2\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad&\|\nu_i\|_2\le1\quad(i=1,\ldots,N).
\end{aligned}
```

Its objective is concave quadratic and its feasible set is a compact product of Euclidean balls. The primal is continuous, coercive and strongly convex because of the quadratic term, so it has a unique minimizer. All norm functions are finite everywhere and the only lifted constraints are affine, so affine-equality regularity gives strong duality and a dual optimum exists. From any dual optimizer recover the primal optimizer as $`x^\star=x_0-\sum_iA_i^T\nu_i^\star`$.

The remaining KKT condition is $`\nu_i^\star\in\partial\|A_ix^\star+b_i\|_2`$: it equals the unit residual direction when that residual is nonzero, and may be any vector in the unit ball when the residual is zero. These conditions also directly verify the recovery formula and equality of primal and dual objectives.

## Validation

The equality orientation fixes the signs of both the data term and the primal recovery. Zero residuals are handled by the norm subdifferential.
