# Exercise 2.23: Disjoint closed convex sets without strict separation

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.23, p. 63](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=77).

Original solution.

In $`\mathbb R^2`$, take
```math
C=\{(u,v):v\ge e^u\},\qquad D=\{(u,0):u\in\mathbb R\}.
```
The epigraph $`C`$ is closed and convex because the exponential is continuous and convex; $`D`$ is a closed affine line. They are disjoint since $`e^u>0`$.

Suppose a hyperplane strictly separated them. Orient its affine functional so that $`a_1u+a_2v>b`$ on $`C`$ and $`a_1u<b`$ on $`D`$. Boundedness of $`a_1u`$ for all $`u\in\mathbb R`$ forces $`a_1=0`$. The strict inequality on $`D`$ then gives $`b>0`$. Since $`C`$ contains vertical rays, the other inequality forces $`a_2>0`$. But $`a_2e^u\to0`$ as $`u\to-\infty`$, contradicting $`a_2e^u>b>0`$. Thus strict separation is impossible, though $`v=0`$ weakly separates the sets.
