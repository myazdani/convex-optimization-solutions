# Exercise 5.10: Duals for D- and A-optimal experiment design

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.10](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=290). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`M(x)=\sum_i x_iv_iv_i^T`$. Introduce $`X=M(x)`$, keep $`x\succeq0`$ in the minimization domain, and use a symmetric multiplier $`Z`$ for $`X-M(x)=0`$ and $`\nu`$ for $`\mathbf1^Tx=1`$. For either criterion $`f(X)`$,

```math
L=f(X)+\mathrm{tr}(ZX)+\sum_i x_i(\nu-v_i^TZv_i)-\nu.
```

The infimum over nonnegative $`x`$ is finite exactly when $`v_i^TZv_i\le\nu`$ for every $`i`$.

## (a) D-optimality

For $`f(X)=-\log\det X`$, the matrix infimum is finite exactly when $`Z\succ0`$. It is attained at $`X=Z^{-1}`$ and equals $`\log\det Z+n`$. If $`Z`$ has a negative eigenvalue, a large positive eigenvalue of $`X`$ in that direction gives value $`-\infty`$; a zero eigenvalue also gives $`-\infty`$ through the negative logarithm. Thus a dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad\log\det Z+n-\nu\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad Z\succ0,\quad v_i^TZv_i\le\nu\quad\text{for all }i.
```

If the vectors span, $`\nu>0`$ for every feasible $`Z`$. Write $`Z=\nu W`$, with $`v_i^TWv_i\le1`$. Maximizing the scalar term $`n\log\nu-\nu+n`$ gives $`\nu=n`$. Hence the simpler equivalent dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad\log\det W+n\log n\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad W\succ0,\quad v_i^TWv_i\le1.
```

The recovery is $`Z=nW,\nu=n`$.

## (b) A-optimality

For $`f(X)=\mathrm{tr}(X^{-1})`$, the infimum is $`-\infty`$ unless $`Z\succeq0`$. For positive semidefinite $`Z`$,

```math
\mathrm{tr}(X^{-1})+\mathrm{tr}(ZX)-2\mathrm{tr}(Z^{1/2})
=\|X^{-1/2}-X^{1/2}Z^{1/2}\|_F^2\ge0.
```

If $`Z\succ0`$, equality holds at $`X=Z^{-1/2}`$. If $`Z`$ is singular, take those eigenvalues of $`X`$ to infinity on its kernel and use the inverse square root on its positive eigenspace; the same infimum is approached. Therefore the dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad2\mathrm{tr}(Z^{1/2})-\nu\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad Z\succeq0,\quad v_i^TZv_i\le\nu.
```

Eliminating $`\nu`$ gives the concave objective $`2\mathrm{tr}(Z^{1/2})-\max_i v_i^TZv_i`$ over the PSD cone. A useful normalized conic version is to maximize $`\mathrm{tr}(Y)`$ subject to

```math
Y\succeq0,\qquad\|Yv_i\|_2\le1\quad\text{for all }i.
```

Its optimal value squared equals the A-dual optimum: writing $`Z=t^2Y^2,\nu=t^2`$, the objective is $`2t\mathrm{tr}(Y)-t^2`$, maximized at $`t=\mathrm{tr}(Y)`$ with value $`(\mathrm{tr}Y)^2`$. Conversely every nonzero feasible dual pair with $`\nu>0`$ gives $`Y=Z^{1/2}/\sqrt\nu`$, so the normalization loses no bound. The zero pair gives value zero.

## Feasibility, strong duality, and attainment

The primal domain is nonempty exactly when the $`v_i`$ span $`\mathbb R^n`$. In that case uniform positive weights give $`X\succ0`$ and satisfy relative-interior Slater for both lifted problems, so strong duality holds. The weight simplex is compact and either criterion diverges as an information eigenvalue tends to zero, yielding a primal optimizer; standard regularity yields an attained dual optimum. If the vectors do not span, both primals are infeasible. The displayed duals become unbounded by enlarging their matrix in an orthogonal null direction, consistently with value $`+\infty`$.

## Validation

The matrix minimizations include singular dual matrices where permitted, and the normalized A-design objective is explicitly squared when converting back to the actual dual value.
