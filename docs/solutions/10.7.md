# Exercise 10.7: Closedness and Lipschitz properties of the KKT residual

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.7, printed page 558. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=572).

The residual is $`r(x,\nu)=(\nabla f(x)+A^T\nu,Ax-b)`$, with derivative $`Dr=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla^2f(x)&A^T\\A&0\end{bmatrix}`$.

## (a) Closed residual norm
Extend the residual norm by $`+\infty`$ outside the differentiable open domain. Consider a convergent sequence $`(x_j,\nu_j)\to(\bar x,\bar\nu)`$ with bounded residual norms. Then $`\nabla f(x_j)`$ is bounded because $`\nu_j`$ is bounded. Choose any fixed $`z\in\mathrm{dom}f`$. Convexity gives
```math
f(x_j)\le f(z)+\nabla f(x_j)^T(x_j-z),
```
so these function values are bounded above. If $`\bar x`$ were outside the domain, closedness of $`f`$ would require $`\liminf f(x_j)=+\infty`$, a contradiction. Thus $`\bar x`$ is in the domain, and continuity of its gradient makes the residual norm continuous at the limit. Every residual sublevel set is consequently closed, proving the assertion.

## (b) Lipschitz equivalence
The constant blocks cancel in a difference, giving the exact identity
```math
\|Dr(x,\nu)-Dr(\hat x,\hat\nu)\|_2
=\|\nabla^2f(x)-\nabla^2f(\hat x)\|_2.
```
A Hessian Lipschitz bound $`L\|x-\hat x\|`$ therefore implies the same derivative Lipschitz constant in the product Euclidean norm. Conversely, compare points with the same $`\nu`$ to recover precisely the Hessian Lipschitz bound. This proves the stated equivalence on the domain (or on a product region of interest). On an arbitrary nonproduct restricted residual set, the forward implication still holds directly; a reverse same-constant claim requires that the same-multiplier comparisons used in it are available. The block identity is the underlying property in every case.
