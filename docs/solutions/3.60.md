# Exercise 3.60: Cone sublevels and cone epigraphs

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.60, p. 125](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=139).

Original solution.

## (a) Sublevel convexity

Let $`x,y\in C_\alpha`$ and $`z=\theta x+(1-\theta)y`$, $`0\le\theta\le1`$. Cone-convexity gives
```math
f(z)\preceq_K\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)
\preceq_K\theta\alpha+(1-\theta)\alpha=\alpha.
```
The second inequality follows by adding and nonnegatively scaling cone inequalities. The domain is convex by the definition of cone-convexity, so $`z`$ is admissible and belongs to $`C_\alpha`$.

## (b) Epigraph equivalence

If $`f`$ is $`K`$-convex and $`(x,s),(y,t)\in\mathrm{epi}_K f`$, then
```math
f(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)
\preceq_K\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)
\preceq_K\theta s+(1-\theta)t.
```
Thus the corresponding convex combination lies in the cone epigraph, proving its convexity.

Conversely suppose that epigraph is convex. Its projection onto the first coordinates is the function domain, since $`(x,f(x))`$ always belongs; hence the domain is convex. Apply epigraph convexity to $`(x,f(x))`$ and $`(y,f(y))`$. Membership of their convex combination means precisely
```math
f(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)
\preceq_K\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y).
```
This is $`K`$-convexity, completing the equivalence.
