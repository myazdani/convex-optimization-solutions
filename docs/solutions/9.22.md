# Exercise 9.22: Newton complexity under a scaled third-derivative condition

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.22, printed page 517. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=531).

Let the constant in (9.42) be $`k>0`$, interpreted on every affine line in the multivariable case. Put $`c=k^2/4`$ and $`F=cf`$. Then
```math
|D^3F[v,v,v]|\le2(D^2F[v,v])^{3/2},
```
so $`F`$ is self-concordant. Positive scaling does not change the Newton step, since $`(c\nabla^2f)^{-1}(c\nabla f)=(\nabla^2f)^{-1}\nabla f`$, or the accepted Armijo step sizes, since the entire Armijo inequality scales by $`c`$.

To make $`f(x)-p^\star\le\epsilon`$, it suffices to solve the scaled problem to tolerance $`c\epsilon`$. Under the same closed-sublevel/attainment assumptions as the chapter's complexity theorem, (9.56) gives
```math
N\ \le\
\frac{20-8\alpha}{\alpha\beta(1-2\alpha)^2}
\frac{k^2}{4}\bigl(f(x^{(0)})-p^\star\bigr)
+\log_2\log_2\left(\frac4{k^2\epsilon}\right),
```
with the theorem's integer rounding and small-tolerance convention. For a tolerance outside that regime one may run the bound at any smaller admissible tolerance, for example replacing $`c\epsilon`$ by $`\min(c\epsilon,1/4)`$. The Newton decrement scales as $`\lambda_F=\sqrt c\,\lambda_f`$, which is the corresponding stopping-threshold adjustment. If the third derivatives vanish identically, the objective is quadratic on its connected domain; a feasible full Newton step solves it exactly when its Hessian is positive definite.
