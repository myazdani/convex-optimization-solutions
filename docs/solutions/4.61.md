# Exercise 4.61: Convex optimization of logistic purchase probabilities

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.61](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=224). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write $`z=a^Tx+b`$ and $`p=1/(1+e^{-z})`$.

## (a) Purchase probability

The logistic function is strictly increasing, since its derivative is $`p(1-p)>0`$ for every finite $`z`$. Therefore maximize $`a^Tx+b`$ subject to $`Fx\preceq g`$, an LP. Its optimizers are exactly those maximizing purchase probability. If the LP is unbounded above, purchase probability has supremum one but no finite maximizer; a diverging affine-score sequence provides probabilities tending to one. If the feasible set is empty, both formulations are infeasible.

## (b) Expected profit

The assumed profit $`h(x)=c^Tx+d`$ is positive on the feasible set, and logistic probability is positive. Taking the increasing logarithm preserves the objective order:

```math
\log(p(x)h(x))
=\log(c^Tx+d)-\log(1+e^{-(a^Tx+b)}).
```

The first term is concave; the second is the negative of log-sum-exp of two affine functions, hence concave. Thus the convex formulation is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad
\log(c^Tx+d)-\log(1+e^{-a^Tx-b})
\qquad\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad Fx\preceq g.
```

Equivalently minimize its negative. The original optimal expected profit is the exponential of the logarithmic optimum, with the corresponding extended-value interpretation if unbounded. Every finite optimizer transfers without changing $`x`$. Positivity of profit is essential to this logarithmic transformation.

## Validation

The first part uses monotonicity rather than falsely asserting global concavity of the logistic function. The second uses concavity of log probability, not of the untransformed expected-profit product.
