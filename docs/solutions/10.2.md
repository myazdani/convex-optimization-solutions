# Exercise 10.2: Projected gradient descent and the reduced problem

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.2, printed page 557. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=571).

Write $`g=\nabla f(x)`$ and $`\Pi=I-A^T(AA^T)^{-1}A`$, the orthogonal projector onto $`\ker A`$.

## (a) Projection and multiplier least squares
The displayed KKT equations give $`v=-g-A^Tw`$ and $`Av=0`$, hence
```math
w=-(AA^T)^{-1}Ag,\qquad v=-\Pi g.
```
These are exactly the normal equations for minimizing $`\|g+A^Ty\|^2`$ over $`y`$, uniquely because $`A^T`$ is injective. The vector $`v`$ lies in $`\ker A`$, and $`-g-v=A^Tw`$ is perpendicular to that subspace, so $`v`$ is its unique Euclidean projection of $`-g`$.

## (b) Orthonormal reduced coordinates
Write feasible points as $`x=\hat x+Fz`$ with $`F^TF=I`$ and range $`\ker A`$. Then $`\Pi=FF^T`$ and
```math
-\nabla\widetilde f(z)=-F^Tg,\qquad
\Delta x_{\rm pg}=F(-\nabla\widetilde f(z)).
```
The reduced gradient step is mapped isometrically into the projected step. Values along their search lines and their directional derivatives agree, so the same backtracking test selects the same step size.

## (c) Sufficient convergence assumptions
For example assume the feasible initial sublevel set is closed, and on it the reduced Hessian satisfies $`mI\preceq F^T\nabla^2f(x)F\preceq MI`$ for constants $`0<m\le M<\infty`$. The reduced objective is then strongly convex with Lipschitz gradient; the closed sublevel set is bounded by strong convexity and contains its unique minimizer. Gradient descent with $`0<\alpha<1/2,0<\beta<1`$ has a step bounded below by $`\min(1,2\beta(1-\alpha)/M)`$. Armijo decrease and $`\|\nabla\widetilde f\|^2\ge2m(\widetilde f-p^\star)`$ give a geometric decrease in the value gap. Strong convexity then gives convergence of $`z`$, hence $`x`$, to the unique feasible minimizer. These assumptions concern curvature only along feasible directions; full-space strong convexity is unnecessary.
