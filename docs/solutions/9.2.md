# Exercise 9.2: A closed quadratic-over-linear minimization

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.2, printed page 514. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=528).

Put $`Q=A^TA\succ0`$, $`x_1=Q^{-1}A^Tb`$, and $`e=\|Ax_1-b\|^2>0`$. Least-squares orthogonality gives
```math
\|Ax-b\|^2=e+(x-x_1)^TQ(x-x_1).
```
Assume the denominator-positive domain is nonempty, as needed for a minimizer.

## (a) Closedness
The extended-value objective is continuous inside its domain. At any finite boundary point with $`c^Tx+d=0`$, its numerator stays at least $`e>0`$, so approaching from the domain makes the value tend to $`+\infty`$. Points with negative denominator have a neighborhood disjoint from the domain. Thus the extended-value function is lower semicontinuous everywhere, or equivalently its epigraph is closed.

## (b) Explicit minimizer
Set $`x_2=Q^{-1}c`$, $`s_1=c^Tx_1+d`$, and $`\kappa=c^TQ^{-1}c`$. With $`s=c^Tx+d>0`$ and numerator $`N`$, the stationarity equation is
```math
2sQ(x-x_1)-Nc=0.
```
Thus $`x=x_1+t x_2`$, where $`t=N/(2s)>0`$. Substitution gives
```math
\kappa t^2+2s_1t-e=0.
```
If $`c\ne0`$, then $`\kappa>0`$, and only the root
```math
t=\frac{-s_1+\sqrt{s_1^2+\kappa e}}{\kappa}
```
has positive denominator $`s=s_1+\kappa t=\sqrt{s_1^2+\kappa e}`$. It gives the unique stationary point and minimizer. The objective is convex by the quadratic-over-positive-linear composition rule; alternatively completing the numerator relative to this stationary point proves the supporting inequality. Its growth at infinity is at least linear, since its numerator grows quadratically and its positive denominator at most linearly, so a minimizer exists.

If $`c=0`$, nonempty domain requires $`d>0`$, and the unique minimizer is simply $`x_1`$; one can take $`t=e/(2d)`$ in the degenerate linear equation, while $`x_2=0`$. If $`c=0,d\le0`$, the domain is empty and there is no minimizer; closedness still holds for the identically infinite extension.
