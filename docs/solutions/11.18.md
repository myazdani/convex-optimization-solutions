# Exercise 11.18: A degree-one logarithm for the second-order cone

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.18, printed page 628. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=642).

Let $`y=(u,s)`$. There is a domain typo in the printed exercise, verified in the PDF: it states $`s>\sum_i u_i^2`$, whereas the SOC interior is $`s>\|u\|_2`$. Literally on the printed domain the answer is no: for instance $`u=1/2,s=3/10`$ satisfies $`s>u^2`$ but makes $`s-u^2/s<0`$, so its logarithm is not even real. That domain is also not a cone.

On the intended SOC interior, the formula does define a generalized logarithm, of degree one. Put
```math
h(u,s)=s-\frac{\|u\|^2}{s}>0,
\qquad\psi(u,s)=\log h(u,s).
```
The function $`h`$ is concave on $`s>0`$, being linear minus quadratic-over-linear, and positive on the SOC interior; the increasing concave logarithm therefore makes $`\psi`$ concave. For a direction $`(v,a)`$, direct differentiation gives
```math
D^2h[(v,a),(v,a)]= -\frac2s\left\|v-\frac asu\right\|^2,
\qquad
D^2\psi=\frac{D^2h}{h}-\frac{(Dh)^2}{h^2}.
```
Both terms are nonpositive. If the first is zero, $`v=(a/s)u`$, and the direction is radial; homogeneity of $`h`$ then gives $`Dh=(a/s)h`$. The second term vanishes only if $`a=0`$, which also gives $`v=0`$. Thus the Hessian is negative definite.

Moreover $`h(ru,rs)=r h(u,s)`$, so $`\psi(ry)=\psi(y)+\log r`$. The function is twice continuously differentiable on the interior. At a nonzero cone boundary point its positive log argument tends to zero; at the apex $`0<h\le s\to0`$ gives the same behavior. Thus $`\psi\to-\infty`$ at every finite boundary point, so its hypograph is closed under the extended concave-function convention. All generalized-logarithm conditions hold on the corrected domain. Generalized logarithms are not required to have self-concordant negatives; degree one here therefore does not contradict the usual degree-two SOC self-concordant barrier.
