# Exercise 6.14: Positive-real interpolation and the Pick cone

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.14, printed page 348. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=362).

Write $`w_k=z_k^{-1}`$, so the $`w_k`$ are distinct nonzero points of the open unit disk. All vector spaces below are real vector spaces with inner product $`\Re(x^Hy)`$, even though their coordinates are complex. Use the representation stated in the exercise in the form
```math
y=i\beta\mathbf1+\int_0^{2\pi}K(\theta)\,d\mu(\theta),
\qquad K_k(\theta)=\frac{1+w_ke^{-i\theta}}{1-w_ke^{-i\theta}},
```
where $`\beta\in\mathbb R`$ and $`\mu`$ is a finite positive measure on the circle. This is the Stieltjes-measure version of its nondecreasing $`\rho`$.

## (a) Closed convex cone
Nonnegative linear combinations of such representations remain of the same form, proving the cone and convexity properties. For closedness, suppose represented vectors $`y^{(j)}`$ converge. The real part of the first kernel satisfies
```math
\Re K_1(\theta)=\frac{1-|w_1|^2}{|1-w_1e^{-i\theta}|^2}
\ge\frac{1-|w_1|}{1+|w_1|}>0.
```
Thus $`\Re y_1^{(j)}`$ bounds the masses $`\mu_j([0,2\pi])`$ uniformly. Boundedness of the continuous kernel and the equality for $`\Im y_1^{(j)}`$ also bound $`\beta_j`$. Positive measures of bounded mass on a compact circle have a weakly convergent subsequence, and a further subsequence makes $`\beta_j`$ converge. Integrals of the continuous kernels converge to the corresponding integrals against the limiting positive measure. The limiting vector therefore has a representation, proving closedness. This argument avoids the generally false assertion that every linear image of a closed cone is closed.

## (b) Dual cone
Nonnegativity of $`\Re(x^Hy)`$ for every $`\beta\in\mathbb R`$ forces $`\Im(\mathbf1^Tx)=0`$. Testing point masses at each $`\theta`$ then forces
```math
\Re\sum_l x_l\frac{e^{-i\theta}+\bar w_l}{e^{-i\theta}-\bar w_l}\ge0
\quad\text{for every }\theta.
```
Conversely these conditions make the pairing nonnegative for every positive measure, while the imaginary constant contributes zero. They are exactly the stated dual-cone description.

## (c) Positive semidefinite representation of the dual
Define $`D_{kl}=1-w_k\bar w_l`$ and
```math
A(Q)_l=\sum_k\frac{Q_{kl}}{D_{kl}}.
```
For Hermitian $`Q`$, $`\sum_l A(Q)_l`$ is real by conjugating and interchanging its indices. If $`x=A(Q)`$, direct use of
```math
\frac{K_k(\theta)+\overline{K_l(\theta)}}{2D_{kl}}
=\frac1{(1-w_ke^{-i\theta})(1-\bar w_le^{i\theta})}
```
gives
```math
\Re\sum_lx_l\overline{K_l(\theta)}
=\sum_{k,l}\frac{Q_{kl}}
{(1-w_ke^{-i\theta})(1-\bar w_le^{i\theta})}\ge0
```
when $`Q\succeq0`$. For example if $`Q=qq^H`$ the right side is $`|\sum_kq_k/(1-w_ke^{-i\theta})|^2`$; summing rank-one decompositions proves the general case. Thus $`A(\mathbb H_+^n)\subseteq K_{\rm np}^*`$.

For the reverse inclusion take $`x\in K_{\rm np}^*`$ and put $`h(t)=\sum_lx_l(1+\bar w_lt)/(1-\bar w_lt)`$. On $`|t|=1`$, $`r(t)=\Re h(t)\ge0`$. Multiply by $`|d(t^{-1})|^2`$, where $`d(\zeta)=\prod_k(1-w_k\zeta)`$. The resulting expression is a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial. Its possible extreme coefficients of degree $`n`$ vanish: the coefficient of $`t^n`$ is
```math
\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}2\left(\prod_k\bar w_k\right)
\left(\sum_lx_l-\sum_l\bar x_l\right)=0,
```
and the opposite coefficient is its conjugate. Its degree is therefore at most $`n-1`$. The Riesz–Fejér result supplied in the question provides a polynomial $`p`$ of degree at most $`n-1`$ such that
```math
r(t)=\left|\frac{p(t^{-1})}{d(t^{-1})}\right|^2.
```
The polynomials $`\prod_{j\ne k}(1-w_j\zeta)`$ form a basis of polynomials of degree at most $`n-1`$: evaluating a linear combination at $`\zeta=1/w_k`$ proves their independence. Consequently there are $`q_k`$ with
```math
\frac{p(\zeta)}{d(\zeta)}=\sum_k\frac{q_k}{1-w_k\zeta}.
```
Set $`Q=qq^H\succeq0`$ and $`x'=A(Q)`$. The first direction proves that $`x'`$ has the same function $`r`$ and a real coordinate sum. To prove $`x'=x`$, apply the maximum principle to the real part of the analytic disk function $`h_x-h_{x'}`$: it vanishes on the circle, so vanishes inside. The function is an imaginary constant, and its value at zero is real, hence it is identically zero. Uniqueness of partial fractions at the distinct poles $`1/\bar w_l`$ now forces each $`x_l-x'_l=0`$. This proves the asserted equality of cones, including $`Q=0`$ when $`r=0`$.

## (d) Pick-matrix characterization
Let $`P(y)_{kl}=(y_k+\bar y_l)/D_{kl}`$. This is Hermitian, and the preceding linear map satisfies
```math
\Re(A(Q)^Hy)=\tfrac12\,\mathrm{tr}(P(y)\bar Q).
```
(The entrywise conjugate appears because the exercise defines $`A`$ using columns of $`Q`$.) Conjugation maps the positive semidefinite Hermitian cone onto itself. Thus the pairing is nonnegative for every $`Q\succeq0`$ exactly when $`P(y)\succeq0`$: necessity follows by testing rank-one matrices, sufficiency by factoring either positive semidefinite matrix in the trace. By part (a) and the separating-hyperplane characterization of a closed convex cone, $`K_{\rm np}^{**}=K_{\rm np}`$. Taking the dual of part (c) therefore gives
```math
K_{\rm np}=\{y:P(y)\succeq0\}.
```

## (e) Least-squares fitting
The finite-dimensional convex problem is
```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{minimize}_{y\in\mathbb C^n}&\sum_k|y_k-w_k^{\rm target}|^2\\
\text{subject to}&P(y)\succeq0.
\end{array}
```
Here $`w_k^{\rm target}`$ denotes the specified target value, distinguished from the reciprocal location used in the proof. Real and imaginary parts of $`y`$ are the real decision variables. For a real SDP solver replace $`P\succeq0`$ by $`\begin{bmatrix}\Re P&-\Im P\\\Im P&\Re P\end{bmatrix}\succeq0`$; the quadratic objective can be epigraphed with a second-order cone. The cone is nonempty and closed, and the objective is coercive and strictly convex in the sampled values, so there is a unique optimal $`y`$. Part (d) guarantees at least one positive-real function realizing it, although that function need not be unique.
