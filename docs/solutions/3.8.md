# Exercise 3.8: The Hessian characterization of convexity

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.8, p. 114](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=128).

Original solution.

Use the usual second-order convention: $`f`$ is twice differentiable on an open domain. The theorem is that convexity is equivalent to a convex domain together with $`\nabla^2f(x)\succeq0`$ everywhere. On a lower-dimensional domain, the corresponding criterion is instead restricted to tangent directions, as in Exercise 3.9.

For necessity, a convex function has a convex domain by definition. Fix $`x`$ and a direction $`v`$. For sufficiently small positive $`h`$, convexity on the line gives
```math
f(x+hv)+f(x-hv)-2f(x)\ge0.
```
Second-order expansion of the left side is $`h^2v^T\nabla^2f(x)v+o(h^2)`$. Divide by $`h^2`$ and let $`h\downarrow0`$ to obtain a nonnegative quadratic form for every $`v`$.

For sufficiency, fix any segment between domain points $`x,y`$ and put $`g(t)=f(x+t(y-x))`$. Convexity of the domain keeps the segment in the domain, and
```math
g''(t)=(y-x)^T\nabla^2f(x+t(y-x))(y-x)\ge0.
```
The mean value theorem applied to $`g'`$ shows that $`g'`$ is nondecreasing. For $`s<t`$, the mean value theorem applied to $`g`$ then gives $`g(t)\ge g(s)+g'(s)(t-s)`$; the analogous inequality holds when $`t<s`$. This first-order inequality implies convexity: apply it from an interior point $`r=(1-\theta)s+\theta t`$ to both endpoints and add with weights $`1-\theta,\theta`$, cancelling the derivative terms. Hence $`g(r)\le(1-\theta)g(s)+\theta g(t)`$. As the segment was arbitrary, $`f`$ is convex.
