# Exercise 6.9: Quasiconvex rational minimax fitting

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.9, printed page 347. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=361).

For fixed $`t`$, the denominator $`q(t)`$ is affine in its coefficients. Therefore the domain, an intersection of the open halfspaces $`q(t)>0`$ for $`t\in[\alpha,\beta]`$, is convex. No assertion that an arbitrary intersection of open sets is open is required.

For a candidate error $`\eta\ge0`$, positivity of the denominators lets us multiply the inequalities without reversing them:
```math
\left|\frac{p(t_i)}{q(t_i)}-y_i\right|\le\eta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
-\eta q(t_i)\le p(t_i)-y_iq(t_i)\le\eta q(t_i).
```
For fixed $`\eta`$ these are affine inequalities in $`a,b`$. The objective sublevel set is their intersection with the convex domain, hence convex. For $`\eta<0`$ it is empty. This proves quasiconvexity and supplies the feasibility test used in bisection. Positivity must hold over the entire interval as specified, not just at the sample locations; dropping that condition would change the problem.
