# Exercise 11.20: A dual barrier method for network utility maximization

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.20, printed page 628. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=642).

Let $`A\in\mathbb R^{L\times n}`$ be the nonnegative route-incidence matrix and $`c\succ0`$ the link capacities. Assume each route uses at least one link, so every column of $`A`$ is nonzero.

## (a) Dual and scalar conjugates
For prices $`\lambda\ge0`$, the maximization Lagrangian is
```math
\sum_iU_i(x_i)+\lambda^T(c-Ax).
```
Its supremum over $`x\succ0`$ separates, so the dual is
```math
\text{minimize}_{\lambda\ge0}\quad
G(\lambda)=c^T\lambda+\sum_iV_i(-(A^T\lambda)_i),
\qquad V_i(a)=\sup_{x>0}\{ax+U_i(x)\}.
```
For each $`a<0`$, the derivative $`a+U_i'(x)`$ decreases continuously from positive infinity to the negative value $`a`$. Strict concavity gives a unique root $`U_i'(x)=-a`$, which uniquely maximizes the scalar objective and makes $`V_i(a)`$ finite. For $`a>0`$, the supremum is infinite.

There is a missing condition in the source's claimed domain equality at $`a=0`$. In fact $`V_i(0)=\sup_x U_i(x)`$, so
```math
\mathrm{dom}V_i=(-\infty,0)
\quad\text{or}\quad(-\infty,0],
```
depending on whether the utility is unbounded above or bounded above. The stated derivative limits do not exclude the second case: $`U(x)=-1/x`$ is strictly concave, has exactly the prescribed derivative limits, and $`V(0)=0`$. With the additional usual assumption $`U_i(x)\to\infty`$, the printed domain $`-\mathbb R_{++}`$ is correct. This qualification does not affect the strictly positive price barrier method below.

## (b) Barrier Newton method and comparison
Start with $`\lambda\succ0`$, which ensures strictly positive route prices $`p=A^T\lambda`$. For each route solve $`U_i'(x_i)=p_i`$. On portions with $`U_i''(x_i)<0`$, let $`D=\mathrm{diag}(-1/U_i''(x_i))\succ0`$. Differentiating the scalar optimum gives
```math
\nabla G=c-Ax,\qquad\nabla^2G=ADA^T.
```
At barrier parameter $`t`$ minimize $`tG(\lambda)-\sum_{\ell=1}^L\log\lambda_\ell`$. Its gradient and Hessian are
```math
g=t(c-Ax)-\lambda^{-1},\qquad
H=tADA^T+B,\quad B=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_\ell^{-2}).
```
Compute a Newton step, backtrack to keep prices positive and decrease the barrier objective, and increase $`t`$ until the desired gap is reached. At an exact center, stationarity gives $`c-Ax=(1/t)\lambda^{-1}\succ0`$, so the recovered rates are primal feasible and their utility gap to the dual value is
```math
G(\lambda)-\sum_iU_i(x_i)=\lambda^T(c-Ax)=L/t.
```
This supplies a direct primal recovery and stopping certificate.

When $`AA^T`$ is sparse, factor the $`L\times L`$ dual Hessian directly; its graph joins links sharing a route. When $`A^TA`$ is sparse, use Woodbury and factor
```math
(tD)^{-1}+A^TB^{-1}A,
```
an $`n\times n`$ matrix whose graph joins routes sharing a link. For example the Newton direction is $`-B^{-1}g+B^{-1}A[(tD)^{-1}+A^TB^{-1}A]^{-1}A^TB^{-1}g`$. This is the same pair of sparsity alternatives as the primal barrier system in §11.8.4, with their direct/Schur roles exchanged. Sparse assembly and vector work are proportional to route incidence plus the resulting nonzeros, while factorization depends on fill. Dense costs are $`O(nL^2+L^3)`$ for the link system or $`O(Ln^2+n^3)`$ for the route system, plus the scalar utility inversions. Neither method has a universal structural advantage.

Strict concavity alone need not give $`U_i''<0`$ at every point. The smooth Hessian formulas assume that nonzero-curvature condition and twice differentiability; at a zero-curvature point the conjugate can fail to be twice differentiable and a nonsmooth or suitably regularized solver is needed. This is an algorithmic regularity condition beyond the scalar existence/uniqueness claim in (a).
