# Exercise 11.8: First-order prediction versus a new centering Newton step

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.8, printed page 625. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=639).

Evaluate all derivatives at $`x=x_*(t)`$ and set $`H_t=t\nabla^2f_0+\nabla^2\phi`$. The tangent formula from Exercise 11.7 gives the predictor
```math
\hat x=x-(\mu-1)t H_t^{-1}\nabla f_0(x).
```
At the same point, the gradient of the new centering objective is
```math
\mu t\nabla f_0+\nabla\phi=(\mu-1)t\nabla f_0,
```
while its Hessian is $`H_{\mu t}=\mu t\nabla^2f_0+\nabla^2\phi`$. Its full Newton update is therefore
```math
x+\Delta x_{\rm nt}
=x-(\mu-1)t H_{\mu t}^{-1}\nabla f_0(x).
```
The predictor uses the old centering Hessian, whereas the actual new Newton update uses the new one. They agree exactly when the objective is linear, since $`\nabla^2f_0=0`$; they also agree in any instance where that Hessian difference annihilates the relevant solved direction. For a nonlinear objective they generally differ even though both are first-order accurate in $`\mu-1`$. Either proposed full step may leave the strict feasible domain, so a practical predictor-corrector implementation must check feasibility and shorten the prediction if necessary.
