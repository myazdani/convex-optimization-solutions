# Exercise 3.56: Gamma and Dirichlet log-concavity

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.56, p. 124](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=138).

Original solution.

## (a) Gamma density

For $`x>0`$, its logarithm is
```math
\log f(x)=\lambda\log\alpha-\log\Gamma(\lambda)
 +(\lambda-1)\log x-\alpha x.
```
The first two terms are constants. Since $`\lambda\ge1`$, the log term has a nonnegative coefficient and is concave; the final term is affine. Equivalently its second derivative is $`-(\lambda-1)/x^2\le0`$. Hence the density is log-concave. At zero, the continuous extension is zero for $`\lambda>1`$ and $`\alpha`$ for $`\lambda=1`$; the multiplicative log-concavity inequality follows there by limits. Thus the stated nonnegative-halfline domain is covered.

## (b) Dirichlet density

On the open simplex $`x_i>0,\sum_i x_i<1`$, write $`C`$ for the positive normalization constant. Then
```math
\log f(x)=\log C+\sum_{i=1}^n(\lambda_i-1)\log x_i
 +(\lambda_{n+1}-1)\log(1-\mathbf1^Tx).
```
Every coefficient is nonnegative by $`\lambda\ge\mathbf1`$. Each logarithm is concave on this convex domain, the last by affine composition. Their nonnegative weighted sum is concave. Parameters equal to one simply remove the corresponding term, so the boundary parameter cases require no separate curvature argument.
