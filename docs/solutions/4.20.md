# Exercise 4.20: Wireless max-min SINR as a fractional program

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.20](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=210). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`I_i(p)=\sum_{k\ne i}G_{ik}p_k`$ and let $`\mathcal P`$ be the polytope defined by nonnegative powers, individual upper bounds, group upper bounds, and received-power upper bounds. A direct generalized linear-fractional maximization is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}_{p\in\mathcal P}\quad
\min_i\frac{G_{ii}p_i}{I_i(p)+\sigma_i}.
```

All denominators are strictly positive on $`\mathcal P`$ because $`\sigma_i>0`$. Equivalently, in the book's minimization convention, minimize

```math
\max_i\frac{-G_{ii}p_i}{I_i(p)+\sigma_i}\quad\text{over }p\in\mathcal P.
```

This is a maximum of affine-over-positive-affine functions with linear constraints, hence a generalized linear-fractional program. It avoids requiring positive desired-signal powers. The often-used reciprocal formulation is valid when the optimum SINR is positive:

```math
\mathrm{minimize}_{p\in\mathcal P,\ G_{ii}p_i>0}\quad
\max_i\frac{I_i(p)+\sigma_i}{G_{ii}p_i},
```

whose optimum is the reciprocal of the maximum minimum SINR.

For clarity, a trial nonnegative SINR $`\gamma`$ is achievable exactly when the following LP feasibility problem is feasible:

```math
\begin{aligned}
G_{ii}p_i-\gamma\sum_{k\ne i}G_{ik}p_k&\ge\gamma\sigma_i,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,\\
0\le p_i&\le P_i^{\max},\\
\sum_{k\in K_\ell}p_k&\le P_\ell^{\mathrm{gp}},\quad\ell=1,\ldots,m,\\
\sum_kG_{ik}p_k&\le P_i^{\mathrm{rc}},\quad i=1,\ldots,n.
\end{aligned}
```

Thus feasible levels form an interval and bisection can find the optimum. The feasible power polytope is nonempty (it contains zero) and compact, and the SINR objective is continuous, so a maximum exists. If some $`G_{ii}=0`$, the maximum minimum SINR is zero, and the direct formulation remains valid. If all diagonal gains are positive, a sufficiently small common positive power satisfies every strictly positive limit, giving strictly positive SINR and validating the reciprocal formulation. A finite upper bound for bisection is $`\min_i G_{ii}P_i^{\max}/\sigma_i`$.

## Validation

The formula keeps receiver noise strictly positive and does not confuse total received-power limits with interference-only limits. Zero direct gains are treated explicitly.
