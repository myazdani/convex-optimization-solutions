# Exercise 3.21: Convexity of maxima and ordered absolute sums

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.21, p. 117](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=131).

Original solution.

## (a) Largest affine-residual norm

Each function $`f_i(x)=\|A^{(i)}x-b^{(i)}\|`$ is convex by the norm triangle inequality. Therefore
```math
\begin{aligned}
\max_i f_i(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)
&\le\max_i\{\theta f_i(x)+(1-\theta)f_i(y)\}\\
&\le\theta\max_i f_i(x)+(1-\theta)\max_i f_i(y),
\end{aligned}
```
proving convexity.

## (b) Sum of the largest absolute coordinates

For $`1\le r\le n`$,
```math
\sum_{i=1}^r|x|_{[i]}
=\max_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\ |S|=r}\sum_{i\in S}|x_i|
=\max_{\substack{|S|=r\\\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}}}
\sum_{i\in S}\epsilon_ix_i.
```
The last expression is a maximum of finitely many linear functions, hence convex. Equality follows by selecting the largest absolute coordinates and their signs; ties and zero coordinates cause no difficulty. The convention $`r=0`$ would give the identically zero function.
