# Exercise 8.2: Chebyshev sets are closed and convex

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.2, printed page 447. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=461).

Write $`P(x)`$ for the unique Euclidean nearest point and $`d(x)=\|x-P(x)\|_2`$.

## (a) Nonemptiness and closedness
Existence of even one projection implies nonemptiness. If $`c_j\in C`$ converges to $`c`$, the distance of $`c`$ to $`C`$ is zero. Its attained nearest distance is zero, so $`P(c)=c\in C`$. Thus $`C`$ is closed.

## (b) Continuity of the projection
If $`x_j\to x`$, comparison with a fixed $`c\in C`$ bounds $`\|x_j-P(x_j)\|`$, and hence bounds $`P(x_j)`$. Any convergent subsequence has a limit $`p\in C`$ by closedness. Passing to the limit in
```math
\|x_j-P(x_j)\|\le\|x_j-c\|\quad(c\in C)
```
shows $`p`$ is a nearest point to $`x`$, so uniqueness forces $`p=P(x)`$. Every subsequential limit is the same and the sequence is bounded, proving $`P(x_j)\to P(x)`$.

## (c) Segment from the projection
Put $`p=P(x_0)`$, $`r=\|x_0-p\|>0`$, and $`x=p+\theta(x_0-p)`$ with $`0\le\theta\le1`$. For every $`c\in C`$,
```math
r\le\|x_0-c\|\le\|x_0-x\|+\|x-c\|
=(1-\theta)r+\|x-c\|.
```
Thus $`\|x-c\|\ge\theta r=\|x-p\|`$. Uniqueness gives $`P(x)=p`$.

## (d) Extension along the outward ray
We give the additional argument needed to extend beyond the segment; continuity alone does not automatically extend a projection ray.

First, $`d^2`$ is continuously differentiable. Comparing the distance at $`x+h`$ with the candidate $`P(x)`$ gives
```math
d(x+h)^2-d(x)^2\le2(x-P(x))^Th+\|h\|^2.
```
Using $`P(x+h)`$ as a candidate at $`x`$ gives the reverse bound with $`P(x+h)`$ in place of $`P(x)`$. Part (b) makes their difference $`o(\|h\|)`$. Thus $`\nabla d^2(x)=2(x-P(x))`$, continuously. Off $`C`$,
```math
\nabla d(x)=\frac{x-P(x)}{d(x)},\qquad\|\nabla d(x)\|=1.
```
Take a solution of the continuous-vector-field initial value problem $`z'(s)=\nabla d(z(s))`$, $`z(0)=x_0`$. Local existence follows by the usual Euler-polygon compactness construction for a continuous bounded vector field. The chain rule gives $`d(z(s))=r+s`$, and unit speed gives $`\|z(s)-x_0\|\le s`$. Since $`p\in C`$,
```math
r+s=d(z(s))\le\|z(s)-p\|
\le\|z(s)-x_0\|+\|x_0-p\|\le s+r.
```
All inequalities are equalities. Equality in the Euclidean triangle inequality forces $`z(s)=p+(r+s)(x_0-p)/r`$; also $`p`$ is a nearest point, so $`P(z(s))=p`$. The solution extends for every finite $`s\ge0`$: its speed is bounded, and its distance from the closed exceptional set $`C`$ is at least $`r`$, so at a finite endpoint it has a limit at which local existence applies again. This proves the entire outward-ray claim with $`\theta=1+s/r`$.

## (e) Convexity
For $`x_0\notin C`$ set $`v=x_0-P(x_0)`$ and $`p=P(x_0)`$. Parts (c),(d) give, for all $`t>0`$ and $`c\in C`$,
```math
t^2\|v\|^2\le\|p+tv-c\|^2
=t^2\|v\|^2-2t\,v^T(c-p)+\|c-p\|^2.
```
Divide by $`2t`$ and let $`t\to\infty`$ to get $`v^T(c-p)\le0`$. The closed halfspace $`\{z:v^T(z-p)\le0\}`$ contains $`C`$ and excludes $`x_0`$. Intersect these halfspaces over all $`x_0\notin C`$: the intersection is exactly $`C`$, hence convex. If $`C=\mathbb R^n`$, convexity is immediate.
