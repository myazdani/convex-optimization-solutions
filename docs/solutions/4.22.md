# Exercise 4.22: The positive-definite trust-region secular equation

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.22](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=211). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

The closed unit ball is compact and the objective is strictly convex, so there is a unique optimizer. The origin satisfies the inequality strictly, hence Slater's condition holds. Use the Lagrangian

```math
L(x,\lambda)=\tfrac12x^TPx+q^Tx+r+\tfrac\lambda2(x^Tx-1),\qquad\lambda\ge0.
```

The KKT conditions, necessary and sufficient here, are

```math
(P+\lambda I)x=-q,\quad \|x\|_2\le1,\quad
\lambda(\|x\|_2^2-1)=0,\quad\lambda\ge0.
```

Since $`P+\lambda I\succ0`$, they give $`x=-(P+\lambda I)^{-1}q`$.

If $`\|P^{-1}q\|_2\le1`$, take $`\lambda=0`$; the unconstrained optimizer is feasible. Otherwise the constraint must be active, and $`\lambda>0`$ solves

```math
h(\lambda)=q^T(P+\lambda I)^{-2}q=1.
```

Diagonalize $`P=U\mathrm{diag}(p_i)U^T`$, $`p_i>0`$, and put $`a=U^Tq`$. On $`[0,\infty)`$,

```math
h(\lambda)=\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{(p_i+\lambda)^2},\qquad
h'(\lambda)=-2\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{(p_i+\lambda)^3}\lt 0
```

when $`q\ne0`$. Also $`h(\lambda)\to0`$. Therefore $`h(0)>1`$ gives one and only one positive root, obtainable by scalar bisection. It is the largest real root of the secular equation: no larger positive root can exist, and negative roots are smaller. When $`h(0)\le1`$, there are no positive roots, and any largest defined root is nonpositive, so taking its maximum with zero recovers $`\lambda=0`$.

## Degeneracy in the wording of the root formula

For $`q=0`$ the equation is $`0=1`$ and has no root, although the optimization problem has the perfectly valid unique solution $`x^\star=0`$. Thus the source's largest-root notation needs this exception (or the convention that a missing root yields zero after truncation). More generally, negative poles associated with zero components of $`q`$ are irrelevant to optimization. The unambiguous prescription for every input is: test the unconstrained solution first; only if its norm exceeds one solve the unique positive-root equation. This retains exactly the stated solution formula where the largest-root expression is defined.

## Validation

The multiplier scaling matches the displayed $`P+\lambda I`$. Sufficiency, root uniqueness on the relevant interval, inactivity, and the zero-vector exception are proved without numerical assumptions.
