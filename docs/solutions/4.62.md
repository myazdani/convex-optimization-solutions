# Exercise 4.62: Joint power and bandwidth allocation

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.62](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=224). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

For $`W>0`$, define $`r_i(P,W)=\alpha_iW\log(1+\beta_iP/W)`$. It is the perspective of the concave increasing scalar function $`\alpha_i\log(1+\beta_iP)`$, so it is jointly concave on $`P\ge0,W>0`$.

The boundary convention $`r_i(P,0)=0`$ is its continuous limit for finite $`P\ge0`$. At $`(0,0)`$ continuity on the nonnegative orthant also follows from $`0\le W\log(1+\beta P/W)\le\beta P`$. The extension remains concave, by taking limits of the concavity inequality at positive bandwidths.

An explicit convex formulation introduces achieved-rate variables $`R_i\ge0`$:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{maximize}\quad&\sum_i u_i(R_i)\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad&R_i\le r_i(P_i,W_i),\qquad P_i,W_i\ge0,\\
&\sum_iP_i=P_{\mathrm{tot}},\qquad\sum_iW_i=W_{\mathrm{tot}}.
\end{aligned}
```

Each rate inequality is a hypograph of a concave function; the objective is concave and all remaining constraints are affine. Since each utility is nondecreasing, taking $`R_i=r_i(P_i,W_i)`$ never hurts. Thus every physical allocation has an equal-value lift, and every lifted allocation's utility is at most its true-rate utility. Values and optimizer recovery are exact even when a utility has flat intervals and some rate bounds remain slack.

One can also eliminate $`R`$: each composition $`u_i(r_i(P_i,W_i))`$ is concave because the outer function is concave and nondecreasing. With finite concave utilities on their stated full domain, the objective is continuous on the compact product of the resource simplices, so a maximum exists.

## Validation

The zero-bandwidth boundary, positive fixed resource totals, and the monotonicity needed for composition and rate recovery are retained.
