# Exercise 8.27: Convex restrictions for minimum separations

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.27, printed page 452. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=466).

For fixed unit vectors $`a_{ij}`$, all added inequalities $`a_{ij}^T(x_i-x_j)\ge D_{\min}`$ are affine in the concatenated variables. Together with the original convex constraints and convex objective they form a convex optimization problem.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives, at every restricted feasible point,
```math
\|x_i-x_j\|_2\ge a_{ij}^T(x_i-x_j)\ge D_{\min},
```
so each original distance constraint is satisfied. It is a restriction rather than a relaxation; it can be infeasible even when the original nonconvex problem is feasible, and its optimal objective is an upper bound on the unrestricted minimum when it is feasible.

For distinct approximate positions, the suggested $`a_{ij}=(\hat x_i-\hat x_j)/\|\hat x_i-\hat x_j\|`$ is the gradient of that pair's distance at the approximation. Convexity of the norm makes its affine tangent a global lower bound, explaining the construction. Coincident approximate positions require another chosen unit direction because the proposed normalization divides by zero. No approximation to the actual feasibility guarantee is involved once the directions are fixed.
