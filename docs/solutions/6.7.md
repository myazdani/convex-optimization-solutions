# Exercise 6.7: SVD solutions on norm trade-off curves

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 6.7, printed page 346. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=360).

Complete the right singular vectors to an orthonormal basis $`V\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}`$. Put $`z=V^Tx`$, $`d_i=\sigma_i^2`$ for $`i\le r`$ and zero otherwise, and $`c_i=\sigma_i u_i^Tb`$ for $`i\le r`$ and zero otherwise. Then
```math
\|Ax-b\|_2^2=\|b\|_2^2+\sum_{i=1}^n(d_i z_i^2-2c_i z_i),
\qquad \|x\|_2^2=\sum_i z_i^2.
```
All statements below allow rank deficiency and repeated singular values.

## (a) Tikhonov regularization
For $`\delta>0`$ the positive definite diagonal quadratic has the unique minimizer
```math
z_i=\frac{c_i}{d_i+\delta},\qquad
x=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{\sigma_i u_i^Tb}{\sigma_i^2+\delta}v_i.
```

## (b) Minimum on a sphere
Let $`d_- =\min_i d_i`$. Seek $`\lambda>-d_-`$ satisfying
```math
\sum_i\frac{c_i^2}{(d_i+\lambda)^2}=\gamma.\tag{1}
```
The left side is continuous, decreasing unless $`c=0`$, and tends to zero at infinity. If its limit at $`-d_-`$ exceeds $`\gamma`$, there is exactly one root; set $`z_i=c_i/(d_i+\lambda)`$.

Otherwise every component of $`c`$ in the minimum-eigenvalue eigenspace is zero. Set $`\lambda=-d_-`$, define $`z_i=c_i/(d_i-d_-)`$ for $`d_i>d_-`$, and choose the remaining coordinates arbitrarily subject to
```math
\sum_{d_i=d_-}z_i^2=\gamma-\sum_{d_i>d_-}\frac{c_i^2}{(d_i-d_-)^2}.
```
The right side is nonnegative by the case condition. This includes equality, $`c=0`$, and the rank-deficient large-radius case.

To prove global optimality, let $`D=\mathrm{diag}(d)`$. The construction gives $`D+\lambda I\succeq0`$, $`(D+\lambda I)z=c`$, and $`\|z\|^2=\gamma`$. For every other point $`w`$ on the sphere, direct expansion yields
```math
(w^TDw-2c^Tw)-(z^TDz-2c^Tz)
=(w-z)^T(D+\lambda I)(w-z)\ge0.
```
This proves the result even when the multiplier is negative.

## (c) Maximum on a sphere
Let $`d_+=\max_i d_i`$. If the limit at $`\lambda\downarrow d_+`$ of $`\sum_i c_i^2/(\lambda-d_i)^2`$ exceeds $`\gamma`$, solve
```math
\sum_i\frac{c_i^2}{(\lambda-d_i)^2}=\gamma,
\qquad \lambda>d_+,
```
and take $`z_i=-c_i/(\lambda-d_i)`$. Otherwise $`c`$ vanishes in the top eigenspace: take $`\lambda=d_+`$, set $`z_i=-c_i/(d_+-d_i)`$ off that eigenspace, and put the unused squared radius into that eigenspace. For every equal-radius $`w`$,
```math
(z^TDz-2c^Tz)-(w^TDw-2c^Tw)
=(w-z)^T(\lambda I-D)(w-z)\ge0.
```
Finally recover $`x=Vz`$. The secular equations require only scalar bisection after the SVD, with $`O(n)`$ work per scalar evaluation. Eigenvalue-endpoint cases must be tested explicitly rather than dividing by a zero denominator.
