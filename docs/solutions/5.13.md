# Exercise 5.13: Boolean Lagrangian relaxation equals the box LP bound

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.13](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=290). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

## (a) Quadratic-equality Lagrange dual

Use $`\lambda\succeq0`$ for $`Ax-b\preceq0`$ and unrestricted $`\nu_i`$ for $`x_i(1-x_i)=0`$. Let $`a=c+A^T\lambda`$. Then

```math
L=-b^T\lambda+\sum_i[-\nu_i x_i^2+(a_i+\nu_i)x_i].
```

Writing $`\mu_i=-\nu_i`$, a finite infimum requires $`\mu_i\ge0`$. For $`\mu_i>0`$ the scalar infimum is $`-(a_i-\mu_i)^2/(4\mu_i)`$. At $`\mu_i=0`$ it is zero when $`a_i=0`$, and $`-\infty`$ otherwise. Thus a dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}_{\lambda\succeq0,\,\mu\succeq0}\quad
-b^T\lambda-\sum_i\frac{(c_i+(A^T\lambda)_i-\mu_i)^2}{4\mu_i},
```

with the just-stated closed quadratic-over-linear convention at zero denominators. The negative perspective terms are concave, so this is a convex optimization problem. Weak duality gives a lower bound on the Boolean optimum without asserting strong duality for that nonconvex primal.

## (b) Equality with the LP relaxation bound

For any fixed scalar $`a`$, the best scalar term over $`\mu\ge0`$ is

```math
\sup_{\mu\ge0}-\frac{(a-\mu)^2}{4\mu}=\min(0,a).
```

To prove the upper bound, the scalar quadratic evaluated at $`x=0`$ has value zero and at $`x=1`$ has value $`a`$, so its unrestricted minimum cannot exceed either. Equality is attained by $`\mu=|a|`$ when $`a\ne0`$, or by $`\mu=0`$ when $`a=0`$. Eliminating all $`\mu_i`$ therefore gives the dual bound

```math
\sup_{\lambda\succeq0}\left[-b^T\lambda+
\sum_i\min\{0,c_i+(A^T\lambda)_i\}\right].
```

Now form the Lagrange dual of the LP relaxation while keeping $`0\preceq x\preceq1`$ in the domain. Its dual function is exactly

```math
-b^T\lambda+\inf_{0\preceq x\preceq1}(c+A^T\lambda)^Tx
=-b^T\lambda+\sum_i\min\{0,c_i+(A^T\lambda)_i\}.
```

Thus the two dual suprema are identical, with explicit optimizer lifts $`\mu_i=|c_i+(A^T\lambda)_i|`$, $`\nu_i=-\mu_i`$. The box LP is compact when feasible, and LP strong duality makes this common bound equal to its optimal value. If it is infeasible, the LP dual is unbounded above (it is always dual-feasible at $`\lambda=0`$ in this implicit-box form), and both relaxation bounds equal $`+\infty`$. None of this says that the Boolean and relaxed primal values coincide unless an additional integral-optimum certificate holds.

## Validation

Free equality multipliers acquire the necessary nonpositive effective sign through scalar minimization. Zero coefficients and zero quadratic weights are included exactly.
