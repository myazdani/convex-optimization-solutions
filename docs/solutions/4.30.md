# Exercise 4.30: Pipe and insulation design as a geometric program

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.30](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=213). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

All variables and physical coefficients are positive. Maximizing the monomial heat flow is equivalent to minimizing its reciprocal. The GP is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{minimize}\quad &\alpha_4^{-1}T^{-1}r^{-2}\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad &\frac{\alpha_1}{C_{\max}}Trw^{-1}
+\frac{\alpha_2}{C_{\max}}r
+\frac{\alpha_3}{C_{\max}}rw\le1,\\
&T_{\min}/T\le1,\quad T/T_{\max}\le1,\\
&r_{\min}/r\le1,\quad r/r_{\max}\le1,\\
&w_{\min}/w\le1,\quad w/w_{\max}\le1,\\
&10w/r\le1.
\end{aligned}
```

The objective is a monomial; the cost is a sum of three positive monomials; each remaining inequality has a monomial left side. Thus this is a GP in posynomial form. The logarithmic substitution $`(T,r,w)=(e^u,e^v,e^z)`$ yields a convex problem: the logarithm of the objective is affine, the cost constraint is a log-sum-exp bound, and all other inequalities become affine.

Every feasible positive design is unchanged by the reformulation, and the reciprocal reverses the objective order exactly. Recover the maximal heat flow as the reciprocal of the minimum GP objective. The thin-insulation approximation is part of the model; $`w\le0.1r`$ is retained rather than silently dropped. With consistent positive lower/upper bounds, a nonempty feasible design set is compact and an optimum exists.

The three separate cost contributions, the flow exponent $`r^2`$, positivity, and every bound in the source are represented.
