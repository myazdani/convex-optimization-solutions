# Exercise 9.17: Mixed derivatives, fixed nullspace, and Hessian comparison

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.17, printed page 516. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=530).

Let $`H(x)=\nabla^2f(x)`$ and $`T_x=D^3f(x)`$.

## (a) Mixed third derivatives
Restrict to the two-dimensional span of the directions under consideration. Self-concordance gives $`|T_x(u,u,u)|\le2(u^THu)^{3/2}`$. First replace the possibly singular $`H`$ by $`H+\epsilon I\succ0`$, which only enlarges the right side, and whiten coordinates using its square root. The symmetric-trilinear norm identity supplied in the question then gives
```math
|T_x(u,v,w)|\le2\sqrt{u^T(H+\epsilon I)u}
\sqrt{v^T(H+\epsilon I)v}\sqrt{w^T(H+\epsilon I)w}.
```
Let $`\epsilon\downarrow0`$. Taking all directions equal to coordinate vectors proves the pure-coordinate bound, and taking $`u=v=e_i,w=e_j`$ proves
```math
|f_{iij}(x)|\le2 f_{ii}(x)\sqrt{f_{jj}(x)}.
```
The argument also establishes the same mixed bound for any pair of directions in any dimension, by restriction to their span.

## (b) The Hessian nullspace is fixed
For domain points $`x,y`$ and a fixed $`w`$, put $`z(t)=x+t(y-x)`$, $`q(t)=w^TH(z(t))w`$, and $`a(t)=\sqrt{(y-x)^TH(z(t))(y-x)}`$. The mixed inequality gives $`|q'(t)|\le2a(t)q(t)`$. The continuous function $`a`$ is bounded on the compact segment. If $`q(0)=0`$, integrating this differential inequality (or multiplying by $`\exp[-2\int_0^t a(s)ds]`$) gives $`q(t)=0`$ throughout. Reversing $`x,y`$ proves equality of nullspaces at all points. A PSD matrix has $`w^THw=0`$ exactly when $`Hw=0`$.

If a nonzero fixed null direction existed, the restriction of $`f`$ to every sufficiently short segment in that direction would have zero second derivative and be affine. This contradicts strict convexity. Hence a strictly convex self-concordant function has positive-definite Hessian everywhere.

## (c) Hessian comparison
The correct step range is $`0\le t\alpha<1`$, where $`\alpha=\sqrt{v^TH(x)v}`$. The printed $`t<\alpha`$ is a reciprocal typo. Along the segment, the one-dimensional reciprocal-curvature bound gives
```math
\sqrt{v^TH(x+sv)v}\le\frac\alpha{1-s\alpha}.
```
For each $`w`$ with $`q(0)=w^TH(x)w>0`$, the mixed inequality therefore yields
```math
\left|\frac d{ds}\log q(s)\right|
\le\frac{2\alpha}{1-s\alpha}.
```
Integration from zero to $`t`$ gives
```math
(1-t\alpha)^2q(0)\le q(t)\le(1-t\alpha)^{-2}q(0).
```
If $`q(0)=0`$, part (b) supplies the same inequalities with both sides zero. If $`\alpha=0`$, the mixed bound makes every $`q`$ constant along the line. Since the scalar comparison holds for every $`w`$, it is exactly the two matrix inequalities requested, on the corrected range and while the segment lies in the domain. For instance $`f(s)=-\log s`$ at $`x=1,v=2,t=3/4`$ violates the claimed lower bound under the printed $`t<\alpha`$ range, confirming the correction is necessary.
