# Exercise 4.36: Reducing every GP posynomial to two terms

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.36](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=214). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Consider a positive sum of monomials $`p(x)=m_1(x)+\cdots+m_K(x)`$ with $`K>2`$. Introduce positive partial-sum upper bounds $`s_2,\ldots,s_K`$ and impose

```math
\frac{m_1(x)}{s_2}+\frac{m_2(x)}{s_2}\le1,\qquad
\frac{s_{j-1}}{s_j}+\frac{m_j(x)}{s_j}\le1,
\quad j=3,\ldots,K.
```

Every inequality has exactly two monomial terms in the enlarged positive variables. Induction gives $`s_j\ge\sum_{i=1}^j m_i(x)`$.

For an original constraint $`p(x)\le1`$, add the monomial inequality $`s_K\le1`$. Any lifted feasible point then satisfies the original constraint. Conversely if the original constraint holds, set every $`s_j`$ equal to its actual partial sum; these numbers are positive and all new inequalities hold, with the recursive ones tight.

For a multi-term objective, replace it by minimizing its final partial-sum bound $`s_K`$, a one-term monomial objective. Every original point lifts with equal objective, and every lifted point has objective at least its original objective. Thus optimal values agree, and optimal original variables are recovered simply by discarding the bounds. The exact-sum lift of any original optimizer is optimal in the new GP.

Apply this independently to every posynomial with more than two terms; keep one- and two-term posynomials and all monomial equalities unchanged. The construction adds $`K-1`$ variables and a linear number of constraints per replaced sum. A balanced binary sum tree gives the same result with logarithmic depth if desired.

## Validation

The construction uses inequalities, not forbidden posynomial equalities. Positivity of every partial sum ensures it is an admissible GP variable, and projection exactness is proved in both directions.
