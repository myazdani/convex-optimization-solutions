# Exercise 4.60: Convexity of log-optimal portfolio allocation

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.60](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=223). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Omit zero-probability scenarios and require $`p_j^Tx>0`$ for each remaining scenario. Each function $`\log(p_j^Tx)`$ is concave on its affine-positive domain: its Hessian is

```math
-\frac{p_jp_j^T}{(p_j^Tx)^2}\preceq0.
```

The nonnegative weighted sum $`\sum_j\pi_j\log(p_j^Tx)`$ is therefore concave, while $`x\succeq0,\mathbf1^Tx=1`$ and the positive-return domain are convex. Maximizing this objective over the simplex is a convex optimization problem, or equivalently minimizing its negative is convex.

Zero return in a positive-probability scenario gives log growth $`-\infty`$, reflecting complete wealth loss. A zero-probability scenario creates no logarithmic restriction. If all asset returns are nonnegative and at least one allocation has positive return in every positive-probability scenario, the extended objective is upper semicontinuous on the compact simplex and finite somewhere, so a finite maximum is attained. Strict uniqueness need not hold: it depends on whether the scenario return vectors distinguish allocations on the simplex's affine hull.

The supplied IID and finite-scenario assumptions justify the long-run interpretation for any fixed admissible allocation by the law of large numbers. They are not needed merely to verify convexity of the finite optimization problem.

## Validation

The proof checks the logarithmic domain and separates convexity from the probabilistic long-run interpretation.
