# Exercise 3.25: Maximum event-probability distance

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.25, p. 118](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=132).

Original solution.

Let $`r=p-q`$. Since both vectors have total mass one, $`\sum_i r_i=0`$. The positive and negative total masses therefore agree:
```math
\sum_{r_i>0}r_i=-\sum_{r_i<0}r_i=\tfrac12\sum_i|r_i|.
```
For every event $`C`$, $`\sum_{i\in C}r_i`$ lies between the negative total and the positive total. The event $`C=\{i:r_i>0\}`$ attains the upper endpoint. Hence
```math
d_{\mathrm{mp}}(p,q)=\tfrac12\|p-q\|_1.
```
This is convex jointly in $`p,q`$, being a nonnegative multiple of a norm composed with a linear map. The simplex-product domain is convex. The formula gives a natural convex extension to all pairs of real vectors. If one instead extends the original maximum-over-subsets definition literally without the equal-total assumption, its value is $`(\|p-q\|_1+|\mathbf1^T(p-q)|)/2`$, also convex; the two extensions coincide on the stated domain.
