# Exercise 5.16: An exact maximum-violation penalty

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.16](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=291). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

## (a) Convexity

The violation $`v(x)=\max\{0,f_1(x),\ldots,f_m(x)\}`$ is a pointwise maximum of convex functions, hence convex. Therefore $`\phi=f_0+\alpha v`$ is convex for $`\alpha>0`$.

## (b) Dual of the auxiliary epigraph

Use nonnegative multipliers $`\lambda_i`$ for $`f_i(x)-y\le0`$ and $`\mu`$ for $`-y\le0`$. Its Lagrangian is

```math
f_0(x)+\sum_i\lambda_i f_i(x)+y(\alpha-\mathbf1^T\lambda-\mu).
```

Infimizing over free $`y`$ requires $`\mu=\alpha-\mathbf1^T\lambda\ge0`$. Infimizing over $`x`$ gives the original dual function $`g(\lambda)`$. Hence the auxiliary dual is

```math
\mathrm{maximize}\quad g(\lambda)\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad\lambda\succeq0,\quad\mathbf1^T\lambda\le\alpha.
```

The lifted auxiliary problem always satisfies Slater: pick any $`x`$ and then $`y>\max(0,f_1(x),\ldots,f_m(x))`$, since the functions are finite on all of space.

## (c) Exactness threshold

Suppose $`\lambda^\star`$ is an attained original dual optimizer with finite value $`g(\lambda^\star)=p^\star`$ and $`\alpha>\mathbf1^T\lambda^\star`$. It is feasible for the auxiliary dual, so the auxiliary infimum is at least $`p^\star`$. Restricting to original feasible points gives the reverse inequality, even if the original infimum is approached by a sequence rather than known attained.

More precisely, for any $`x`$,

```math
\begin{aligned}
f_0(x)+\alpha v(x)
&=L(x,\lambda^\star)+\alpha v(x)-\sum_i\lambda_i^\star f_i(x)\\
&\ge p^\star+(\alpha-\mathbf1^T\lambda^\star)v(x).
\end{aligned}
```

Here each $`f_i(x)\le v(x)`$ and each multiplier is nonnegative. Any attained auxiliary optimizer has objective $`p^\star`$, so the strict positive coefficient forces $`v(x)=0`$. It is original-feasible, and its unchanged objective is $`p^\star`$, proving the claim. If the original value is $`-\infty`$, the auxiliary infimum is also $`-\infty`$ and no finite auxiliary optimizer exists, so the statement about any such optimizer is vacuous.

The strict threshold matters: for $`\min -x`$ subject to $`x\le0`$, the optimal multiplier is one. At $`\alpha=1`$, every $`x\ge0`$ minimizes $`-x+\max(0,x)`$, including infeasible points. A strictly larger penalty removes them.

## Validation

The multiplier budget is a one-norm bound, and the proof establishes feasibility of every attained auxiliary optimizer rather than only equality of infima.
