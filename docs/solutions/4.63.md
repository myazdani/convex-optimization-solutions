# Exercise 4.63: Manufacturing cost versus log-concave yield

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.63](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=225). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`X=\{x:Ax\preceq b\}`$ and let $`I=\{c^Tx:x\in X\}`$ be the achievable cost interval. The assumptions give positive costs and yields on $`X`$. Put $`g(x)=\log Y(x)`$, a concave function.

## (a) Yield frontier

On a nonempty cost slice,

```math
\log f(a)=\sup\{g(x):x\in X,\ c^Tx=a\}.
```

The identity holds because the exponential is continuous and increasing; it does not require the supremum to be attained. For $`a_1,a_2\in I`$, choose slice-feasible points whose log yields approach the two suprema. Their convex combination is feasible at $`a_\theta=\theta a_1+(1-\theta)a_2`$, and concavity of $`g`$ gives a log yield at least the corresponding weighted average. Taking approximation errors to zero yields

```math
\log f(a_\theta)\ge\theta\log f(a_1)+(1-\theta)\log f(a_2).
```

Thus $`f`$ is log-concave on $`I`$, with the usual zero extension outside the achievable interval if desired. Empty slices are infeasible; their log frontier is $`-\infty`$. Evaluating the frontier is the convex problem of maximizing $`g(x)`$ over affine/convex slice constraints. Numerically this yields an approximation if the slice supremum is not attained.

## (b) One-dimensional outer search

For a fixed positive cost $`a`$, the best cost per acceptable unit is $`a/f(a)`$. Indeed every point on that slice has $`a/Y(x)\ge a/f(a)`$, and a sequence approaching the yield supremum approaches that ratio. Taking infima over slices proves

```math
\inf_{x\in X}\frac{c^Tx}{Y(x)}=\inf_{a\in I}\frac{a}{f(a)}.
```

Evaluate frontier values on a cost grid spanning the range of interest, compare $`a/f(a)`$, and refine promising intervals with further slice solves. Retain the associated designs; an actual design gives a valid upper bound on the optimum, whereas a frontier supremum by itself may need an approximating design for recovery. If the grid's minimum occurs at an unsearched range endpoint, expand the range or establish an independent bound before calling it globally optimal.

Concavity of $`\log f`$ can guide certified refinement: chords give lower bounds on the log frontier between sampled points, while supporting lines, when available from slice dual multipliers or a supergradient, give upper bounds. On a positive interval $`[a_L,a_U]`$, any upper yield bound $`M`$ gives the valid lower ratio bound $`a_L/M`$. Refine intervals whose lower bound beats the best realized design until the desired gap is achieved. Sampling alone, without regularity or such bounds, is only an approximation and does not prove global optimality. In general $`\log a-\log f(a)`$ is not convex, so a single local search or convexity assertion for this outer ratio is unwarranted.

## Validation

The proof separates frontier evaluation from outer search, handles unattained slice suprema, and states exactly what finite samples do and do not certify.
