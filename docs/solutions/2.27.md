# Exercise 2.27: Converse to the supporting-hyperplane theorem

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.27, p. 63](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=77).

Original solution.

For each boundary point choose one of its supporting closed halfspaces, oriented to contain $`C`$, and let $`H`$ be their intersection. Clearly $`C\subseteq H`$. We prove that every point outside $`C`$ is excluded by one such halfspace, without presupposing convexity of $`C`$.

Fix $`z\notin C`$ and $`u\in\mathrm{int}\,C`$. Along $`u+t(z-u)`$, define $`t_0`$ as the end of the initial interval contained in $`C`$. Interior membership at $`u`$ gives $`t_0>0`$; openness of the complement near $`z`$ gives $`t_0<1`$. Closedness gives $`x_0=u+t_0(z-u)\in C`$, and points of the complement approach it along the segment, so $`x_0\in\mathrm{bd}\,C`$.

Let $`a^T(x-x_0)\le0`$ on $`C`$ be its chosen support, with $`a\ne0`$. In fact $`a^T(u-x_0)<0`$: equality would let a small displacement of the interior point $`u`$ in the direction $`a`$ remain in $`C`$ while violating the support inequality. Since
```math
z-x_0=-\frac{1-t_0}{t_0}(u-x_0),
```
we have $`a^T(z-x_0)>0`$. Thus $`z\notin H`$. Consequently $`C=H`$, an intersection of halfspaces, so $`C`$ is convex. The cases $`C=\mathbb R^n`$ and empty boundary are consistent with the empty-intersection convention.
