# Exercise 4.35: A sign-pattern class of signomial programs is geometric

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.35](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=214). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

On the positive orthant every monomial denominator is strictly positive. Therefore the inequality $`p_i(x)-q_i(x)\le0`$ is equivalent to $`p_i(x)/q_i(x)\le1`$. Its left side is a posynomial: division by a monomial subtracts its exponent vector from every exponent and divides each positive coefficient by its positive coefficient.

Similarly, $`r_j(x)-s_j(x)=0`$ is equivalent to the monomial equality $`r_j(x)/s_j(x)=1`$. Retain the posynomial objective. The complete GP is

```math
\mathrm{minimize}\quad f_0(x)\qquad
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad p_i(x)/q_i(x)\le1,\quad
r_j(x)/s_j(x)=1,\quad x\succ0.
```

The variables, feasible set, objective, optimal values, and optimizers are unchanged. In logarithmic variables $`x=e^y`$, the objective can be replaced by its logarithm, each inequality becomes a convex log-sum-exp inequality, and each monomial equality becomes affine. No cancellation or general signomial relaxation was used; the prescribed one-negative-term and two-term sign patterns are what permit positive monomial division.

## Validation

The equivalences depend on strictly positive monomials, and are valid for arbitrary real exponents on the stated positive-variable domain.
