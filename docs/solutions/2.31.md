# Exercise 2.31: Properties of dual cones

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.31, p. 64](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=78).

Original solution.

Let $`K`$ be a nonempty convex cone, with dual defined by nonnegative inner products.

## (a) Convex cone

If $`y,z\in K^*`$ and $`\alpha,\beta\ge0`$, then for every $`x\in K`$,
$`x^T(\alpha y+\beta z)=\alpha x^Ty+\beta x^Tz\ge0`$. Zero belongs as well, so $`K^*`$ is a convex cone.

## (b) Reversal of inclusion

If $`K_1\subseteq K_2`$, nonnegativity against all of $`K_2`$ implies nonnegativity against all of $`K_1`$. Thus $`K_2^*\subseteq K_1^*`$.

## (c) Closure

The representation $`K^*=\bigcap_{x\in K}\{y:x^Ty\ge0\}`$ is an intersection of closed halfspaces, so the dual is closed. Continuity also gives $`K^*=(\mathrm{cl}\,K)^*`$.

## (d) Interior

The source must exclude zero from its strict-positivity test; the printed version with every $`x\in\mathrm{cl}\,K`$ is false because $`0\in\mathrm{cl}\,K`$. The correct statement is
```math
\mathrm{int}\,K^*
=\{y:y^Tx>0\ \text{for every }x\in\mathrm{cl}\,K\setminus\{0\}\}.
```
If $`y`$ is interior and a nonzero $`x\in\mathrm{cl}\,K`$ has $`y^Tx=0`$, the arbitrarily small perturbation $`y-\epsilon x`$ is not dual-feasible, a contradiction. Conversely, suppose the strict test holds. If $`\mathrm{cl}\,K=\{0\}`$, the dual is all space and the test is vacuous. Otherwise the compact set $`D=\mathrm{cl}\,K\cap\{x:\|x\|_2=1\}`$ has
$`\delta=\min_{x\in D}y^Tx>0`$. Every perturbation of $`y`$ of norm less than $`\delta`$ remains nonnegative on $`D`$, hence on the whole cone by scaling. Thus $`y`$ is interior.

## (e) Solid primal implies pointed dual

If $`y,-y\in K^*`$, then $`y^Tx=0`$ for every $`x\in K`$. A linear functional vanishing on a set with nonempty interior is zero: evaluate it on a ball and on differences of points in that ball. Hence $`y=0`$, proving pointedness.

## (f) Bipolar identity

Every point of $`\mathrm{cl}\,K`$ pairs nonnegatively with $`K^*`$, so $`\mathrm{cl}\,K\subseteq K^{**}`$. For the reverse, take $`z\notin\mathrm{cl}\,K`$. Strict separation from the closed convex cone gives $`a`$ and $`\beta`$ with $`a^Tz<\beta\le a^Tx`$ on the cone. The zero point gives $`\beta\le0`$, and scaling cone points shows $`a^Tx\ge0`$ throughout: a negative value would tend to $`-\infty`$. Thus $`a\in K^*`$ and $`a^Tz<0`$, excluding $`z`$ from $`K^{**}`$.

## (g) Pointed closure implies solid dual

Put $`L=\mathrm{cl}\,K`$. If $`L=\{0\}`$ the assertion is immediate. Otherwise take the compact unit-direction set $`D=L\cap\{x:\|x\|_2=1\}`$. Its convex hull is compact in finite dimensions (by Carathéodory's theorem). It does not contain zero: from a convex combination summing to zero, any term with positive weight would have its negative in $`L`$, contradicting pointedness.

Let $`p\ne0`$ minimize distance to zero over $`\mathrm{conv}\,D`$. The projection inequality gives $`p^Td\ge\|p\|_2^2>0`$ for every $`d\in D`$. Scaling implies strict positivity on every nonzero point of $`L`$, so part (d) gives $`p\in\mathrm{int}\,K^*`$. This proves the asserted nonempty interior.
