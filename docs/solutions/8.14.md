# Exercise 8.14: Dimension factors for an inscribed ellipsoid

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.14, printed page 449. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=463).

Assume the maximum-volume ellipsoid exists with positive finite volume; for the polyhedron in the question this additionally requires boundedness. Strict feasibility alone is insufficient, for example for a halfspace. Apply an invertible affine transformation sending an optimal ellipsoid to the unit ball centered at zero, so every nonzero inequality is $`a_i^Tx\le b_i`$ with $`b_i\ge\|a_i\|`$.

The convex inscribed-ellipsoid problem maximizes $`\log\det B`$ under $`\|Ba_i\|+a_i^Tc\le b_i`$. It has a strictly feasible small ball. Its stationarity at $`(B,c)=(I,0)`$, with nonnegative constraint multipliers, yields weights $`w_i\ge0`$ supported only on active inequalities and unit outward normals $`u_i=a_i/\|a_i\|`$ such that
```math
\sum_iw_i u_i u_i^T=I,\qquad
\sum_iw_i u_i=0,\qquad\sum_iw_i=n.\tag{1}
```
Indeed the derivatives of $`\|Ba_i\|`$ and $`a_i^Tc`$ are $`a_ia_i^T/\|a_i\|`$ and $`a_i`$; put $`w_i=\lambda_i\|a_i\|`$ and take the trace. Complementarity makes $`u_i^Tx\le1`$ valid for all points of the transformed polyhedron.

## (a) General polyhedron
Fix a point $`x`$ and write $`r=\|x\|`$, $`t_i=u_i^Tx`$. Then $`-r\le t_i\le1`$, $`\sum_iw_it_i=0`$, and $`\sum_iw_it_i^2=r^2`$. On that scalar interval, $`(1-t_i)(t_i+r)\ge0`$, or $`t_i^2\le(1-r)t_i+r`$. Sum with weights (1) to obtain $`r^2\le nr`$. Therefore $`r\le n`$, proving containment in the radius-$`n`$ ball. Transforming back proves the claimed expansion factor.

## (b) Centrally symmetric polyhedron
Uniqueness of the maximum-volume ellipsoid implies its center is zero, since reflection would otherwise produce a different optimum. A linear transformation can thus normalize it without destroying symmetry. Each supporting inequality $`u_i^Tx\le1`$ is accompanied by the reflected bound, giving $`|t_i|\le1`$. Now (1) immediately gives
```math
\|x\|^2=\sum_iw_it_i^2\le\sum_iw_i=n.
```
Hence expansion by $`\sqrt n`$ suffices. The proof uses the existence assumption explicitly rather than claiming a maximum-volume ellipsoid for an arbitrary unbounded strictly feasible polyhedron.
