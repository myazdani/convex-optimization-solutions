# Exercise 4.65: Convex hybrid-vehicle fuel optimization

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.65](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=226). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Write $`g_t=p_{\mathrm{eng}}(t)`$, $`m_t=p_{\mathrm{mg}}(t)`$, $`b_t=p_{\mathrm{br}}(t)`$, and $`\phi(m)=m+\eta|m|`$. Replace the battery equalities by the convex inequalities

```math
E_{t+1}+\phi(m_t)\le E_t.
```

The complete convex problem minimizes $`\sum_{t=1}^TF(g_t)`$ subject to these inequalities and

```math
\begin{aligned}
p_{\mathrm{req}}(t)&=g_t+m_t-b_t,\qquad b_t\ge0,\\
0\le g_t&\le P_{\mathrm{eng}}^{\max},\qquad
P_{\mathrm{mg}}^{\min}\le m_t\le P_{\mathrm{mg}}^{\max},\\
0\le E_t&\le E_{\mathrm{batt}}^{\max},\qquad E_1=E_{T+1}.
\end{aligned}
```

Use the power constraints for $`t=1,\ldots,T`$ and energy bounds through $`T+1`$. The fuel objective is convex, the energy inequality is convex because $`\eta>0`$, and everything else is affine. Every physical trajectory is feasible here with the same fuel.

## Exact recovery, including battery capacity and cyclic charge

A relaxed trajectory may discard energy, so exactness needs more than a local assertion that its inequalities are active. We show that it can always be converted into a physical trajectory with the same engine powers, hence the same fuel, by reducing some regeneration and increasing friction braking.

First suppose $`0<\eta<1`$. For the fixed relaxed motor powers let $`d_t=\phi(m_t)`$. Positive motor power has $`d_t\ge0`$ (discharge), and negative motor power has $`d_t\le0`$ (charge). Put $`M=E_{\mathrm{batt}}^{\max}`$ and define the capped recursion from an initial value $`e\in[E_1,M]`$:

```math
\widehat E_1=e,\qquad
\widehat E_{t+1}=\min(M,\widehat E_t-d_t).
```

Induction using relaxed feasibility shows $`\widehat E_t\ge E_t\ge0`$ at every time, because both arguments of the minimum are at least $`E_{t+1}`$. Let $`H(e)=\widehat E_{T+1}`$. It is continuous (a composition of affine functions and minima), $`H(E_1)\ge E_1`$, and $`H(M)\le M`$. The intermediate value theorem therefore gives $`e^*\in[E_1,M]`$ with $`H(e^*)=e^*`$. The capped trajectory started there is within the energy bounds and cyclic.

When $`m_t\ge0`$, no upper-cap truncation occurs: $`\widehat E_t-d_t\le M`$. Keep $`\widehat m_t=m_t`$. When $`m_t<0`$, define the realized nonnegative charge $`q_t=\widehat E_{t+1}-\widehat E_t`$, which lies in $`[0,-d_t]`$, and choose

```math
\widehat m_t=-q_t/(1-\eta)\in[m_t,0].
```

Then $`\widehat E_{t+1}=\widehat E_t-\phi(\widehat m_t)`$ exactly. The motor bounds are preserved because the adjusted negative power lies between its old value and zero. Keep $`\widehat g_t=g_t`$ and set $`\widehat b_t=b_t+\widehat m_t-m_t\ge0`$; this restores exactly the same wheel-power balance. Thus all physical constraints hold with unchanged fuel.

The source only assumes $`\eta>0`$, so also consider $`\eta\ge1`$. Here $`\phi(m)\ge0`$ for every real $`m`$. Summing the relaxed energy inequalities around the cycle gives $`\sum_t\phi(m_t)\le0`$, forcing every term to vanish. The nonnegative slacks also sum to zero, so all energy inequalities were equalities already. For $`\eta>1`$ this forces $`m_t=0`$; for $`\eta=1`$ negative motor powers may have zero modeled energy change. In either case the relaxed trajectory is already exact.

These constructions prove identical optimal values and recover an original optimizer from any convex optimizer. They do not require strict increase of the fuel map or uniqueness of an optimum.
