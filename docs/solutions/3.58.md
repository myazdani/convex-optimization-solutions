# Exercise 3.58: Matrix concavity of the Schur complement

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.58, p. 124](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=138).

Original solution.

For a fixed $`z\in\mathbb R^{n-k}`$, the Schur-complement quadratic form has the variational representation
```math
z^T(C-B^TA^{-1}B)z
=\inf_{u\in\mathbb R^k}
\begin{bmatrix}u\\z\end{bmatrix}^T
X\begin{bmatrix}u\\z\end{bmatrix}.
```
Indeed the right side is $`u^TAu+2u^TBz+z^TCz`$, whose minimum, since $`A\succ0`$, is attained at $`u=-A^{-1}Bz`$ and has the stated value.

For fixed $`u,z`$ this expression is linear in the entire block matrix $`X`$. Its infimum over $`u`$ is therefore a concave scalar function of $`X\succ0`$. Applying that concavity inequality for every $`z`$ gives
```math
S(\theta X+(1-\theta)Y)
\succeq\theta S(X)+(1-\theta)S(Y),
```
which is matrix concavity. The proof includes variation of every block $`A,B,C`$ jointly, not just variation of the lower-right block.
