# Exercise 8.9: Least-squares fitting of Euclidean distances

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.9, printed page 448. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=462).

Introduce a centered Gram matrix $`G\succeq0`$, $`G\mathbf1=0`$, and squared distances
```math
D_{ij}=G_{ii}+G_{jj}-2G_{ij}\ge0.
```
Every Euclidean point configuration can be translated to have zero centroid, giving such a Gram matrix. Conversely factor $`G=X^TX`$ and take its columns as the points; their actual distances are $`\sqrt{D_{ij}}`$. The dimension can be $`\mathrm{rank}(G)\le n-1`$.

The objective is convex in the squared distances because $`\hat d_{ij}\ge0`$:
```math
(\sqrt{D_{ij}}-\hat d_{ij})^2
=D_{ij}-2\hat d_{ij}\sqrt{D_{ij}}+\hat d_{ij}^2.
```
An explicit SDP is obtained by introducing $`s_{ij}\ge0`$ and minimizing
```math
\sum_{i,j}(D_{ij}-2\hat d_{ij}s_{ij}+\hat d_{ij}^2)
```
subject to the Gram constraints above and
```math
\begin{bmatrix}D_{ij}&s_{ij}\\s_{ij}&1\end{bmatrix}\succeq0.
```
The last constraint says $`s_{ij}\le\sqrt{D_{ij}}`$. Whenever $`\hat d_{ij}>0`$, minimization makes it tight; when $`\hat d_{ij}=0`$, the objective does not depend on $`s_{ij}`$ and already equals the true squared fitting error $`D_{ij}`$. This proves exact equivalence rather than a relaxation. The measured diagonal contributes the unavoidable constant $`\sum_i\hat d_{ii}^2`$. If the optimal Gram matrix is zero, one may take $`k=1`$ with all points zero instead of using a zero-dimensional notation.
