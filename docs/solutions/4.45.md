# Exercise 4.45: Sum-of-squares Gram matrices and a bivariate quartic

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.45](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=217). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Index all monomials of total degree at most $`k`$ by their multi-indices and collect them in $`f(x)`$.

## (a) Gram matrix equivalence

If $`V\succeq0`$, factor $`V=C^TC`$. Then $`f^TVf=\|Cf\|_2^2`$ is a sum of squares of polynomials of degree at most $`k`$. Conversely, if $`p=\sum_jq_j^2`$, write each $`q_j=c_j^Tf`$. The matrix $`V=\sum_jc_jc_j^T\succeq0`$ gives $`p=f^TVf`$. Thus the Gram condition is equivalent to SOS, and either representation can be recovered from the other by factoring or collecting coefficients.

## (b) Linear coefficient constraints

If $`p(x)=\sum_\alpha p_\alpha x^\alpha`$, expansion gives

```math
p_\alpha=\sum_{\beta+\delta=\alpha}V_{\beta,\delta},\qquad |\alpha|\le2k.
```

The sum is over ordered pairs of monomials in $`f`$, so off-diagonal entries appear twice when appropriate. These are affine equalities in the polynomial coefficients and matrix entries. Together with $`V\succeq0`$ they are an SDP-representable SOS condition. It is an exact condition for SOS, but it is not asserted equivalent to nonnegativity for general multivariate polynomials.

## (c) Degree four in two variables

Use $`f=(1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2)^T`$ and write $`p=\sum_{i+j\le4}p_{ij}x^iy^j`$. Find a symmetric $`6\times6`$ matrix $`V\succeq0`$ satisfying all fifteen equations:

```math
\begin{aligned}
p_{00}&=V_{11},&p_{10}&=2V_{12},&p_{01}&=2V_{13},\\
p_{20}&=V_{22}+2V_{14},&p_{11}&=2V_{15}+2V_{23},&p_{02}&=V_{33}+2V_{16},\\
p_{30}&=2V_{24},&p_{21}&=2V_{25}+2V_{34},&p_{12}&=2V_{26}+2V_{35},\\
p_{03}&=2V_{36},&p_{40}&=V_{44},&p_{31}&=2V_{45},\\
p_{22}&=V_{55}+2V_{46},&p_{13}&=2V_{56},&p_{04}&=V_{66}.
\end{aligned}
```

Zero or missing terms in the polynomial mean their corresponding coefficients are zero in these same equations. Feasibility is necessary and sufficient for the requested SOS representation, and a factorization of $`V`$ explicitly supplies the squared polynomials.

## Validation

Every monomial of degree at most four occurs in the coefficient list, with ordered-pair multiplicities checked directly.
