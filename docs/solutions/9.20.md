# Exercise 9.20: Legendre duality preserves self-concordance

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 9.20, printed page 517. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=531).

## (a) A unique gradient preimage
For $`y\in\mathrm{dom}f^*`$, the function $`F_y(x)=f(x)-y^Tx`$ is bounded below, closed, strictly convex, and self-concordant: subtracting an affine function changes neither its Hessian nor third derivative. All its sublevel sets are closed. Exercise 9.19(b), with these hypotheses checked, gives an attained minimum; strict convexity makes it unique. First-order optimality gives $`\nabla f(x)=y`$.

The positive-definite Hessian makes this inverse gradient map locally smooth by the inverse-function theorem. Its image contains an open neighborhood of every such $`y`$, and the same stationary-point identity makes the conjugate finite there. Thus $`\mathrm{dom}f^*`$ is open and the local inverse maps agree globally by uniqueness. In particular
```math
\nabla f^*(y)=x(y),\qquad
\nabla^2f^*(y)=\nabla^2f(x(y))^{-1}.
```
These identities also follow by differentiating $`f^*(y)=y^Tx(y)-f(x(y))`$, whose $`dx`$ terms cancel.

## (b) Matching second and opposite third derivatives
Let $`H=\nabla^2f(\bar x)`$ and $`w=Hv`$. Along $`y(t)=\bar y+tw`$, differentiating $`\nabla f(x(t))=y(t)`$ gives $`x'(0)=H^{-1}w=v`$. Hence
```math
h''(0)=w^TH^{-1}w=v^THv=g''(0).
```
Differentiating the inverse Hessian gives
```math
h'''(0)=-w^TH^{-1}(D H(\bar x)[v])H^{-1}w
=-D^3f(\bar x)[v,v,v]=-g'''(0).
```
Therefore $`|h'''(0)|\le2(h''(0))^{3/2}`$. Every dual direction $`w`$ is of the form $`Hv`$, so this holds on every line at every dual-domain point. Convexity of the conjugate and its convex domain, together with these linewise inequalities, prove its self-concordance.
