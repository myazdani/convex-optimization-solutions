# Exercise 5.31: KKT conditions imply the supporting-gradient criterion

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.31](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=297). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`x`$ be any feasible point and put $`d=x-x^\star`$. Convexity and differentiability of each inequality function give

```math
\nabla f_i(x^\star)^Td\le f_i(x)-f_i(x^\star).
```

Multiply by $`\lambda_i^\star\ge0`$ and sum. Feasibility of $`x`$ and complementary slackness at $`x^\star`$ yield

```math
\sum_i\lambda_i^\star\nabla f_i(x^\star)^Td
\le\sum_i\lambda_i^\star f_i(x)-\sum_i\lambda_i^\star f_i(x^\star)
\le0.
```

Stationarity now gives

```math
\nabla f_0(x^\star)^T(x-x^\star)
=-\sum_i\lambda_i^\star\nabla f_i(x^\star)^Td\ge0.
```

This holds for every feasible $`x`$, exactly the requested supporting-hyperplane criterion. Convexity of the objective then implies $`f_0(x)\ge f_0(x^\star)+\nabla f_0(x^\star)^T(x-x^\star)\ge f_0(x^\star)`$, recovering global optimality as well.

## Validation

Inactive constraints need no separate treatment because complementary slackness eliminates their weighted values. Slater is not needed: the KKT conditions are supplied as hypotheses.
