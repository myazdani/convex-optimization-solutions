# Exercise 4.59: Convexity under stochastic and worst-case uncertainty

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.59](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=222). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

## (a) Expectations

Assume the expectations are well defined, either finite or proper extended values with no undefined infinity-minus-infinity operation. For $`0\le\theta\le1`$, pointwise convexity gives

```math
f_i(\theta x+(1-\theta)y,u)
\le\theta f_i(x,u)+(1-\theta)f_i(y,u).
```

Taking expectations preserves this inequality. Thus each expected objective or constraint function is convex on its expectation domain, and the stated stochastic problem is convex. The distribution of $`u`$ is fixed, independent of the decision vector; a decision-dependent distribution would require separate analysis.

## (b) Worst-case values

For a nonempty fixed uncertainty set, taking suprema gives

```math
\sup_u f_i(\theta x+(1-\theta)y,u)
\le\theta\sup_u f_i(x,u)+(1-\theta)\sup_u f_i(y,u).
```

Hence each worst-case function is convex, possibly taking $`+\infty`$. Equivalently each constraint is the intersection of the convex inequalities $`f_i(x,u)\le0`$ for all $`u\in U`$. The objective is a pointwise supremum of convex functions. No convexity of the uncertainty set or joint convexity in $`x,u`$ is needed.

## (c) Finite scenarios

For values $`u_1,\ldots,u_N`$,

```math
\mathbb E f_i(x,u)=\sum_{j=1}^N p_j f_i(x,u_j),\qquad
\sup_{u\in U}f_i(x,u)=\max_{1\le j\le N}f_i(x,u_j).
```

Thus the stochastic problem directly minimizes the weighted sum for index zero, subject to the analogous weighted-sum inequalities for the constraints. Zero-probability scenarios contribute no expected term and may be omitted from the expectation.

For the worst-case problem introduce $`t`$, minimize it, impose $`f_0(x,u_j)\le t`$ for every scenario, and impose $`f_i(x,u_j)\le0`$ for every constraint index and scenario. Every original point lifts with $`t`$ equal to its finite maximum, and every lifted objective bounds that maximum, proving exact epigraph equivalence.

## Validation

The expectation and worst-case domains, fixed-distribution assumption, and zero-probability versus worst-case scenario distinction are explicit.
