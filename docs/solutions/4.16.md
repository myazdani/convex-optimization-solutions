# Exercise 4.16: Minimum-fuel control as a linear program

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.16](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=208). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

The scalar fuel map can be written as a maximum of four affine functions:

```math
f(u)=\max\{u,-u,2u-1,-2u-1\}.
```

Indeed $`\max(u,-u)=|u|`$ and $`\max(2u-1,-2u-1)=2|u|-1`$; their maximum switches at $`|u|=1`$.

Introduce one fuel bound $`s_t`$ per input. The desired LP is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{minimize}\quad &\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}s_t\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad &x_0=0,\qquad x_N=x_{\mathrm{des}},\\
&x_{t+1}=Ax_t+bu_t,\quad t=0,\ldots,N-1,\\
&s_t\ge u_t,\quad s_t\ge-u_t,\quad
s_t\ge2u_t-1,\quad s_t\ge-2u_t-1.
\end{aligned}
```

For any admissible input trajectory, choosing $`s_t=f(u_t)`$ gives an LP-feasible point with its exact fuel cost. Conversely any feasible LP point gives an admissible trajectory and has objective at least its fuel cost. Every fuel bound is tight at an LP optimum because it has a positive objective coefficient. Thus the formulation is exact in feasible inputs, values, and optimizers.

One may eliminate the states: the terminal constraint is

```math
\begin{bmatrix}A^{N-1}b&A^{N-2}b&\cdots&b\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}u_0&u_1&\cdots&u_{N-1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}=x_{\mathrm{des}}.
```

This also characterizes feasibility. If feasible, a minimizer exists because $`\sum_t f(u_t)\ge\|u\|_1`$ has compact sublevel sets and the terminal equality is closed. Once the inputs are fixed, all states are uniquely determined.

## Validation

Both slopes and both signs of the fuel map are represented. The recurrence and zero initial state are retained exactly.
