# Exercise 5.32: Convexity of the perturbed optimal value

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.32](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=297). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Take two perturbations $`(u^1,v^1)`$, $`(u^2,v^2)`$ with finite upper epigraph levels $`t_1\ge p^\star(u^1,v^1)`$, $`t_2\ge p^\star(u^2,v^2)`$. For any $`\epsilon>0`$, choose feasible points $`x_j`$ for those perturbations with $`f_0(x_j)\le t_j+\epsilon`$. Such points exist even if the infimum is unattained; if the infimum is $`-\infty`$, any prescribed finite upper level can likewise be reached.

For $`0\le\theta\le1`$, let $`x_\theta=\theta x_1+(1-\theta)x_2`$. Convexity of the common domain and inequalities gives

```math
f_i(x_\theta)\le\theta f_i(x_1)+(1-\theta)f_i(x_2)
\le\theta u_i^1+(1-\theta)u_i^2,
```

while the affine equality gives $`Ax_\theta-b=\theta v^1+(1-\theta)v^2`$. The objective satisfies $`f_0(x_\theta)\le\theta t_1+(1-\theta)t_2+\epsilon`$. Taking the infimum at the combined perturbation and letting $`\epsilon\downarrow0`$ proves

```math
p^\star(\theta u^1+(1-\theta)u^2,\theta v^1+(1-\theta)v^2)
\le\theta t_1+(1-\theta)t_2.
```

Thus the epigraph is convex, which proves convexity of the extended optimal-value function. In the usual proper case this is the familiar convexity inequality with $`t_j=p^\star(u^j,v^j)`$. Infeasible perturbations have value $`+\infty`$ and impose no finite epigraph condition.

The same fact follows by partial minimization of the jointly convex function consisting of $`f_0(x)`$ plus the indicator of $`f_i(x)\le u_i,Ax-b=v`$. Neither argument proves closedness; indeed a convex value function can fail to be lower semicontinuous at a boundary perturbation, as Exercise 5.21 illustrates.

## Validation

Approximate minimizers avoid silently assuming attainment, and the proof does not confuse convexity of a projected epigraph with automatic closedness.
