# Exercise 7.3: Convex probit maximum likelihood

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.3, printed page 393. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=407).

Let $`\Phi`$ be the standard Gaussian distribution function and put $`s_i=1-2y_i\in\{-1,1\}`$. Symmetry of the Gaussian gives
```math
\Pr(y_i\mid u_i)=\Phi(s_i(a^Tu_i+b)).
```
Thus estimate $`a,b`$ by the convex problem
```math
\text{minimize}_{a,b}\quad-\sum_{i=1}^N\log\Phi(s_i(a^Tu_i+b)).
```
For completeness, writing the Gaussian density as $`\varphi`$, the second derivative of $`\log\Phi(t)`$ is
```math
-\frac{\varphi(t)}{\Phi(t)}
\left(t+\frac{\varphi(t)}{\Phi(t)}\right)<0.
```
For $`t\ge0`$ the bracket is positive. For $`t<0`$, integration of $`\varphi'(s)=-s\varphi(s)`$ yields $`\varphi(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t(-s)\varphi(s)ds>(-t)\Phi(t)`$, giving the same conclusion. Affine composition and summation prove convexity. With separated data the infimum may be approached only by diverging parameters; the formulation does not falsely promise a finite maximizer or identifiability when the design is rank deficient.
