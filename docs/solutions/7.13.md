# Exercise 7.13: Sharp finite-support moment bounds

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 7.13, printed page 396. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=410).

Let $`p_j=\Pr(X=\alpha_j)`$, $`c_j=\mathbf1_{\{\alpha_j\in S\}}`$, and $`F_{ij}=f_i(\alpha_j)`$. The largest permitted event probability is the finite LP
```math
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{maximize}&c^Tp\\
\text{subject to}&Fp=b,\quad\mathbf1^Tp=1,\quad p\ge0.
\end{array}\tag{P}
```
Any feasible coefficients in the proposed bounding problem define a function $`h(\alpha)=x_0+\sum_ix_if_i(\alpha)`$ that pointwise dominates the event indicator. Thus every compatible distribution satisfies
```math
\Pr(X\in S)\le\mathbb Eh(X)=x_0+\sum_ib_ix_i.
```
This proves the upper bound directly.

To show sharpness, take multipliers $`x_0,x`$ for the equality constraints in (P). Its Lagrangian for maximization is
```math
x_0+b^Tx+\sum_jp_j(c_j-x_0-(F^Tx)_j).
```
The supremum over $`p\ge0`$ is finite exactly when $`x_0+(F^Tx)_j\ge c_j`$ for all $`j`$, yielding precisely the LP in the exercise. The given moments are assumed compatible with at least one distribution, so (P) is feasible; its feasible set is a closed subset of the probability simplex and hence compact. Its maximum is attained and lies in $`[0,1]`$. Finite-dimensional LP strong duality gives equality with the bounding LP's minimum, so a maximizing probability vector in (P) achieves the stated upper bound. If the moment data are inconsistent, no such distribution exists and the attainment assertion has no meaning; compatibility is the necessary implicit premise.
