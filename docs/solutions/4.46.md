# Exercise 4.46: The multivariate moment matrix

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.46](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=218). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`\mathcal I_k=\{(i,j):i,j\ge0,\ i+j\le k\}`$ and order it consistently with the coefficient vector $`c`$. There are $`(k+1)(k+2)/2`$ such indices. Define

```math
H(\mu)_{(i,j),(r,s)}=\mu_{i+r,j+s},\qquad (i,j),(r,s)\in\mathcal I_k.
```

These required moments all have total degree at most $`2k`$, and each entry is linear in the given moment vector. Symmetry follows by exchanging the two multi-indices. Expanding the square and taking expectations gives

```math
\mathbb E\,p(t)^2
=\sum_{(i,j),(r,s)\in\mathcal I_k}c_{ij}c_{rs}\mathbb E(t_1^{i+r}t_2^{j+s})
=c^TH(\mu)c.
```

A square is nonnegative, so this quadratic form is nonnegative for every coefficient vector; hence $`H(\mu)\succeq0`$. A probability distribution also has the normalization $`\mu_{00}=1`$.

For example, at $`k=1`$, ordering the basis as $`(1,t_1,t_2)`$ gives

```math
H(\mu)=\begin{bmatrix}
\mu_{00}&\mu_{10}&\mu_{01}\\
\mu_{10}&\mu_{20}&\mu_{11}\\
\mu_{01}&\mu_{11}&\mu_{02}
\end{bmatrix}\succeq0.
```

The derivation proves a necessary condition for genuine moments: it tests nonnegativity on all squares of degree at most $`k`$. No converse for arbitrary bivariate truncated moment data is claimed; not every nonnegative multivariate polynomial is represented by those square tests.

## Validation

The matrix uses sums of multi-indices, includes all cross terms, and has precisely the dimension of the coefficient vector specified in the exercise.
