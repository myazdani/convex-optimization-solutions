# Exercise 2.39: Separation of cones with disjoint interiors

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.39, p. 66](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=80).

Original solution.

Apply separation to the nonempty disjoint open convex sets $`\mathrm{int}\,K`$ and $`\mathrm{int}\,\widetilde K`$. Choose the orientation so that for some $`y\ne0`$ and $`\beta`$,
```math
y^Tx\ge\beta\ge y^Tz
\qquad(x\in\mathrm{int}\,K,\ z\in\mathrm{int}\,\widetilde K).
```
The interiors are preserved by positive scaling. Sending the scale to zero on each side gives $`0\ge\beta`$ and $`\beta\ge0`$, so $`\beta=0`$. Hence $`y`$ pairs nonnegatively with the first interior and nonpositively with the second.

Every point of a convex set with nonempty interior lies in the closure of its interior. Indeed, if $`u`$ is interior and $`x`$ belongs to the set, then $`(1-t)x+tu`$ is interior for $`0<t\le1`$: an interior ball around $`u`$, scaled by $`t`$ and translated by $`(1-t)x`$, remains in the set. Letting $`t\downarrow0`$ proves the claim. Continuity therefore extends the two inner-product inequalities to all of the corresponding cones. Thus
```math
y\in K^*,\qquad -y\in\widetilde K^*,\qquad y\ne0,
```
as required, even if the original cones are not closed.
