# Exercise 3.13: Nonnegativity and equality of KL divergence

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 3.13, p. 115](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=129).

Original solution.

For positive vectors the book's divergence is
```math
D_{\mathrm{kl}}(u,v)=\sum_i\left(u_i\log\frac{u_i}{v_i}-u_i+v_i\right).
```
Set $`f(x)=\sum_i x_i\log x_i`$. Then $`\nabla f(x)_i=1+\log x_i`$ and $`\nabla^2f(x)=\mathrm{diag}(1/x_i)\succ0`$. Direct substitution gives
```math
D_{\mathrm{kl}}(u,v)=f(u)-f(v)-\nabla f(v)^T(u-v).
```
Convexity implies that this quantity is nonnegative. For completeness, along $`v+t(u-v)`$ its exact second-order integral form is
```math
D_{\mathrm{kl}}(u,v)
=\int_0^1(1-t)\sum_i
\frac{(u_i-v_i)^2}{v_i+t(u_i-v_i)}\,dt.
```
Every denominator is positive. The integral is zero when $`u=v`$ and strictly positive otherwise, proving the equality characterization. No normalization of the coordinate sums is assumed; the linear correction terms are essential for arbitrary positive vectors.
