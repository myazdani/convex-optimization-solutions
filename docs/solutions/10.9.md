# Exercise 10.9: Equality-constrained entropy: existence and Newton assumptions

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 10.9, printed page 558. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=572).

Write $`f(x)=\sum_i x_i\log x_i`$, $`x\succ0`$, and assume $`A`$ has full row rank and some strictly positive $`\hat x`$ satisfies $`A\hat x=b`$.

## (a) Unique strictly positive minimizer
Extend each term continuously to zero by $`0\log0=0`$. This extension is strictly convex on the nonnegative orthant and coercive: each scalar term is at least $`-1/e`$ and tends to infinity as its argument tends to infinity. On the nonempty closed feasible set $`x\ge0,Ax=b`$ it therefore has a unique minimizer $`x_*`$.

It has no zero coordinate. Otherwise move to $`x(t)=(1-t)x_*+t\hat x`$. A zero coordinate contributes $`t\hat x_i\log(t\hat x_i)=t\hat x_i\log t+O(t)`$; positive coordinates change their terms by $`O(t)`$. The strictly negative $`t\log t`$ contributions dominate for small positive $`t`$, contradicting optimality. Thus $`x_*\succ0`$ and solves the original problem uniquely.

## (b) Nonclosed feasible objective sublevel set
Take $`n=2,p=1,A=[1\ 0],b=1,x^{(0)}=(1,1)`$. The initial value is zero, and $`(1,t)`$ is feasible with value $`t\log t\le0`$ for $`0<t\le1`$. These points approach $`(1,0)`$, which is excluded from the domain. Hence the sublevel set is not closed.

## (c) A necessary correction to the stated residual-set assertion
For the literal full residual sublevel set (10.26), the assertion is false, even with a feasible initial point. Use the same data as (b) and $`\nu^{(0)}=0`$. The initial residual norm is $`\sqrt2`$. At
```math
x^{(k)}=(e^{-k},e^{-1}),\qquad\nu^{(k)}=k-1,
```
the dual residual $`\log x+\mathbf1+A^T\nu`$ is zero and the primal residual is $`e^{-k}-1`$. These pairs are all in that full residual sublevel set. Its Hessian entry $`1/x_1=e^k`$ has unbounded variation, and the KKT inverse contains the entry $`-1/x_1=-e^k`$. Thus both the bounded-inverse and Lipschitz assumptions fail there. This is not repaired merely by starting at a feasible point.

The intended convergence argument is valid on the invariant feasible slice, because Exercise 10.8 shows that every iterate started feasible keeps $`Ax=b`$. Here is the full compactness proof on that slice. Suppose $`\|q\|\le R`$, where $`q=\log x+\mathbf1+A^T\nu`$, and $`Ax=A\hat x=b`$. Then
```math
\sum_i(x_i-\hat x_i)(\log x_i+1)
=(x-\hat x)^Tq\le R\|x-\hat x\|.
```
For fixed $`\hat x_i>0`$, every scalar term on the left is bounded below, diverges at zero, and grows as $`x_i\log x_i`$ at infinity. If $`\max_i x_i\to\infty`$, the left side grows faster than the linear right side. Thus all coordinates are bounded above. The right side is now bounded, so divergence of a scalar term at zero bounds every coordinate away from zero as well. Bounded $`q`$ and $`\log x`$ bound $`A^T\nu`$, and injectivity of $`A^T`$ bounds $`\nu`$.

This feasible residual sublevel set is compact and stays inside the positive domain. On it the Hessian $`\mathrm{diag}(1/x_i)`$ is Lipschitz; the KKT matrix is nonsingular everywhere and its inverse is continuously bounded. Thus the chapter's Newton convergence argument applies to this invariant slice, which is all the feasible-start iterates need. The distinction from the full set in (10.26) must be made explicitly.

## (d) Dual method
The scalar infimum of $`x\log x+ux`$ over $`x>0`$ occurs at $`x=e^{-u-1}`$ and equals $`-e^{-u-1}`$. Hence the dual is unconstrained concave maximization
```math
g(\nu)=-b^T\nu-\sum_i\exp(-(A^T\nu)_i-1),
\qquad x_i(\nu)=\exp(-(A^T\nu)_i-1).
```
At its maximizer $`\nabla g=Ax(\nu)-b=0`$, and the recovered $`x`$ satisfies all primal KKT conditions, so it is the unique optimizer.

To verify the feasible-start Newton assumptions for $`F=-g`$, use $`b=A\hat x`$ to write, with $`u=A^T\nu`$,
```math
F(\nu)=\sum_i\left(e^{-u_i-1}+\hat x_i u_i\right).
```
Each scalar summand is coercive in both directions and bounded below; therefore a sublevel set bounds every $`u_i`$, and full row rank bounds $`\nu`$. The continuous function has closed compact sublevel sets. Its Hessian is $`A\mathrm{diag}(x(\nu))A^T\succ0`$, so on each such set its eigenvalues have positive lower and finite upper bounds and its derivative is bounded, giving the required Hessian Lipschitz constant. Thus the dual assumptions genuinely hold on the entire relevant sublevel set for every starting multiplier. The optimal multiplier is unique as well.
