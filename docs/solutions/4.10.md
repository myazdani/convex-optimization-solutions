# Exercise 4.10: Converting a general linear program to standard form

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 4.10](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=207). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Start from objective $`c^Tx+d`$, inequalities $`Gx\preceq h`$, and equations $`Ax=b`$, with unrestricted $`x`$. Introduce nonnegative vectors $`x^+,x^-`$ and $`s`$. With $`z=(x^+,x^-,s)`$, the standard form is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{minimize}\quad &[c^T,-c^T,0]z+d\\
\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad &
\begin{bmatrix}G&-G&I\\A&-A&0\end{bmatrix}z=
\begin{bmatrix}h\\b\end{bmatrix},\qquad z\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
```

The additive constant may be omitted by a solver; its omission subtracts $`d`$ from the reported optimum and changes no optimizers.

For any original-feasible $`x`$, choose $`x_i^+=\max(x_i,0)`$, $`x_i^-=\max(-x_i,0)`$, and $`s=h-Gx`$. These are nonnegative and satisfy the displayed equations. Conversely, every standard-form feasible point gives original-feasible $`x=x^+-x^-`$, with $`s=h-Gx`$. The objective is exactly preserved when the constant is retained.

The correspondence is surjective onto the original feasible set but not injective. All lifts of a fixed $`x`$ are

```math
x^+=\max(x,0)+w,\qquad x^-=\max(-x,0)+w,\qquad w\succeq0,
```

with the unique associated slack $`h-Gx`$. Thus the added directions may make the lifted feasible or optimal set unbounded without implying objective unboundedness.

Because each feasible point in either problem has a value-preserving partner, the infima agree, including $`+\infty`$ for infeasibility and $`-\infty`$ for unboundedness. A point is optimal in one formulation exactly when its image, or any of its lifts, is optimal in the other. Attainment is therefore equivalent as well.

## Validation

Both feasible-set maps, all nonunique lifts, objective constants, infeasible cases, and unbounded cases are covered explicitly.
