# Exercise 2.9: Voronoi polyhedra and a non-Voronoi partition

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, [Convex Optimization, exercise 2.9, p. 60](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=74).

Original solution.

## (a) Halfspace representation

Squaring each distance comparison gives $`Ax\le b`$, with row and right-hand-side entries
```math
A_{i,:}=2(x_i-x_0)^T,\qquad b_i=\|x_i\|_2^2-\|x_0\|_2^2,
\quad i=1,\ldots,K.
```

## (b) Constructing sites from a full-dimensional polyhedron

Write $`P=\{x:a_i^Tx\le b_i,\ i=1,\ldots,K\}`$ after removing zero and duplicate constraints, and choose $`x_0\in\mathrm{int}\,P`$. Every retained constraint has $`a_i\ne0`$ and $`s_i=b_i-a_i^Tx_0>0`$. Reflect $`x_0`$ through each bounding hyperplane:
```math
x_i=x_0+\frac{2s_i}{\|a_i\|_2^2}a_i.
```
The perpendicular bisector of $`x_0,x_i`$ is exactly $`a_i^Tx=b_i`$, and the side nearer $`x_0`$ is $`a_i^Tx\le b_i`$. Intersecting the corresponding Voronoi inequalities gives $`P`$. Coincident reflected sites can be removed without changing the intersection. If $`P=\mathbb R^n`$, no competitor sites are needed.

## (c) Decompositions need not be Voronoi decompositions

Each actual Voronoi cell is a polyhedron by part (a). A ball of radius smaller than half the minimum distance from its site to the other sites lies in its interior. Every point has at least one nearest site, so the cells cover space. Two different cells can intersect only on their sites' perpendicular bisector, which has empty interior, proving disjointness of their interiors.

The converse fails already in one dimension. Consider the five intervals
```math
(-\infty,0],\quad[0,1],\quad[1,4],\quad[4,5],\quad[5,\infty).
```
If they were Voronoi cells, their sites would be ordered $`z_0<z_1<z_2<z_3<z_4`$, with adjacent midpoint boundaries $`0,1,4,5`$. Each site lies strictly inside its own cell, so $`0<z_1<1`$ and $`4<z_3<5`$. But the midpoint equations give $`z_2=2-z_1`$ and $`z_3=8-z_2=6+z_1>6`$, a contradiction. Hence a general polyhedral partition is not generated by unweighted Euclidean Voronoi sites.
