# Exercise 5.25: Convex-concave minimax through conjugacy

Source: [Boyd and Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, exercise 5.25](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=295). The source exercise and all of its subparts were checked directly.

Let $`h_w`$ and $`g_z`$ be the closed convex extensions specified in the exercise. They are proper because the two sets are nonempty and $`f`$ is finite on their product.

## (a) Convexity of the perturbation function

Define $`F(w,u)=\sup_{z\in Z}(f(w,z)+u^Tz)`$ for $`w\in W`$, and $`+\infty`$ otherwise. Each function $`g_z(w)+u^Tz`$ is jointly convex in $`(w,u)`$, so their supremum is jointly convex. Its partial infimum $`p(u)=\inf_w F(w,u)`$ is convex. Explicitly, take approximately minimizing $`w_1,w_2`$, apply joint convexity at their convex combination, and let approximation errors vanish. This proof does not assume that the infimum is attained or that its epigraph projection is closed.

## (b) Conjugate

For fixed $`w`$, $`F(w,u)=h_w^*(u)`$. Reversing the infimum by negation and commuting two suprema gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
p^*(v)
&=\sup_u\sup_{w\in W}\{u^Tv-h_w^*(u)\}\\
&=\sup_{w\in W}h_w^{**}(v)
=\sup_{w\in W}h_w(v).
\end{aligned}
```

The last equality is biconjugacy for a proper closed convex function. Therefore $`p^*(v)=-\inf_{w\in W}f(w,v)`$ if $`v\in Z`$, and $`+\infty`$ otherwise.

## (c) Biconjugate and max-min value

Applying the conjugate definition again yields

```math
p^{**}(u)=\sup_{z\in Z}\left[u^Tz+\inf_{w\in W}f(w,z)\right]
=\sup_{z\in Z}\inf_{w\in W}(f(w,z)+u^Tz).
```

At zero, $`p(0)`$ is the min-max side and $`p^{**}(0)`$ the max-min side. Equality is exactly closure of this convex value function at zero under the cited regularity conditions.

## (d) Bounded sets

Choose $`w_0\in W,z_0\in Z`$. A proper closed convex function with bounded domain has a finite attained minimum: a nonempty sublevel is closed and bounded, hence compact, and lower semicontinuity gives a finite minimum there. Apply this to $`g_{z_0}`$ and $`h_{w_0}`$. It follows that

```math
-\infty<\inf_{w\in W} f(w,z_0)
\le p(0)\le\sup_{z\in Z}f(w_0,z)<+\infty.
```

If $`\|z\|\le M`$ on $`Z`$, then for every $`w`$, adding $`u^Tz`$ changes its supremum by at most $`M\|u\|`$. Taking infima gives $`|p(u)-p(0)|\le M\|u\|`$. Thus $`p`$ is finite everywhere and in particular $`0\in\mathrm{int}\,\mathrm{dom}p`$. The stated biconjugacy result yields the minimax equality. Boundedness of the domains did not have to imply they themselves are closed; closedness of the extended functions supplied compact sublevels.

## (e) Bounded sublevels

In fact it is enough that one fixed $`g_{z_0}`$ has bounded sublevel sets. To prove lower semicontinuity, take $`u_j\to u`$ with finite bounded-above $`p(u_j)`$ along a subsequence realizing its lower limit, and choose $`w_j`$ with $`F(w_j,u_j)\le p(u_j)+1/j`$. Then

```math
g_{z_0}(w_j)\le F(w_j,u_j)-u_j^Tz_0
```

is bounded above. Its closed bounded sublevel is compact, so a subsequence has $`w_j\to w\in W`$. For every fixed $`z\in Z`$, closedness of $`g_z`$ and convergence of $`u_j`$ yield

```math
f(w,z)+u^Tz\le\liminf_j F(w_j,u_j)=\liminf_jp(u_j).
```

Taking the supremum in $`z`$ and then using $`p(u)\le F(w,u)`$ proves $`p(u)\le\liminf_jp(u_j)`$. A lower limit of $`-\infty`$ is impossible here because $`g_{z_0}`$ has a finite global lower bound, so $`p(u_j)\ge\inf_wg_{z_0}(w)+u_j^Tz_0`$. If the lower limit is $`+\infty`$, the lower-semicontinuity inequality is automatic. Thus $`p`$ is closed. When $`0\in\mathrm{dom}p`$, it is proper and biconjugacy gives the desired equality.

## Validation

The conjugate order exchange is between suprema only; the minimax equality is obtained afterward from convex closure, not assumed in its derivation. Open bounded domains and unattained intermediate infima are explicitly allowed.
