# Exercise 8.5: Depth and signed distance of a convex set

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 8.5, printed page 447. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=461).

Distances are measured in the chosen norm, with dual norm $`\|\cdot\|_*`$.

## (a) Concavity of depth
Let $`x,y\in C`$, and choose radii $`r<\mathrm{depth}(x,C)`$, $`s<\mathrm{depth}(y,C)`$, both nonnegative when available. Their open norm balls are contained in $`C`$. Convexity implies that the open ball centered at $`\theta x+(1-\theta)y`$ with radius $`\theta r+(1-\theta)s`$ is contained in $`C`$: express any displacement as the same unit-ball direction times the two radii and take the convex combination. Let the radii increase to their depths. Zero-depth cases follow by taking the corresponding displacement zero. Thus
```math
\mathrm{depth}(\theta x+(1-\theta)y,C)
\ge\theta\,\mathrm{depth}(x,C)+(1-\theta)\,\mathrm{depth}(y,C).
```

## (b) Convexity of signed distance
First suppose $`C`$ has nonempty interior and is a proper subset of space. Replacing it by its closure changes neither outside distance nor inside depth, and boundary points have both values zero. Let $`h_C(a)=\sup_{z\in C}a^Tz`$. We claim
```math
s(x)=\sup_{\substack{\|a\|_*=1\\h_C(a)\lt \infty}}
\{a^Tx-h_C(a)\}.\tag{1}
```
For an interior $`x`$, a norm ball of radius $`r`$ about $`x`$ lies in the closed convex set exactly when $`a^Tx+r\|a\|_*\le h_C(a)`$ for every supporting functional. This follows from the halfspace representation of a closed convex set and the definition of the dual norm. Taking the largest $`r`$ proves (1) inside, including the boundary limit.

For an exterior $`x`$, every unit-dual functional gives $`a^Tx-h_C(a)\le\inf_{z\in C}\|x-z\|`$. For the opposite inequality separate the open ball of radius $`d=\mathrm{dist}(x,C)>0`$ centered at $`x`$ from the closed convex set. A separating nonzero functional satisfies $`h_C(a)\le a^Tx-d\|a\|_*`$; after normalization it attains the reverse bound. Thus (1) holds outside as well. It is a supremum of affine functions and hence convex.

If $`C`$ has empty interior, its complement approaches every point of $`C`$, so depth is zero everywhere on $`C`$ and $`s(x)=\mathrm{dist}(x,C)`$ everywhere. Distance to a convex set is convex, as follows by combining approximate nearest points and applying the triangle inequality. If $`C=\mathbb R^n`$, depth is $`+\infty`$ and the signed distance is identically $`-\infty`$ under the extended convention; it is not a finite-valued distance-to-boundary function. The usual finite formulation implicitly excludes this case.
