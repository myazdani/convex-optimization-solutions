# Exercise 11.19: A lifted linear system for barrier Newton steps

Source: Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, exercise 11.19, printed page 628. [Official book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf#page=642).

Let $`F_t=tf_0+\phi`$, $`g=\nabla F_t(x)`$, $`J=Df(x)`$, and $`D=\mathrm{diag}(f_i(x))`$, which is invertible at a strictly feasible point. Define
```math
B=t\nabla^2f_0(x)+\sum_i\frac1{-f_i(x)}\nabla^2f_i(x).
```
The centering Hessian is $`H=B+J^TD^{-2}J`$. At an equality-feasible point, solve the larger system
```math
\begin{bmatrix}
B&J^T&A^T\\
J&-D^2&0\\
A&0&0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}v\\w\\\nu\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}-g\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}.
```
Its middle row gives $`w=D^{-2}Jv`$. Substitution in the first row gives $`Hv+A^T\nu=-g`$, and the last row gives $`Av=0`$. These are exactly the standard barrier Newton KKT equations, so its first block is the desired Newton direction. Conversely every solution of those equations extends uniquely by this $`w`$, proving equivalence.

The lift is useful when $`B`$, $`J`$, and $`A`$ are sparse or block structured but $`J^TD^{-2}J`$ creates a dense matrix: for example constraints involving small variable subsets, decomposed scenario models, and sparse quadratic constraints with a low-rank dense Hessian update. The extra middle block is diagonal and can be handled cheaply in suitable elimination orders. One should compare fill-in of the lifted sparse symmetric-indefinite factorization with that of the reduced KKT system; a larger system is not automatically faster. At an infeasible equality iterate replace the final right-hand side by $`b-Ax`$, with the corresponding infeasible-start residual in the top row.
